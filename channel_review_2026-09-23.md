None of the three is an outlier on its own channel. Applying the two-channel test to each topic:

### 1a. Kmart / "Is Changing Fast — Customers Are Starting to Notice" — DO NOT MAKE
- The construction has one real outlier: the same channel's McDonald's Australia version, 68,903 in 4 days (2.1×). Broken Canada's "Princess Auto Is Changing Fast — And Canadians Are Starting to Notice" did 50,823 in 8 days, which is 1.1× Broken Canada's average, so not an outlier. Oz Grocery Alert's Supercheap Auto version did 25,699.
- Canadian Counter already tried this construction: "Something Is Seriously Wrong at McDonald's... Canada Is Starting to Notice" (13 Sept) did 5,188, half the channel's 90-day median.
- Kmart is a non-food store. Canadian Counter's non-food store videos all flopped: Winners 4,883, Canadian Tire 5,238, Shoppers Drug Mart 3,812, Amazon Prime 3,505. The store construction only works on the channel for weekly-grocery stores (Costco 235,628; No Frills 106,098).

### 1b. Wine brands — DO NOT MAKE
- The topic is multi-channel proven in the US and Australia, but seven to eight months ago: The Hidden Menu 658,743; Dishclosure 201,868; All Foods Uncovered 195,307; Flashback USA 128,754; The Hidden Pantry 75,785; Australia Food Exposed's earlier version 65,996. No Canadian channel has a hit on it (the only Canadian result is a 14K red-wine ranking).
- Canadian Counter's own alcohol record is the weakest category on the channel: wine 8,367 (Feb 2026), ice wine 194, "14 WORST Canadian Alcohol Brands" 643, lagers 4,265, whisky 12,708, beer 18,318 and 17,133. Seven attempts, none above 19K.
- The format's hook is additives and "safe to drink", which is health framing the house rules exclude. The non-health angle (phantom brands, "International Canadian Blend" wine that is mostly imported juice) is real but has never been proven by any channel.

### 1c. Restaurant chains customers are abandoning — YES, BUT IN CANADIAN COUNTER'S OWN PROVEN FORM
- In Plain Bite's video is not an outlier, but the Canadian version of the topic is two-channel proven, and one of the two channels is Canadian Counter:
  - Canadian Counter, "Millions of Canadians Are Boycotting These 8 Restaurants (And They're Fighting Back)", 27 Apr 2026: 103,726.
  - North America Crisis Radar, "7 Canada Fast Food Chains That Are Struggling To Stay In Business", May 2026: 81,000 (8.3× that channel's average).
  - Supporting: Canadian Counter "7 Canadian Restaurants That Got Too Greedy and Paid the Price" 52,565; North America Crisis Radar's version 39,000 (4.0×).
- The April video is now five months old, which is the window where a same-construction redo has worked before (yogurt 127,837 in Dec → 285,047 in June).
- Warning: the two September chain videos flopped (ownership 6,636; McDonald's 5,188), but both used different constructions. Keep the April construction: "Millions of Canadians Are [Boycotting/Abandoning] These N Restaurants" with 2026 evidence (closures, Restaurants Canada numbers, earnings calls, price increases), not In Plain Bite's "The Numbers Just Got Worse" phrasing.

## 4. RECOMMENDATION

1. Make now: the restaurant-abandonment video in the channel's own April construction. Two Canadian channels proven, one of them this channel, and the five-month redo window is open.
2. Make next: Giant Tiger, "Watch This Before You Shop at Giant Tiger Again" or "Don't Shop at Giant Tiger Until You Watch This". Two channels at 331K and 185K, and this channel has never touched it.
3. Hold for a documented event: a "[Banner] Just Did What No Canadian Grocery Store Would" video. The construction is proven on six videos across three channels, but it needs a real, dated retailer action to be honest.
4. Bank by date: Costco membership redo (mid-October), 30 Fake Foods (late October), coffee brands (November).
5. Stop making: marginal staple categories (salt, water, juice, soya, cream, cookies, cream cheese), non-food store videos, alcohol, and different-construction redos of past hits. Those account for all thirteen flops.
6. Cadence: 31 uploads in 30 days is burying the hits. The channel's outliers came at the same rate whether or not the flops were published.
