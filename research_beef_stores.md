## 2. 🔥 THE SPINE OF THE VIDEO — CBC CAUGHT THEM TWICE

This is store-specific, weight-specific and repeat-offence. It is the single best fit for a store-test format found anywhere in this session's research.

### Wave 1 — CBC, 9 January 2025
**Methodology:** a whistleblower complaint + CFIA records + CBC's own testing. CBC **weighed meat packages bought from seven stores across three provinces in late 2024**, comparing stated net weight to actual meat weight with packaging excluded. **Underweight packages found in four of the seven stores.**

| Store | Products underweight | Basket | Overcharge | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Loblaws** (Toronto) + **No Frills** (Calgary) | chicken; chicken, pork, ground beef | 6 items, $107.43 | **$5.14** | **5%** |
| **FreshCo** (Sobeys, Toronto) | pork, chicken, beef | 6 items, $38.08 | **$2.62** | **7%** |
| **Walmart** (Richmond BC) | turkey drumsticks + others | 6 items, $47.42 | **$3.07** | **6.9%** |

Per-item overcharges **4% to 11%**.

🔥 **THE MECHANISM WAS THE TRAY.** A **48 g** tray sold as chicken at Loblaw. A **50 g** tray sold as ground beef at FreshCo. A **35 g** tray sold as turkey at Walmart. **You were paying beef prices for polystyrene.**

**The law:** posted net weights for packaged food — and prices based on them — **cannot include the packaging.**

**Enforcement context:** in fiscal 2023-24 the CFIA conducted **125 planned inspections for food weight accuracy across Canada's 8,000+ grocery stores.**

**Company responses:**
- **Loblaw** (Catherine Thomas): apologized, blamed an *"operational error"* from a packaging change. *"Any pricing issue resulting in overcharge is one too many."* Offered discounts and "refreshed training," **would not name affected stores.**
- **Sobeys:** stores *"adhere to federal weight regulations"*; expects compliance from **third-party weighing agents**.
- **Walmart** (Felicia Fefer): *"We take concerns of this nature very seriously"* — addressing it with **third-party weighing partners**.

**Quotes worth using verbatim:**
- **Mark Olivier**, 20+ year former grocery manager: *"There's no bite to the enforcement… What's the motivation for the store to do things properly if they're more profitable when they err to the detriment of the consumer?"*
- **Terri Lee**, retired CFIA inspector (24 years): *"There was a whole myriad of excuses… The store would blame head office and head office might blame the store."*
- **Daniel Tsai**, consumer-protection lawyer: *"That's going to add up into a very large number, potentially into millions and millions of dollars."*
- **CFIA's Patrick Girard:** *"Industry does face repercussions, and they are proportionate to the food safety risk and the seriousness of the non-compliance."*

### 🆕 THE REGULATORY SEAM — why nobody catches this
**Three regulators split the job and the gap is in the middle:**
- **Measurement Canada** — *Weights and Measures Act*; oversees **scale devices** and short-measure complaints
- **CFIA** — Safe Food for Canadians Regulations; enforces **accurate net quantity declarations on labels**
- **Competition Bureau** — *Competition Act*; misleading representations

The structural gap: the **five-year cycle** device examination catches **device accuracy** but not **"product-specific tare entries"** at meat counters. **The scale is certified accurate. The number typed into it for the weight of the tray is not checked by anyone.** That is precisely where systematic errors slip through.
⚠️ **Do not attribute the 236 inspections or the 7 warnings to Measurement Canada — that enforcement was CFIA's.**

## 3. WHERE THE BEEF ACTUALLY COMES FROM

### 🔥 The structural fact that governs everything
Canada's beef kill is a **duopoly**:
- **Cargill High River, Alberta** — ~**4,500 head/day**, about **36% of Canada's entire beef processing capacity**
- **JBS Canada, Brooks, Alberta** — ~**4,200 head/day**
- Together **~77% of Canada's ~3 million federally inspected slaughter**; ~70% of all Canadian beef is killed in those two Alberta plants
- **Both foreign-owned** — Cargill is American, JBS is Brazilian
- Cargill also runs a beef facility in **Guelph, Ontario**

> **The banner on the package is branding. For most Canadian beef, the animal went through one of two buildings regardless of which store you shop at.**

### Case-ready — the mechanism behind the scandal
Case-ready = beef cut, weighed, wrapped and **priced at a central plant or third-party packager**, arriving ready to place in the case. The store never touches it. **That's why the labels were wrong: they were printed elsewhere, and the tray was counted as meat.** Every chain's response pointed at third parties — Empire at Farm Boy's third-party CFIA-certified facilities, Sobeys at "third-party weighing agents," Walmart at "third-party weighing partners," Loblaw at a packaging-change "operational error."

### Chain by chain
**Loblaw** — does **not** publish an owned-slaughter footprint; buys boxed beef from packers. First Canadian retailer to buy CRSB Certified beef (1M lbs 2020, +3M lbs by 2023), ~500 Ontario beef farmers.
⚠️ **Correct a common misreading:** only ~**30% of the beef Loblaw sources for its Ontario Corn Fed Beef brand** is targeted as CRSB Certified — **not 30% of all its beef.**

**Empire/Sobeys** — sources beef *"through processors and distributors who are supplied by a range of primarily Canadian farms."* 🔥 **Sobeys has no formal animal welfare policy at all**, per World Animal Protection — while overseeing welfare for Farm Boy, FreshCo, Foodland, IGA, Longo's, Safeway and Thrifty Foods.

**Walmart Canada** — **strongest published claim of any chain:** *"Walmart Canada sources 100% of its beef in Canada and is a proud supporter of the Canadian beef industry."* (press release, 10 May 2021). First Canadian grocery retailer with an **on-pack CRSB certified sustainable claim**. CRSB commitment **2.5 million pounds** cumulative (1M Oct 2021 + 1M Aug 2022). Animal welfare: adheres to the **Five Freedoms**.
⚠️ 2.5M lbs is cumulative, not annual, and small relative to total volume — but Walmart Canada's total beef volume isn't published, so **do not state a percentage on camera.**

**Costco Canada** — sells under an explicit **"Canada AAA Beef"** program plus Kirkland Signature; Canada AAA is verifiable on business-centre listings. **The clearest consistent grade-labeller of any banner.**
⚠️ costco.ca/canada-beef.html and canada-aaa-beef.html returned **empty/expired content on 18 Aug 2026** — re-check before quoting. Global FY2021 disclosure: *over 88% of the beef in Kirkland Signature items came from the US, Canada and New Zealand* — a **combined** figure, **NOT a Canadian-origin claim.**

### Grading — and why "no grade" is itself the tell
- Done by the **Canadian Beef Grading Agency**, a private non-profit accredited by CFIA. **Grading is voluntary**, but *"virtually all fed beef carcasses processed commercially in Canada are graded."*
- Marbling: **Canada Prime** = slightly abundant · **AAA** = small · **AA** = slight · **A** = trace
- 2023: Prime + AAA + AA + A = **95.4%** of graded youthful slaughter cattle
- **Costco brands the grade on the case. Discount banners frequently sell beef with no grade stated.** Good on-camera test: photograph every label and count how many name a grade.

## 6. THE WINNERS SIDE

### 🚨 THE "BUY A QUARTER COW AND SAVE 50%" CLAIM FAILS AT CURRENT CANADIAN PRICES — DO NOT REPEAT IT
**Chatsworth Farm (Ontario), published pricing:**

| Order | Price (hanging weight, cut & wrapped incl.) | Take-home yield | Est. total | **Effective $/lb take-home** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole | $9.25/lb | 450–510 lb | ~$7,175 | **$14.06–$15.94** |
| Half | $10.50/lb | 240–310 lb | ~$3,400 | **$10.97–$14.17** |
| Front quarter | $11.00/lb | 90–115 lb | ~$1,568 | **$13.63–$17.42** |
| Hind quarter | $12.00/lb | 90–115 lb | ~$1,735 | **$15.09–$19.28** |

**Yield is 60–65% of hanging weight as packaged meat** — that's the number that kills the savings claim, because farms quote **hanging** weight and shoppers think in **take-home** weight.

Compare to StatCan retail per lb (June 2026): ground **$7.53** · stewing **$11.48** · top sirloin **$12.67** · rib **$15.67** · striploin **$17.26**. **A whole carcass is mostly ground and stewing-grade meat, not striploin.**

**Honest framing — and a genuinely strong contrarian segment:**
> *"You're buying better beef and more of it, not cheaper beef — and the savings claim you see online is usually built on hanging weight, not on what actually goes in your freezer."*

### The independent butcher is a vanishing option
- Ontario provincial abattoirs **down 40% since 1991**
- Cattle slaughtered at Ontario provincial plants fell from **189,806 (2004) to 95,346 (2011)**
- Ontario has 6 federally inspected and ~98 provincially inspected abattoirs — but **3 federal plants processed 89% of the 687,752 cattle slaughtered in Ontario in 2018**
- **85% of Canadian meat is slaughtered/processed in federally inspected plants**
- Canadian Federation of Independent Grocers represents **~6,900 independent retailers**

### Certification — what VBP+ / CRSB actually require
- **VBP+ (Verified Beef Production Plus)** certifies producers against the **CRSB Certified Sustainable Beef Framework** via **third-party on-farm audit**
- To earn credits, cattle must be **raised on certified sustainable operations their whole life, be age-verified, and be tracked through TrustBIX** before shipping to a **Cargill plant in Guelph ON or High River AB**
- Five audited principles: Natural Resources; People and Community; Animal Health and Welfare; Food; Efficiency and Innovation
- Users: Loblaw (first Canadian retailer, 2020), Walmart Canada (first with on-pack claim), McDonald's Canada, Swiss Chalet, Original Joe's, Cactus Club
- ⚠️ **Honest caveat:** CRSB is a **sustainability and welfare audit**. It is **not** a grade, **not** a tenderness guarantee, **not** an organic claim.

### Grinding at home — the real food-safety argument
On a whole steak or roast, bacteria sit **on the surface**, where direct heat destroys them. **Grinding takes surface contamination and distributes it throughout the meat** — which is why ground beef must be cooked to a higher internal temperature and why a single contaminated carcass can contaminate an entire batch. Buy a whole cut, grind it at home, and only one animal's surface is involved — and you control it.
Source: **USDA FSIS, *Ground Beef and Food Safety***.
⚠️ US regulator. No equivalent CFIA/Health Canada page states the mechanism as plainly. **Cite FSIS for the mechanism and keep the framing scientific, not jurisdictional.**
