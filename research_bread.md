## THE SPINE — the 95% rule

Bread and flour standards left the *Food and Drug Regulations* under **SOR/2024-244** (registered Nov 29 2024, Canada Gazette Part II Dec 18 2024) and now live in the **Canadian Food Compositional Standards (CFCS), Volume 12 — Grain and Bakery Products**, incorporated by reference into the FDR.

| Old FDR | New CFCS | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| B.13.001 | 12.1.1 | Flour / white flour / enriched flour |
| B.13.005 | **12.1.3** | Whole wheat flour |
| B.13.021 | 12.2.1 | Bread / white bread |
| B.13.022 | 12.2.2 | Enriched bread |

**CFCS 12.1.3, verbatim:** *"Whole Wheat Flour or Entire Wheat Flour is the food prepared by the grinding and bolting of cleaned, milling grades of wheat from which a part of the outer bran or epidermis layer may have been separated; (a) contains the natural constituents of the wheat berry to the extent of **not less than 95%** of the total weight of the wheat from which it is milled…"*

**Health Canada, plain language:** *"Up to 5% of the kernel can be removed to help reduce rancidity and prolong the shelf life of whole wheat flour."* … *"**100% whole wheat bread may not be whole grain** — however, it remains a nutritious choice that provides dietary fibre not found in white bread."*

The removed part is mostly **the germ** (germ oil goes rancid).

⚠️ Say "up to 5% **may** be removed." Not "5% is always removed."

**CFCS 12.2.4:** *"(Naming the percentage) Whole Wheat Bread… contains **not less than 60%** whole wheat flour in relation to the total flour used."* — a loaf legally called "whole wheat bread" needs only 60% whole wheat flour.

**CFCS 12.2.5:** *"Brown Bread is bread coloured by the use of whole wheat flour, graham flour, bran or molasses."* — caramel colour is NOT in the standard.

**CFCS 12.2.1:** bread = yeast-leavened dough of flour and water; may contain up to **5% of flour used** in whole wheat/graham/gluten/non-wheat flours. Yeast is mandatory — a no-yeast product must be called "loaf" or say "made without yeast."

**Enrichment is compulsory:** CFIA — *"The sale of unenriched white flour or foods containing unenriched white flour is not permitted in Canada."* Per 100 g white flour: thiamine 0.64 mg, riboflavin 0.40 mg, niacin 5.30 mg, folic acid 0.15 mg, iron 4.4 mg. **Whole wheat flour is NOT required to be enriched** — so the white loaf carries added folic acid and iron the whole wheat loaf doesn't.

## CLAIMS: REGULATED vs MARKETING

| Term | Status |
|---|---|
| **"Multi-grain"** | Weak. *"2 or more grains… in an amount of 2% each or more."* CFIA's grain list includes soybean, peas, beans, lentils, flaxseed, mustard seed, sunflower seeds. |
| **"12 grain" / "14 grain"** | **No standard.** Only the multigrain guidance — 2% each of 12 listed items. |
| **"Stone ground"** | Literal. *"acceptable for products that have been ground between 2 stones… would not apply to products ground between metal and stone or metal and metal."* |
| **"Brown bread"** | Standardized (12.2.5) |
| **"Made with whole grains"** | **No threshold found — effectively unregulated.** |

### 🔥 The single best line in the CFIA guidance
> *"The statement **'made with unbleached flour'** on products containing **azodicarbonamide**, when added at permitted levels, is acceptable as it does not bleach the flour."*

### Additives — Health Canada permitted list
| Additive | Status | Max |
|---|---|---|
| **Azodicarbonamide (ADA)** | ✅ permitted (bread, white flour, whole wheat flour) | **45 ppm** |
| **Potassium bromate** | ❌ **not on the permitted list** | — |
| Potassium iodate / calcium iodate | ✅ | 45 ppm |
| Benzoyl peroxide | ✅ | 150 ppm |
| SSL | ✅ | 3,000–5,000 ppm |
| L-cysteine hydrochloride | ✅ | 90 ppm |

⚠️ ADA is **permitted and invisible** — a flour treatment that may not appear in the bread's ingredient list. It was NOT found declared on any Canadian retail bread label pulled. Say "permitted, and invisible," never "it's in your bread."
⚠️ Potassium bromate: only the *current absence* from the permitted list is confirmed. The widely-repeated "banned 1994" date is NOT primary-sourced.

**Caramel colour** must now be declared by specific name (SOR/2016-305; enforcement since 2023-12-15).

⚠️ **Two framings exist — do not conflate.** Loblaw/GWL describe *"a period extending from late 2001 to March 2015."* Canada Bread's guilty plea covers **four counts producing two increases — October 2007 and March 2011.** The "14 years" shorthand comes from *allegations*, not from anything proven.

### Canada Bread — what is PROVEN
- **$50 million, June 21 2023, Ontario Superior Court**, four counts under **s. 45 Competition Act**.
- Bureau's exact wording: *"The fine is the highest **price-fixing** fine imposed by a Canadian court to date."* — not "largest corporate fine in Canadian history." Do not overstate.
- Bureau: *"Canada Bread admitted that it arranged with its competitor, Weston Foods (Canada) Inc., to increase prices for various bagged and sliced bread products, such as sandwich bread, hot dog buns and rolls."*
- **PPSC fine arithmetic, verbatim:** *"$10 million each for counts 1 and 2 (before March 2010) and $25 million each for counts 3 and 4 (after March 2010), for a total of $70 million, less a leniency rebate of approximately 30%…"* Payable within 30 days.

**Commissioner Matthew Boswell — the money quote:**
> *"Fixing the price of bread — a food staple of Canadian households — was a serious criminal offence. Our continuing investigation remains a top priority. We are doing everything in our power to pursue those who engage in price-fixing."*

Boswell to CBC: *"We seized more electronic records and electronic information than on any other case in the Bureau's history."*

**Mechanics of the two increases** (agreed statement of facts, via CBC): executives discussed raising prices June 2007, agreed ~6–7¢/loaf for October, then after September discussions **doubled it to 12–14¢**. Repeated: discussions Nov 2010 → 7¢ agreed for early 2011 → a January 2011 meeting **raised it to 14¢** starting February.

**Ownership nuance — verified.** Bureau: *"At the time of the price-fixing, Canada Bread was under the ownership of Maple Leaf Foods. The senior leadership of Canada Bread responsible for the price-fixing is no longer with the company."* PPSC: CBCL *"has been under new ownership since May 2014"* and *"cooperated with the investigation upon learning of the allegations in 2017."* Per the agreed statement of facts, Grupo Bimbo *"only learned about the price-fixing arrangements after the Competition Bureau executed a search warrant… on Oct. 31, 2017"* and *"the due diligence otherwise completed by Grupo Bimbo did not reveal"* it.

### Loblaw / George Weston — they ADMITTED, they didn't merely "cooperate"
From their own Dec 19 2017 release, verbatim:
> *"George Weston and Loblaw announce actions taken to address **their role** in an industry-wide price-fixing arrangement…"*
> *"The arrangement involved the coordination of retail and wholesale prices… Under the arrangement, the participants regularly increased prices on a coordinated basis."*
> *"As a result of **their admission that they participated** in the arrangement and their cooperation… the Companies will not face criminal charges or penalties."*

**Galen G. Weston:** *"This sort of behaviour is wrong and has no place in our business or Canada's grocery industry. This should never have happened."*
**Spokesperson Kevin Groh (Jan 2018):** *"We have admitted our role, and **you cannot price fix alone**."*

Immunity is **criminal only** — it did not shield them from civil class actions, hence the $500M.

**$25 Loblaw Card:** registration Jan 8 – May 8 2018. Forecast *"between $75 and $150 million depending on customer take-up and to be an offset against civil liability."* **Actual: $96 million.** Loblaw warned: *"The card program cost should not be viewed as an estimate of damages."*

### The "$1.50 a loaf" and the "7/10 convention"
Both are **Competition Bureau allegations from the sealed ITO released January 31 2018** — sworn search-warrant material, **not proven damages findings**. Always attribute.

ITO, verbatim: *"the retailers demanded that the suppliers actively manage retail competition by co-ordinating retail prices for their respective fresh commercial bread products and ensuring pricing alignment amongst the retailers."* The nickname: **"the 7/10 convention"** — ~7¢ wholesale increase, 10¢ at the till. Alleged to have run *"typically by seven cents"* for more than a decade from 2001.

**Person X colour [ALLEGED]:** at an industry convention, allegedly said *"you know, this industry is crazy,"* noted other industries raise prices annually, and remarked *"There's no reason the bakery business shouldn't do the same"* — then presented a plan *"whereby Person X was going to the retailers to get their buy-in for a price increase with the goal of orchestrating alignment through the retail community."*

### The settlement and the payout
**$500M** = George Weston **$247.5M cash** + Loblaw **$252.5M** ($156.5M cash + $96M Loblaw Card credit), negotiated in a mediation presided over by the Chief Justice of the Ontario Superior Court.

**Galen G. Weston:** *"On behalf of the Weston group of companies, we are sorry for the price-fixing behaviour we discovered and self-reported in 2015. This behaviour should never have happened… Reaching a settlement on this matter was the right thing to do in response to previous behaviour that did not meet our values and ethical standards."*

**Justice Ed Morgan, May 7 2025:** *"an excellent and fair result for all concerned."* · *"It is in the best interests of the class as a whole… it falls squarely within the zone of reasonableness."* · *"The parties do not have to do much to convince me that the litigation has been hard fought on all sides."* · *"None of the objectors chose to speak… a review of their written objections indicates that they have no principled grounds for objecting to the settlement other than that they would like more money."*

Four objections, **475 opt-outs**, against *"an estimated 20 million-plus class members."* 78% to the Ontario class. Settlement *"includes assurances that they will co-operate with claims being pursued against the remaining grocers."*

## 🚨 ADA vs POTASSIUM BROMATE — THE SENTENCE MOST VIDEOS GET WRONG

**POTASSIUM BROMATE — not permitted in Canada. Still legal in the US.**
Government primary source, Environment Canada / Health Canada *Screening Assessment for the Challenge — Bromic acid, potassium salt*, verbatim:
> *"Potassium bromate used to be a permitted food additive in Canada, but it was **delisted in 1994** and therefore is no longer permitted to be used as a food additive in foods offered for sale in Canada."*

**This resolves the earlier flag — the 1994 date IS primary-sourced.** (PDF: canada.ca/content/dam/eccc/migration/ese-ees/…/batch9_7758-01-2_en.pdf)

**United States: 21 CFR 136.110(c)(14)** permits potassium bromate *"not more than 0.0075 part for each 100 parts by weight of flour"* — **75 ppm**.
IARC: **Group 2B, "possibly carcinogenic to humans."** ⚠️ the 1999 year is secondary-sourced.

**AZODICARBONAMIDE — permitted in Canada at the SAME 45 ppm as the US.**
- Canada: Health Canada's permitted list — bread, white flour, whole wheat flour, **max 45 ppm**.
- US: **21 CFR 172.806** — *"not to exceed 2.05 grams per 100 pounds of flour (0.0045 percent; 45 parts per million)."*
- EU: not on the authorised list under Reg. (EC) 1333/2008 — only ascorbic acid (E300) and L-cysteine (E920) are authorised flour treatment agents. ⚠️ secondary-verified; cite the EU Food Additives Database if stating it.

**The precise, defensible on-screen line:**
> *"Potassium bromate — banned in Canada since 1994, still legal in American bread at up to 75 parts per million. Azodicarbonamide, the so-called 'yoga mat chemical' — Canada allows it, at exactly the same 45 parts per million the United States allows. Europe allows neither."*

⛔ **DO NOT say ADA is banned in Canada.** Canada is *not* stricter than the US on ADA — the two are identical.

## SODIUM — bread really is #1, and it's primary-sourced
Health Canada, *Sodium Intake of Canadians in 2017*, verbatim:
> *"**Bakery products**, which include: breads, muffins, cookies, desserts, crackers, and granola bars are the **top food sources (20%)** of sodium."*
Mixed dishes 19%, processed meats 11% — *"these 3 broad categories account for half of all the sodium Canadians consume."*

⚠️ It is the **bakery products category** that is #1, not bread alone. Say "bakery products — bread chief among them."

**Voluntary 2020–2025 targets (mg/100 g), sales-weighted average / max:** white bread **360 / 520** · whole wheat & mixed grain **330 / 400** · hearth bread **430 / 560**.

**🔥 Progress — Health Canada interim assessment, published 2025-06-27:**
> **"Only modest progress has been made toward the 2025 SWA sodium targets in the categories surveyed."**
- White bread/rolls/bagels: **445 → 429 mg/100 g** (2017 → 2023-24) — **18.5%** of the way to target.
- **Whole wheat and mixed grain bread went the WRONG WAY: 387 → 392 mg/100 g (−7.6% progress).** One of only four categories that rose. **The "healthy" bread got saltier.** Strongest counterintuitive beat available.
- Hearth bread best: 504 → 474 mg/100 g, 40.3% of target.

**Quebec bread study** — Le Bouthillier et al., *Nutrients* 2021;13(12):4196 (PMID 34959748), abstract verbatim:
> *"294 sliced breads available in Quebec… **64% of the breads purchased were 'low' in fibre** (below 2 g per two slices), while **47% were 'high' in sodium** (above 15% DV per two slices). Attention should be paid to 100% refined grain bread and to bread with refined flour as the main flour, since they are great sellers, while showing a less favourable nutrient content."*

**No Health Canada sugar-reduction target exists for bread** (unlike sodium) — worth saying explicitly.

## FOLIC ACID — the honest counterweight
- **Mandatory since November 1998**: folic acid fortification of white flour, enriched pasta and cornmeal.
- Best citation: **De Wals P, et al., *NEJM* 2007;357:135-142** — a **~46% reduction** in neural-tube defects nationally, largest declines where baseline rates were highest.
- ⚠️ Other figures circulate (1.13→0.58 per 1,000 in Ontario; a "78% drop"). Different designs and denominators — **pick ONE and name the study.** The NEJM 46% national figure is the most defensible.
- **Script framing:** the same industrial white loaf that carries the sodium and the price-fixing history is also the vehicle that cut a devastating birth defect roughly in half.

## "NO PRESERVATIVES" CLAIMS
CFIA guidance (⚠️ canonical URL has moved — re-fetch before quoting verbatim): no objection where the preservative function comes from naturally occurring constituents; but **cultured whey, cultured dextrose, cultured skim milk** can be manipulated to contain propionic/butyric/lactic acids acting as preservatives, and a "no preservatives" claim is **not appropriate** where these are used. Specifically for bakery: it is **misleading** to claim "no preservative added" if **sodium propionate** is introduced indirectly via a **dough-conditioning premix**. Salt and sugar as traditional preservatives are exempt.
