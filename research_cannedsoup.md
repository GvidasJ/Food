1. **No Campbell's soup has been made in Canada since 2019** — Etobicoke plant (opened 1931) closed after Jan 24, 2018 announcement (~380 jobs); Canada now served from Maxton NC, Napoleon OH, Paris TX — **including Habitant, Quebec's own pea soup** (Global News, CBC, Globe and Mail).

# PART 2 — LAW, HISTORY & ECONOMICS

## 1. THE FOP "HIGH IN SODIUM" SYMBOL — legal centrepiece

### 1.1 The regulation — FDR B.01.350 ✅
(https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-B.01.350.html)
- Verbatim (1): "the principal display panel of a prepackaged product must carry a symbol that is set out in Schedule K.1 if (a) the product, as offered for sale, contains a nutrient that is set out in column 1 of the table to this section; and (b) the amount of the nutrient, calculated as a percentage of the daily value, meets or exceeds the applicable threshold"
- Verbatim (2): "the percentage of the daily value for the nutrient is calculated on the basis of the amount of the nutrient, by weight, per serving of stated size or per reference amount, whichever is greater"
- Thresholds (Health Canada FOP industry guide verbatim): ≤30 g/mL RA → ≥10% DV; ">30 g or 30 mL other than main dishes: threshold of ≥ 15% DV"; main dishes ≥200 g → ≥30% DV.
- **Soup's reference amount = 250 mL → soup is in the 15% class.**

### 1.2 Sodium DV and the 345 mg trigger ✅ + DERIVED
- FOP guide verbatim: DVs are "20 g for saturated fat and trans fat, 100 g for sugars and 2300 mg for sodium."
- **OUR ARITHMETIC (safe, label as ours): 15% × 2,300 mg = 345 mg sodium per 250 mL bowl as prepared → symbol mandatory.**
- Symbol design (Health Canada page verbatim): "It has a magnifying glass and highlights what the food is high in: saturated fat, sugars, sodium or any combination of these."

### 1.3 Soup is all over Health Canada's own documents ✅
- Table of Reference Amounts, **Item T.1: "All varieties of soup, including bouillon and broth" — 250 mL**; condensed soup judged on "Amount to make 250 mL final product" — **per bowl as prepared, not per spoonful of concentrate.**
- FOP guide **Figure 8.4 is "Creamy tomato soup with the symbol for 'high in sat fat, sugars and sodium'"** — Health Canada's own example is a soup can wearing a TRIPLE symbol.
- Figure 9.3 "PDS of soup can," verbatim: "cylindrical shaped packages, such as this soup can, don't have a particular surface that is usually visible when displayed for sale. Therefore, 40% of the package's total surface area, excluding the top and bottom is considered the PDS." — **the law measures 40% of the can's curve to place the symbol.**
- Trivia: "individually portioned crackers served with soup" exempt (restaurant portions); dried soup mix exempt only as industrial ingredient.

### 1.4 Mandatory since Jan 1, 2026 — 8 months old ✅
- CFIA verbatim (https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/implementation-plan): "There will be no enforcement discretion after January 1, 2026"; "Products that were imported, manufactured in Canada, or packaged at retail before that date can still be sold and remain on store shelves."
- **Camera beat: because cans last years and pre-2026 stock sells through legally, the September 2026 soup aisle is a visible mix of symbol-wearing and symbol-free cans of the SAME soup.**
- Rule made June 2022 (Health Canada release) with 3.5-year transition. Media naming soups a flagship FOP category: UBC News Jan 2026; Global News explainer.

### 1.5 Honest bowl math (DERIVED — pair with real labels from Part 1)
- Trigger: 345 mg / 250 mL prepared.
- Campbell Canada's own 2010 release: tomato soup reduced to "480 mg per 250 mL serving" → 480/2300 = **21% DV — even the reformulated flagship exceeds the 15% trigger** (COMPUTED from Campbell's stated figure; verify current label).
- Do NOT use 650–890 mg chicken-noodle range until Part 1 supplies actual label numbers.

### 1.6 Regulatory prehistory: voluntary sodium targets ✅
- Health Canada 2020-2025 targets page: Phase 1 (2012, 94 categories); verbatim: "Previous voluntary sodium reduction efforts have yielded modest results." Phase 2: 117 categories; **soup = Category 9a**, 2025 target 240 mg/100 g average, max 320 mg/100 g.
- Arc: voluntary asks (2012) → modest results (2018) → mandatory magnifying glass (2022 rule, hard deadline Jan 1, 2026).
- Industry response (attribute to Campbell): Feb 18, 2010 CNW release — tomato soup cut "an additional 25 per cent to 480 mg per 250 mL serving"… "a nearly 50 per cent reduction over seven years"; removed "the equivalent of more than 30 million teaspoons of salt" since 2003; "more than 80 products at or below 480 mg of sodium per serving." (https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/campbell-canada-serves-up-seven-years-of-sodium-reductions-and-is-hungry-formore-539396341.html)

## 2. STANDARDS

### 2.1 There is NO standard for soup ✅ (verified by absence — phrase carefully)
- CFCS (19 volumes: dairy, meat, salt, vinegar, tea…) — **no volume or entry for soup**. No FDR compositional standard found either.
- **Beat: "chicken noodle soup" has no legal minimum chicken.** Contrast with bread episode: raisin bread must carry raisins ≥50% of flour weight — but chicken soup, legally, owes you no chicken.
- Script phrasing: "we could find no standard prescribing a minimum" (not an absolute).

### 2.2 The quiet law that works — commercial sterility ✅
- FDR B.27.002(1) verbatim: **"No person shall sell a low-acid food packaged in a hermetically sealed container unless the food is commercially sterile."** (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-B.27.002.html)
- B.27.001 definitions verbatim: "commercially sterile" = "the condition obtained in a food that has been processed by the application of heat… to render the food free from viable forms of microorganisms, including spores, capable of growing in the food at temperatures at which the food is designed normally to be held during distribution and storage"; "low-acid food" = pH > 4.6 and water activity > 0.85.
- Exceptions: "Keep Refrigerated"/"Keep Frozen" labels; tomato products pH ≤ 4.7.
- Chicken soup = textbook low-acid food → every can legally must be commercially sterile. **This is the invisible law behind the multi-year pantry staple — frame as "the quiet law that works," never as a scare.**

### 2.3 Common names & highlighted ingredients ✅
- CFIA highlighted-ingredients guidance verbatim: "In principle, any emphasis regarding the presence of an ingredient, component or substance should be accompanied by a statement regarding the amount of that ingredient, component or substance present in the food." No minimum quantities prescribed.
- Soup-specific CFIA detail: pea percentage in pea soup must be based on drained-pea weight only (canning water doesn't count toward the peas).
- With no prescribed standard, "chicken noodle soup" is just the customary common name; backstop = FDA s.5(1) misleading-labelling.

### 2.4 Why is a soup can 284 mL? ✅ + DERIVED (the delightful decode)
- **DERIVED (safe):** 284 mL = 10 imperial fl oz; 540 mL = 19; 796 mL = 28. The weird metric numbers are exact conversions of pre-metric imperial cans.
- CFIA **"Standard Container Sizes"** document (incorporated by reference into SFCR, amended 2024-06-05): canned **vegetables** Table 5 Item 1 verbatim: "284 mL, 398 mL, 540 mL, 796 mL, 1.36 L, 2.65 L, 2.84 L"… — **sizes are still literally written into federal law for the peas and corn one shelf over; soup itself is NOT an enumerated commodity — soup can sizes are convention inherited from the regulated sizes.** (https://inspection.canada.ca/en/about-cfia/acts-and-regulations/list-acts-and-regulations/documents-incorporated-reference/standard-container-sizes)

### 2.5 Best-before on cans is VOLUNTARY ✅
- CFIA date-marking (FDR B.01.007): required only for durable life ≤90 days. Verbatim: "Foods with a shelf life greater than 90 days … are not required to be labelled with a 'best before' date and storage instructions…"
- **Myth-bust beat (mirrors the UK reference video, with Canadian law): the date on the can is not legally required — and CFIA elsewhere says a durable life date "is not an indicator of food safety."**

### 5.4 The can before the opener ✅ (great 50+ beat)
- **Nicolas Appert**, 12,000-franc French prize **1810** (armies on campaign); **Peter Durand**, British patent No. 3372 from George III, **August 25, 1810** — the tin can. By 1820 canned food supplied the Royal Navy.
- **The 48-year gap:** early cans instructed **"Cut round the top near the outer edge with a chisel and hammer."** First US can-opener patent **1858** (Ezra J. Warner, Waterbury CT; adopted in the Civil War) — Smithsonian Magazine.
- Bridge: Canada's official emergency kit still says pack the manual can opener — 216 years on, the can still needs its key.

### 5.5 Aylmer / Baxters — the Canadian canning saga ✅
