## 1. LEAMINGTON & THE TOMATO BELT (fresh angles — NOT the Heinz-ketchup saga)
- "Known since the 20th century as the 'Tomato Capital of Canada'" (Wikipedia, Leamington). Town council courted industry in 1899 with a by-law "offering free water, free gas, exemption from taxation, fire protection, and in some cases a free building site"; "The community's enterprising efforts were rewarded in 1908 when the H.J. Heinz Company decided to locate in Leamington" (leamington.ca history). Use "arrived 1908, opened 1909."
- The water tower "is in the shape and colour of a tomato" (Wikipedia) — B-roll beat.
- THE BIG TOMATO (Visit Leamington, verbatim): "It was opened in 1961 as a collaboration between the Town of Leamington and the H.J. Heinz Company of Canada." "It served as Leamington's official tourist information booth for almost 60 years but was closed in 2019 due to structural concerns." "In early 2024, coinciding with the town's 150th anniversary, restoration work was done on the Big Tomato..." (https://visitleamington.ca/big-tomato/) — 72 Talbot Street West.
- Tomato Festival: grew out of Heinz company picnics; ~31st year by 2014; renamed "Leamington Sunset Tomato Fest" after Heinz pulled out (CBC); last year 2015, defunding needs confirmation (UNVERIFIED). Beat: the Tomato Capital no longer has a tomato festival, but the Big Tomato got restored.
- Greenhouse cluster: "largest concentration of commercial greenhouses in all of North America" (Wikipedia: 1,969 acres); Invest WindsorEssex: "135 businesses spanning 1,300 hectares"; Globe/CIBC: 4,500+ acres Windsor-Essex. SAFE FRAMING: "roughly 2,000-4,500 acres depending on how you draw the line — either way, the largest greenhouse cluster in North America." ## 2. THE CANNING HERITAGE (nostalgia goldmine)
### Canada's first cannery — Picton, 1882
- "in 1882, they built the first successful fruit and vegetable-canning factory in Canada on the corner of West Mary and Spring Streets in Picton" (historyliveshere.ca). Wellington Boulter = "the father of the canning industry in Canada." Prince Edward County = "the Garden County of Canada"; ~50 canneries by 1915; "At its peak in the mid-20th century, 75 canneries operated in The County!" (visitthecounty.com). "Throughout the 1960s and 70s, the canning factories closed their doors one by one..."
### Aylmer — the grandparents' brand (verified chain)
- Factory: joint stock company 1879, Aylmer, Ontario (Elgin County Archives). BRAND: "Seven men registered the Aylmer brand on June 16, 1881 in Aylmer Ontario." (industry veteran Joseph Rider via Aylmer Bulletin: https://www.bulletinaylmer.com/aylmer-tomatoes-stir-up-debate)
- 1903: "thirty of these companies joined to form the Canadian Canners Company... In 1910 Canadian Canners merged with another sixty canneries to form Dominion Canners but in 1923 the company returned to its original name of Canadian Canners." (communitystories.ca / Burlington Historical Society). Hamilton head office.
- Superlative (sourced version): at the 1956 Del Monte deal, Canadian Canners was "the world's second-largest fruit and vegetable canner"; California Packing (Del Monte) "purchased a two-thirds interest in Canadian Canners Limited for a $14-million deal" (fundinguniverse/encyclopedia.com). DO NOT USE "biggest canner in the British Empire."
- The birthplace factory died first: "Canadian Canners rolled out its last cans in 1959... locked for good [1962]. The building was demolished in 1969." (Elgin County Archives)
- CanGro 2008: announced sale/closure of Exeter and St. Davids plants — St. Davids reported as "Canada's last fruit canning plant" (CBC). 2007: Baxters Canada bought the Aylmer/Primo SOUP business from CanGro — why Aylmer soup and Aylmer tomatoes have different owners.
- TODAY: ConAgra "purchased Aylmer Tomatoes from Del Monte Canada in March 2012" (Aylmer Bulletin); Conagra Brands Canada site: "Field to can in just 24 hours. Aylmer products use tomatoes grown in Ontario, Canada."
- DRESDEN PLANT (Chatham Voice, Nov 2023, verbatim): "Dresden is one of Conagra Brands largest processing facilities in Canada and has been preserving Ontario's local produce for over 75 years." "We employee approximately 425 people during the fresh pack season..." Makes AYLMER, HUNT'S (Canadian cans) and RO*TEL; ~140,000 tons of tomatoes/year from ~3,000 acres. THE SAME-FACTORY REVEAL: Aylmer and Hunt's Canada come off the same Dresden line.
- Buy-Canadian tension in one story: an American-owned company (Conagra, Omaha) cans Ontario tomatoes under a Canadian heritage name.
### The tin-can towns
- Simcoe: American Can Company plant (1929-1991) — "one of every two food containers in Canada was made at the Simcoe American Can plant."
- Whitby: Stokely-Van Camp factory built 1943; Stokely also ran an Essex, Ontario operation (LAC fonds).
- Dresden: cannery site since early 1900s ("over 75 years" is the safe line).

## 3. ITALIAN-CANADIAN TOMATO CULTURE (the comment engine)
- CBC Parents: "Why Canning Tomato Sauce Is More Important To Our Family Than Going to the Cottage" — family ordered ~twelve bushels (~600 lb) yielding ~328 bottles (fetch blocked — RE-VERIFY wording before quoting). CBC K-W: "Canning tomatoes is a fall ritual" (Joe Pavia).
- L'Italo-Americano "Sauce Day" (verbatim): "Every year, thousands of Italians across Canada fill back alleys and garages with propane tanks, sauce pots and wooden spoons to make passata di pomodoro (tomato puree) to last the year." Montreal: "in Saint-Michel, Saint-Léonard, Rivière-des-Prairies, and Little Italy — early September had a specific quality in Italian households..." - Pier 21: "During the Pier 21 years 471,940 individuals came to Canada from Italy making them the third largest ethnic group to immigrate between 1928 and 1971." Postwar: ~20,000-30,000/year early 1950s to mid-1960s.
### The pantry institutions (verified)
- UNICO (1917, Toronto): "Unico was established in 1917 to cater to the growing Mediterranean community in Toronto." Founder Edward Pasquale wanted "to bring the 'culture of Italian eating' to every Canadian table." "In 1997, Unico again became a family managed business when Sun-Brite Foods Inc. purchased the company." (unico.ca)
- PRIMO (mid-1950s, Toronto): founded by Primo Poloniato (Emery); family sold 1987; Pillsbury → Nabisco → Kraft; SUN-BRITE ACQUIRED PRIMO FROM KRAFT IN 2006. (Emery Village Voice; Wikipedia)
- SUN-BRITE (1973, Ruthven) — THE HIDDEN GIANT: founded by Onorio (Henry) Iacobelli and wife Lina, "who bought an existing canning plant" — the couple MET WORKING IN A LOCAL CANNERY. First year: 5,000 sq ft, 33,000 cases, "Everything was done by hand. We had 30 ladies peeling tomatoes." Today: ~200,000 sq ft, "70,000 cases of tomato products a day," ~350 harvest workers; "As of 2016, Sun-Brite was Canada's largest tomato processor." Motto: "Success is based on honesty, integrity and hard work." THE SCRIPT'S BEST IRONY: an Italian immigrant cannery worker in Essex County ended up owning BOTH Italian-Canadian pantry brands (Unico 1997, Primo 2006). Also: Sun-Brite "imports most of its cans from the United States" (Wikipedia, 2025) — tariff angle. Client list includes Campbell's and Heinz (co-packing).
- AURORA IMPORTING (1955): founded by Sicilian immigrant Nunzio Tumino; "the largest Italian Dry Food Importer in Canada"; the importer behind many San Marzano DOP tins.
