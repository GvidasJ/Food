# RESEARCH DOSSIER — BREAKFAST CEREAL (CANADA)
## For Canadian Counter — "Cereal" episode (nostalgia-investigation format, counter/label-law spine)
### Research date: August 31, 2026. All prices "at time of research" — re-verify on camera.

**Reference video:** Consumer Exposed cereal (Io3wsSnI1_o, 41K). Reference spine = US "sweetener stacking" mechanism (splitting sugars into 4 names to ride low on the list) + annual-cost device + Kashi lawsuit + teaspoon test. KEY CANADIAN COUNTER-MOVE: **Canada closed the sweetener-stacking loophole — VERIFIED-PRIMARY: FDR B.01.008.3 (added by SOR/2016-305, Dec 14, 2016; transition ended Dec 14, 2021):** sugars-based ingredients must appear "in parentheses immediately following 'Sugars'" in the ingredient list, in descending order by weight (exemptions: single sugar ingredient with "sugar" in its name, sweetening agents alone, juices/purées, <0.5 g/serving). The combined entry sits in the list by its total weight — which is why "Sugars" often lands at ingredient #2 on a Canadian cereal box. Evidence in hand: every Canadian label verified this season shows the format ("Sugars (glucose solids; dextrose)" — Schneiders; "Sugars (glucose)" — no name). So the reference's central trick is dead on Canadian boxes — sugars leap to the top of the list in plain sight. Also: Canadian NFT has NO "added sugars" line (that's US) — total Sugars + %DV instead; the teaspoon test must be rebuilt on total sugars.

**Order (per user): most popular in Canada first.** (Popularity ranking pending Part 1 agent.)

# PART 2 — REGULATION, FOP MATH, FORTIFICATION LAW & ECONOMICS
### (Verbatim from Justice Laws / CFIA / Health Canada / Gazette / StatCan / recalls DB, retrieved Aug 31, 2026)

## 2.1 FOP "HIGH IN SUGARS" — WHO GETS THE MAGNIFYING GLASS (the January beat, 4th payoff)
- FDR B.01.350 (SOR/2022-168; no enforcement discretion since Jan 1, 2026). Sugars DV = **100 g** ⇒ thresholds: **15 g (15%) for RA > 30 g; 10 g (10%) for RA ≤ 30 g**; per stated serving or RA, whichever greater.
- Reference amounts (TRA category C — re-verify row wording on live page): hot cereals 40 g; puffed uncoated 15 g; **flaked/extruded/puffed-coated (20–42 g per 250 mL): 30 g**; fruit-and-nut/granola/biscuit (≥43 g per 250 mL): 55 g.
- **OUR COMPUTATION (from current Canadian labels):**
  - **Froot Loops**: 39 g serving, **14 g sugars (14% DV)** [WK Kellogg SmartLabel Canada] — RA 30 g ⇒ 10 g trigger; serving > RA ⇒ judged on 14 g ⇒ **SYMBOL REQUIRED**
  - **Frosted Flakes**: 38 g serving, **14 g sugars** [SmartLabel] ⇒ **SYMBOL REQUIRED**
  - **Honey Nut Cheerios**: 37 g serving, **12 g sugars (12% DV)** [cheerios.ca — current label; older 28 g/9 g serving is obsolete] ⇒ **SYMBOL REQUIRED**
  - **Shreddies Original**: ~5–6 g per ~47–55 g serving (secondary-sourced — READ BOX; verify which RA row applies) ⇒ 15 g trigger at 55 g RA ⇒ **ESCAPES comfortably**
- THE STORY: dense sweet cereals land in the 30-g bucket with the LOWER 10-g trigger, and their big stated servings (37–39 g) count in full — the childhood mascots get flagged; the dense Canadian wheat pillow walks free.
- CAUTION: never call a borderline product (9–10 g range) either way (rounding rules unverified).

## 2.2 FORTIFICATION — "SAME BOX, DIFFERENT COUNTRY, DIFFERENT LAW" (verified, with the twist)
- **FDR D.03.002**: closed table of what may be added to food; row "Breakfast cereals — Thiamine, niacin, vitamin B6, folic acid, pantothenic acid, magnesium, iron and zinc." **Eight nutrients. NOT on the list: vitamins A, C, D, B12, riboflavin, calcium.** FDR B.13.060 fixes exact per-100g amounts (iron 13.3 mg, etc.). Both sections REMAIN in the FDR (not moved by SOR/2024-244).
- **Same-box evidence (Aug 2026):** US Honey Nut Cheerios lists "Calcium Carbonate, Vitamin C…, Vitamin A (palmitate), Vitamin B2 (riboflavin)…, Vitamin B12, Vitamin D3." Canadian cheerios.ca lists only "Iron, Niacinamide, Calcium pantothenate, Pyridoxine hydrochloride, Folate." Same brand, different chemistry, because Canadian law says so.
- **THE TWIST — DO NOT say "no vitamin D in Canadian cereal" flatly:** Canadian Froot Loops lists "Cholecalciferol (vitamin D3)" (8% DV) under **Temporary Marketing Authorizations issued since 2012** (Gazette RIAS for SOR/2024-88 confirms; Health Canada "plans to address… may include updating the FDR to permit the addition of vitamin D to all breakfast cereals"). Framing: the default law forbids it; some boxes carry it under temporary product-specific permission slips.
- Enriched-flour rule still bites via ingredients: unenriched white flour cannot be sold in food in Canada (CFIA verbatim; Froot Loops contains wheat flour).

## 2.3 COLOURS — legal vs marketing (careful, no health claims)
- Allura red, tartrazine, sunset yellow, brilliant blue = **permitted** in unstandardized foods (List of Permitted Colouring Agents; combination caps ~300 ppm; tartrazine/sunset yellow own-row limits unverified — check before quoting ppm).
- **Naming law:** "the term 'colour' may not be used in the list of ingredients… Food colours must be declared by their specific common names (for example, 'allura red')" — CFIA verbatim. Instrument: **SOR/2016-305 (Dec 14, 2016)**, transition ended Dec 14, 2021. (NOT SOR/2016-74 — correction.)
- **The contrast beat:** synthetic colours are perfectly legal in Canadian cereal — yet verified Canadian Froot Loops colours itself with "concentrated carrot juice… anthocyanin, annatto, turmeric… watermelon juice… blueberry juice… huito juice." That's marketing outrunning the law — and worth saying exactly that way.
- Health Canada's position (their words, if needed): evidence "insufficient to show that synthetic food colours cause this kind of effect." NO health framing beyond quoting them.

## 2.4 "WHOLE GRAIN" CLAIMS — the decoder
- CFIA verbatim: rolled oats/cracked wheat "can be described as 'whole grain cereals'"; "farina, corn meal, white rice, **corn flakes and puffed cereals**… are called 'refined cereals'. The claim 'made from (name of the grain)' should not be used to describe a breakfast cereal that does not contain the whole grain and most of the original nutritive value…"
- **No minimum % exists for "made with whole grains"** — policed only by general misleading rules.
- Health Canada verbatim: "up to 5% of the kernel can be removed" from whole wheat flour ⇒ **"whole wheat" ≠ "whole grain"** in Canada; "'multigrain' and 'organic' are not necessarily whole grain."
- "Made with real fruit" class: CFIA highlighted-ingredients guidance — emphasis "should be accompanied by a statement regarding the amount"; over-emphasis when ingredient "present at very low level."

## 2.5 ADVERTISING TO CHILDREN — exact status Aug 31, 2026 (the mascot beat)
- **No federal law.** Health Canada's 2023 policy to restrict child-directed food ads: pages now marked **"Archived… will not be updating it"** (Dec 2025). Bill C-252 **died at the March 2025 election**. What exists: industry self-regulation (Ad Standards Code, mandatory-per-industry since June 2023, updated early 2026).
- **Quebec is the real thing:** Consumer Protection Act s. 248 verbatim: "no person may make use of commercial advertising directed at persons under thirteen years of age" (1978, c. 9; effective ~1980 — exact in-force date unverified, say "since 1980" loosely or "for over four decades"). Upheld by the Supreme Court in *Irwin Toy* (1989). Covers ALL advertising, not just food — why cereal mascots have always worked differently in Quebec.
- Framing: the mascots on the box are the last unregulated billboard — federal restriction was proposed, archived, and died; only Quebec ever did it.

## 2.6 STANDARDS & ECONOMICS
- **Breakfast cereal is an unstandardized food** — no standard for "corn flakes," "rolled oats," "breakfast cereal" anywhere in CFCS Vol. 12 (full-text checked). Only cereal-specific FDR provision = fortification (B.13.060).
- **StatCan price:** Table 18-10-0245-01 tracks "**Cereal, 400 grams**": Jan 2017 $3.09 → Jan 2024 $4.17 → **June 2026 $4.20** (**≈ +36% since 2017**). (NOT "corn flakes 675 g".)
- **Consumption collapse:** Table 32-10-0054-01, "Breakfast food" kg/person/yr: 1960: 3.05 → 2005 PEAK **5.56** → 2015: 4.72 → 2020: 4.21 → **2025: 3.01** — **down ~46% from peak, below 1960 levels.** Describe as "StatCan's 'Breakfast food' category."
- **Oats — patriotic close:** Cereals Canada verbatim: "As the world's largest exporter of high-quality oats, Canada produces over 3.6 million tonnes of oats per year on average"; "Forty-four percent… produced in Saskatchewan"; "About 81% of Canadian oat exports are shipped to the US, representing a market share of 97%." StatCan 2025 production: 3,919,796 t. America's oatmeal is basically Canadian.

## 2.7 HISTORY (verified, priors corrected)
- **Kellogg's London, Ontario:** corn flakes production in London from ~1905 (Grey St.); Corn Flakes "introduced in Canada" 1914; Kellogg Company of Canada born 1924 (Dundas St.). **Closed December 2014, roughly 500–550 jobs** (CBC ~500 / Food Dive 550 — say "roughly 500"). - **Post Niagara Falls:** plant built **1904** (Shredded Wheat); **Shreddies first produced there 1939** by Nabisco — originally named **"Cubs," renamed Shreddies 1941**; brand chain Nabisco → Post (1993) → Kraft → Ralcorp (2008) → **Post Holdings (2012)**; plant still operating.
