## 🔥 THE LEAD REVEAL
**Lay's Ketchup chips — the most Canadian chip there is — are coloured with AMARANTH (FD&C Red No. 2), banned in the United States since January 1976, permitted in Canada (up to 300 ppm).** The seasoning carries **four synthetic dyes** (amaranth, allura red, sunset yellow FCF, indigotine), MSG — **and zero ketchup** (tomato powder + sugar + vinegar acids). This inverts the usual "other countries get the cleaner version" narrative: on this one, the US banned what Canada eats.

## 1. FRITO-LAY CANADA (PepsiCo)
Canada's largest snack-food maker. Five plants: **Taber AB, Lethbridge AB, Cambridge ON (potato-chip hub), Lauzon/Lévis QC, Kentville NS.** Brands: Lay's, Ruffles, Doritos, Cheetos, Miss Vickie's, Smartfood, Tostitos, SunChips, Fritos, Munchies.
⚠️ Do NOT transplant the US "60% market share" figure to Canada — no Canadian % is citable. "Canada's largest snack food company" is safe.

### Verbatim Canadian labels (tastyrewards.com/en-ca unless noted)
**Lay's Classic:** *"Specially selected potatoes, vegetable oil, salt. Made in Canada from domestic and imported ingredients."* Sodium 330 mg/50 g. Claims: Made in Canada, No Artificial Colours. → **The cheapest flavour is the cleanest.**

**Lay's Ketchup:** *"…Seasoning (sugar, maltodextrin, salt, dextrose, dehydrated vegetables [tomato, garlic, onion], sodium acetate, monosodium glutamate, acetic acid, malic acid, citric acid, modified corn starch, spices, vinegar solids, disodium guanylate, disodium inosinate, **amaranth, allura red, sunset yellow FCF, indigotine**, natural and artificial flavour)."* Sodium 340 mg/50 g (15% DV).

**Lay's All Dressed:** seasoning = maltodextrin, sugar, salt, dextrose, yeast, KCl, sodium acetate, **MSG**, acetic/citric/malic acids, garlic/onion/paprika, disodium inosinate/guanylate. Sodium 330 mg/50 g. **Salt appears twice in the list.** No synthetic dyes (paprika only).

**Lay's Salt & Vinegar:** sodium **410 mg/50 g**. Contains milk (lactose).

**Ruffles All Dressed:** sodium **410 mg per 23 chips (50 g)**.

**Doritos Nacho Cheese (Canada, UPC 060410062227):** seasoning includes MSG, cheddar, romano, caramel, **sunset yellow FCF, tartrazine, allura red**, annatto, turmeric. ~925 mg sodium/100 g.
🔥 **Canada is NOT cleaner than the US here** — same three dye classes under Canadian names. But **UK Doritos Tangy Cheese uses only natural colours** (paprika extract, annatto, plain caramel + rosemary extract), marketed "no artificial colours." Same company. The dye-free recipe exists; Canada doesn't get it.

**Cheetos Crunchy (Canada):** enriched cornmeal, oil, seasoning (modified milk ingredients, cheddar, MSG, **sunset yellow FCF**…). Sodium **440 mg/50 g** — highest checked. Canada also gets **Cheetos Crunchy Ketchup** (Canada-market flavour).

**Miss Vickie's Original:** *"Specially Selected Potatoes, Vegetable Oil, Sea Salt."* Sodium **120 mg/50 g (5% DV)** — cleanest, lowest-sodium mainstream chip.
🔥 Founded 1987 by **Vickie Kerr** of New Lowell, Ontario — **sold to Hostess Frito-Lay February 1, 1993.** The farm-family kettle brand has been PepsiCo for 30+ years.

**Smartfood Simply White Cheddar:** no artificial colours/flavours/preservatives — but **yeast extract** (free-glutamate source).

**Tostitos Cantina:** *"Corn, Vegetable Oil, Salt."* **Simply Tostitos Yellow Corn is organic** — the "Simply" clean sub-line exists in Canada (also Simply Cheetos White Cheddar).

## 2. OTHER MAJORS

**Old Dutch** — Parent: **Old Dutch Foods Inc., Roseville, Minnesota** (family firm since 1934). Canadian head office Winnipeg since 1954; plants Winnipeg, Calgary, Airdrie, Hartland NB, Dartmouth NS, Mississauga. Bought Humpty Dumpty (C$26.7M, 2006).
⚠️ olddutchfoods.ca calls the Canadian subsidiary "a family-owned Canadian Company" — the ultimate parent is the Minnesota family firm. **Present both; don't flatten.**
**Old Dutch Ketchup:** no MSG listed but **hydrolyzed corn protein** (free-glutamate source); colour partly **beet**; website still shows generic "COLOUR" (labels must name colours specifically since the 2016 amendments — website listings can lag packs).

**Pringles (Kellanova)** — 🔥 **In the US the can must say "potato CRISPS"; the Canadian label says "potato CHIPS."** First ingredient dried potatoes, then oil, corn flour, corn starch, rice flour, maltodextrin, mono-/diglycerides, wheat starch. Canada gets **All Dressed and Ketchup Pringles**. ⚠️ The "~42% potato" figure is from a 2008 UK VAT case — do not state for current Canadian cans.

**Takis Fuego (Barcel/Grupo Bimbo)** — Canadian label: MSG + **hydrolyzed soybean protein** + disodium inosinate/guanylate, **Allura Red, Sunset Yellow FCF**, oil "canola/palm/modified palm" (non-committal). Made in Mexico and/or US — check the bag.

**Cheez-It (Kellanova)** — launched in Canada **January 2020**. Canadian Original uses **annatto** (natural) per SmartLabel.

**Bugles Ranch (General Mills, Canadian UPC 0065633496088):** *"…Coconut oil and/or palm kernel oil and/or modified palm kernel oil, Sugars, Salt, …MSG, …**BHT**."* — **the only mainstream chip-aisle product found with BHT on a Canadian label.** Most Canadian chips use no BHT/TBHQ at all. (BHT/BHA/TBHQ permitted in Canada in fats/oils ≤0.02%.)

**Hostess (history):** founded 1935 near Cambridge ON (Edward Snyder); Canada's #1 chips for decades; 1987 PepsiCo partnership brought Doritos to Canada; 1988 merger into Hostess Frito-Lay; **brand replaced by Lay's in 1996.**

## 4. CANADIAN ANGLES

**Ketchup chips:** early 1970s; **Hostess made them famous** (its orange and grape chips flopped; ketchup survived). ⚠️ Origin disputed — Herr's (PA) concurrent claim; a 1968 Scottish "tomato sauce flavour" ad predates both. Say "made famous by Canada's Hostess," NOT "invented in Canada."

**All Dressed:** Quebec's **Yum Yum chips claims creation in 1978**; national brands adopted in the '80s; **Ruffles took All Dressed to the US in 2015, discontinued 2021** — America gave it back. Seasoning decode: ketchup + BBQ + sour-cream-onion + salt-vinegar components in one.

**Food colours:** 10 synthetic colours permitted in Canada — allura red, tartrazine, sunset yellow, **amaranth**, indigotine included. **Never say "banned in Canada."** Since the 2016 FDR amendments (transition ended Dec 14 2021), colours must be declared **by individual name**. Health Canada reviewed the child-behaviour studies and concluded evidence *"insufficient to show that synthetic food colours cause this kind of effect."*

**MSG shell game:** CFIA — "no MSG added" claims are **"considered misleading and deceptive"** when other free-glutamate sources are present. The delivery vehicles on Canadian labels: hydrolyzed corn protein (Old Dutch), yeast extract (Smartfood), hydrolyzed soybean protein (Takis).

**Sodium targets (Health Canada 2020–2025, voluntary):** chips/popcorn SWA **400 mg/100 g, max 750**; extruded cheese snacks SWA 790, max 980. Computed from labels (⚠️ flag as calculation): Lay's Classic 660 · Lay's Ketchup 680 · **Ruffles All Dressed & Lay's S&V 820 — ABOVE the 750 voluntary max** · Cheetos 880 (vs 980 extruded max) · **Miss Vickie's 240 — the only one under the 400 target.** The June 2025 interim assessment didn't cover salty snacks.

**🔥 FRONT-OF-PACK SYMBOL (the big Canadian angle):** SOR/2022-168, Canada Gazette II July 20 2022, **mandatory since January 1, 2026** — black-and-white magnifying glass, "High in / Élevé en" sodium/sugars/sat fat, with "Health Canada" attribution printed on the pack. Threshold ≥**15% DV** per reference amount (sodium DV 2300 mg → **345 mg line**); ≥10% DV for reference amounts ≤30 g. Applied (⚠️ my calculation — verify bags in-store): **Ruffles All Dressed 410 mg (18% DV), Lay's S&V 410 mg, Cheetos 440 mg (19% DV) cross the line → "High in sodium" symbol. Lay's Classic (330) and Lay's Ketchup (340) sit 5–15 mg UNDER the 345 line** — a threshold-skimming story worth filming on-shelf.

**Shrinkflation (CBC-documented):** **Lay's 200 g → 180 g (−10%); Doritos snack bag 80 g → 72 g (−10%).** ⚠️ "200→165 g" is NOT documented as a shrink step (165 g exists as a current listing).

**🔥 The 2022 chip war:** February 2022 — **Frito-Lay Canada stopped shipping to every Loblaw banner** after Loblaw refused a sub-10% price increase. Chip aisles sat stocked with PC/No Name. Resolved ~April 2022.

**Recalls pattern:** the most common Canadian chip recall is **undeclared milk in seasoning** — Old Dutch Ridgies Sour Cream, Green Onion & Bacon (2025), Hardbite Wild Onion & Yogurt (2023), Circle K Classic (2023).

**Price:** StatCan Table 18-10-0245-01 tracks potato chips — ⚠️ pull the live number before scripting.

## 5. STRONGEST REVEALS — ranked
1. **Lay's Ketchup uses amaranth — banned in the US since 1976, legal in Canada.** Four dyes, zero ketchup, on the most Canadian chip.
2. **The magnifying glass arrived Jan 1 2026** — Ruffles All Dressed, Lay's S&V and Cheetos cross the 345 mg line; Lay's Classic and Ketchup sit 5–15 mg under it.
3. **Same Doritos, different continents** — Canadian bags carry tartrazine/sunset yellow/allura red; UK bags are dye-free. The clean recipe exists.
4. **Miss Vickie's is PepsiCo** (sold Feb 1 1993) — and its Original is the cleanest mainstream chip.
5. **The cheapest flavour is the cleanest** — Lay's Classic is three ingredients.
6. **February 2022: Frito-Lay cut off every Loblaws in Canada** over a price increase.
7. **Old Dutch answers to Roseville, Minnesota.**
8. **US cans say "potato crisps"; Canadian Pringles say "potato chips"** — first ingredient dried potatoes plus four other starches.
9. **The CFIA itself calls "no MSG added" claims misleading** when hydrolyzed proteins/yeast extract are present.
10. **Ruffles All Dressed & Lay's S&V exceed Health Canada's voluntary sodium max** (820 vs 750 mg/100 g) — and the targets expired mostly unmet.
11. **Hawkins Cheezies: 7 ingredients, same machine since the 1940s, inventor stayed VP until 2012** — but the orange includes tartrazine.
12. **Ketchup chips contain no ketchup.**
13. **Shrinkflation on record:** Lay's 200→180 g, Doritos 80→72 g.
14. **All Dressed was invented in Quebec (Yum Yum, 1978)** — the US had it 2015–2021, then lost it.
15. **The most common chip recall in Canada is undeclared milk** — an allergy-label failure, not a horror story.

# PART 2 — REGULATION & MARKET (agent 2, compiled 2026-08-20)

## Front-of-pack symbol — exact law
- **SOR/2022-168**, registered June 28 2022, Canada Gazette Part II Vol. 156 No. 15, **July 20 2022**. Transition ended Dec 31 2025; **mandatory January 1 2026**; CFIA enforcing (pre-2026 stock may sell through).
- Symbol: black-and-white **magnifying glass**, "High in / Élevé en" + Sat fat / Sugars / Sodium bars + **"Health Canada / Santé Canada" attribution printed on the pack.**
- Thresholds (per reference amount or stated serving, whichever greater; sodium DV 2,300 mg): RA ≤30 g → **≥10% DV (230 mg)**; general foods (chips: **RA = 50 g**) → **≥15% DV (345 mg sodium, 3 g sat fat, 15 g sugars)**; main dishes ≥200 g → 30% DV.
- **Chips tripwire: ≥345 mg sodium per 50 g.** The ≤30 g rule does NOT apply to chips.
- RIAS economics: benefits **$2.33B** PV vs costs **$894.93M** over 15 yrs → **net ≈ $1.43B**. ⚠️ the circulating "$2.36B net benefit" figure is wrong.
- U of T FLIP research: ~**63.9% of packaged foods** would need a symbol; **74.4% of child-appealing packages**.
- 🔥 **Ground beef and pork were in the June 2022 announcement — ranchers' "vilification" campaign won an exemption in the final rule. Chips got no such mercy.**
- **International: Canada is first in the G7 with a mandatory FOP warning.** US: FDA only *proposed* a rule Jan 16 2025, no final rule, 3–4 yr compliance after finalization. UK traffic lights: voluntary.

## Dyes — Canada/EU/US truth table
- Canada permits (with limits): Allura Red, Tartrazine, Sunset Yellow FCF, **Amaranth**, Indigotine, Brilliant Blue, Erythrosine, Fast Green, Ponceau SX, Citrus Red 2.
- **SOR/2016-305**: generic "colour" killed; every dye named individually; transition ended Dec 14 2021.
- Canada: HC — evidence *"insufficient to show that synthetic food colours cause this kind of effect"*; will act on new data. **EU since July 20 2010**: six Southampton colours (incl. Tartrazine, Sunset Yellow, Allura Red) must carry *"may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children"* (Reg. 1333/2008 Annex V). **US**: Red 3 banned Jan 2025 (eff. 2027); April 2025 **voluntary** phase-out of the six petroleum dyes by end-2026 — not a ban; California school-food ban 2027; West Virginia's 2028 retail ban **blocked by a judge** Dec 2025.
- **Punchline: same dye, three tools — Canada names it, the EU warns about it, the US is banning it state-by-state.**
- **PepsiCo Q2 2025**: Lay's and Tostitos to be reformulated without artificial colours by end-2025; dye-free Doritos/Cheetos versions launched **alongside** originals; >60% of portfolio already colour-free. ⚠️ US corporate statements — Canadian formulations not confirmed; check bags in-store.

## Additives
- TBHQ/BHA/BHT permitted in Canada in fats/oils ≤0.02% (combined). Not found on Frito-Lay Canada chip labels; **Bugles (BHT) is the on-shelf example.**
- **MSG:** Health Canada verbatim — "contains no MSG"/"no added MSG" claims are **"considered misleading and deceptive"** when other free-glutamate sources present (HVP, soy sauce, autolyzed yeast extract, tomatoes, Parmesan).
- Acrylamide: HC advises eating fried snacks "less often"; 2024 HC-scientist study measured Canadian chips at **57–4,660 ng/g (80-fold spread), one brand consistently highest** (brand not named). California 2008 settlement forced 275 ppb. ⚠️ fear-adjacent — use sparingly or not at all.

## Sodium policy
- Targets (voluntary, 2020–2025): chips/popcorn **SWA 400 / max 750 mg/100 g**; extruded corn snacks SWA 790 / max 980.
- **The failure record:** 2012–2016 program — only **14% of 94 categories** met targets, 48% no progress. June 2025 interim assessment (9 categories, snacks NOT assessed): none met targets, some got saltier. **Voluntary flopped twice → that's why the label became law.** ← the causal spine of the video.
- Canadians average **2,760 mg/day**; **58% exceed limits (72% of kids 4–13)**.

## Market / stories
- 1935: Edward Snyder starts frying chips on his mother's stove in Breslau ON → Hostess; 1988 Hostess–Frito-Lay merger; PepsiCo full control 1992; renamed Frito-Lay Canada 2002.
- Cambridge ON plant 550+ employees (only citable Canadian employment figure).
- **Feb 2022 chip war:** Frito-Lay halted ALL shipments to Loblaw over a <10% price increase; Loblaw "laser focused" on prices; aisles filled with PC/No Name; Charlebois: *"the tip of the iceberg… not just about chips."* Resolved after **~2 months, ending mid-April 2022** (terms never disclosed).
- Potatoes: 4th consecutive record crop 2024 — 127.0M cwt; PEI largest seeded area; Alberta biggest by volume (23.7%).
- Slack fill: **Consumer Packaging and Labelling Act s.9(1)** — container must not be "manufactured, constructed or filled… in such a manner that a consumer might reasonably be misled with respect to the quality or quantity." Nitrogen cushioning legal so long as net quantity accurate and not misleading.
- Shrinkflation: CBC Marketplace (Nov 2022) — chips "downsized multiple times"; **Doritos snack bag 80→72 g caught only because the calorie count changed.**
- Snacking: 48% of Canadians report snacking on chips (Ipsos).
- Canada's Food Guide: highly processed foods — **limit**, not fear.
