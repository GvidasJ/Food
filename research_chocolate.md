# PART 2 — REGULATION, COCOA ECONOMICS, HISTORY

## a. The law (verbatim; CFCS Vol. 4 post-SOR/2024-244 — "same numbers, new address," in force continuity confirmed by CFIA)
- **4.1.8 Milk Chocolate** (ex-B.04.008) verbatim core: made of cocoa liquor/cocoa butter/cocoa powder + sweetening; **≥25% total cocoa solids (≥15% cocoa butter, ≥2.5% fat-free cocoa solids), ≥12% milk solids, ≥3.39% milk fat**; may contain <5% whey, spices, flavourings (except imitation chocolate/milk flavours), salt.
- **4.1.6 Chocolate/Dark/Bittersweet/Semi-sweet:** ≥35% total cocoa solids (≥18% cocoa butter, ≥14% fat-free cocoa solids). **4.1.9 White Chocolate:** ≥20% cocoa butter, ≥14% milk solids, ≥3.5% milk fat. Cocoa liquor ≥50% cocoa butter; cocoa powder ≥10%.
- 🔥 **The mechanism: the permitted-ingredient lists contain NO vegetable fat whatsoever.** Only cocoa fats + milk fat may be in anything named chocolate.
- Quirk: "sweetening ingredient… except for icing sugar" — icing sugar is the one sugar you can't legally make chocolate with.
- **CFIA labelling guidance verbatim (the "chocolatey" decode):**
  - "Products with… added vegetable fats other than fat from cocoa or chocolate products… are unstandardized foods and **cannot use a standardized common name**."
  - Compound coatings: "There should be no indication that compound coatings are 'chocolate'. However, **'chocolate flavoured', 'chocolate-like' and 'chocolaty' have been accepted as appropriate descriptions**."
  - 🔥 "a garnish that is referred to as **'chocolate shavings' or 'chocolate chips' must be real chocolate**." (→ "chocolatey chips" = the confession)
  - "Only a solid chocolate candy, bar or other completely chocolate product can be labelled… **'chocolate bar'**" — coated candy = "chocolate coated bar."
  - No-sugar-added: additive sweeteners (maltitol etc.) are "specifically excluded" → **"no sugar added chocolate" legally isn't chocolate** (must be renamed, e.g. "milk chocolate sweetened with maltitol").
  - "Cacao" allowed on front ("70% cacao") but the ingredient list must say "cocoa."
- 🔥 **International contrast — Canada is STRICTER than the EU:** EU Directive 2000/36/EC allows up to **5% non-cocoa vegetable fats** (shea/palm/illipe/sal/kokum/mango kernel) in products still named "chocolate" (with disclosure line "contains vegetable fats in addition to cocoa butter"). US milk chocolate (21 CFR 163.130): cacao fat only BUT floor is just 10% chocolate liquor vs Canada's 25% cocoa solids. FAIRNESS: "different frameworks, not bad faith" — never "US/EU chocolate is fake."
- "Modified milk ingredients" → being renamed **"milk-derived ingredients," mandatory Jan 1, 2030** (CFIA common-names change) — the label line viewers squinted at for 20 years is being retired.

## b. FOP symbols (in force Jan 1, 2026 — the shelf-walk visual)
- Chocolate reference amount = **40 g** (HC Table verbatim: "Candies, confectionaries and chocolates… 40 g") → 15% DV thresholds: **3 g saturated fat / 15 g sugars per 40 g**.
- DERIVED (present as on-screen arithmetic): cocoa butter ≈60% sat fat (⚠️ cite CNF/USDA if numbered) → typical milk chocolate ≈6–8 g sat fat + 18–24 g sugars per 40 g → **most bars now wear BOTH "High in saturated fat" AND "High in sugars"** magnifying glasses. First Halloween season with the changed shelf. Verify per-bar NFTs before naming brands.
- Irony beat (neutral): plain sugar is EXEMPT ("butter, sugar, salt…" exemption — symbol deemed redundant); a chocolate bar is not. High-cacao dark may dodge the sugars symbol (verify per bar).
- Landing: "You used to have to flip the bar. Now the label does the scanning."

## c. Cocoa economics (the timeliness)
- Price ride: ~US$2,500/t through 2022 → **record just under US$13,000/t mid-December 2024** (Bloomberg: broke $12,000 Dec 18, 2024; worst West African supply shortfall in 60+ years — Côte d'Ivoire + Ghana ≈60% of supply; disease, weather, aging trees — attribute causes to reporting) → **≈US$6,233/t as of Aug 27, 2026** ("roughly half the peak, still ~2.5x the old normal").
- **StatCan verbatim (CPI Annual Review 2025, released Jan 19, 2026): Canadians paid "20.3% more for coffee and 7.1% more for confectionery in 2025."** (No chocolate-only CPI series exists — say "confectionery.")
- 🔥 **CBC shrinkflation receipts (Sophia Harris, Oct 31, 2025 — attribute):** same $8.99, same bar count: Mars 65-bar fun-size box **782 g (2024) → 672 g (2025), −14%**; Mars 25-pack −16.8%; Hershey's 50-pack 513→481 g (−6.2%); 100-pack −3.8%. Company responses quoted fairly (Mars: "focus… on delivering great value"; Hershey: assortment changes "can have an impact on weight"). **"The scale is the only witness."**
- Reformulation creep: UK Dec 2025 Nestlé Toffee Crisp/Blue Riband dropped below milk-chocolate threshold → "smooth milk chocolate flavour coating" (⚠️ trade-press sourced — verify primary before air; NEVER imply Canadian versions changed). Canadian framing: "watch the wrapper — when 'milk chocolate' quietly becomes 'chocolatey coating,' that's the law doing its job."
- Industry: confectionery sector sales +7.6% in 2025 (FCC); ~600+ establishments, mostly ON/QC; US takes 97% (~$2.7B) of Canada's chocolate exports (USDA FAS). Canada grows zero cocoa yet manufactures at scale.

## d. History (Canadian gold)
- **Ganong Bros., St. Stephen NB, founded 1873 — Canada's oldest candy company, 5th-generation family-run.** 1910: Arthur Ganong + George Ensor, wrapping chocolate for a fishing trip, created what heritage sources call **"the first 5-cent chocolate nut bar in North America"** (later named Pal-o-Mine, 1920 — one of the oldest continuously produced bars). ⚠️ NEVER "Canada invented the chocolate bar" (Fry's 1840s) — approved phrasing: "by most accounts, the first five-cent chocolate NUT bar in North America." Heart-shaped-box claim = company lore, "the company says" only.
- **Laura Secord (1913):** Frank P. O'Connor, one shop on Yonge St., named for the War of 1812 heroine. Ownership odyssey: O'Connor → **John Labatt (1969)** → **Rowntree Mackintosh (UK, 1983)** → (Nestlé's Rowntree takeover) → **Archibald Candy (Chicago)** → **Gordon Brothers (2004)** → **Jean & Jacques Leclerc, Quebec City (Feb 2010, ~$20M via Nutriart)** — **Canadian-owned again after four decades**. 2025-26: still Leclerc-owned; some mall-store closures 2025; **July 2025 CFIA recall of certain Laura Secord/Nutriart chocolate chips (undeclared peanuts)** — state flatly. ⚠️ Store counts unverified.
- 🔥 **Smiths Falls — the arc of the century:** Hershey plant opened early 1960s, ~750 workers at peak, Chocolate Shoppe tours = Eastern Ontario childhood; **Feb 15, 2007 closure announced**, production to Mexico, closed Dec 2008 ("hundreds of jobs" — counts conflict). 2013-14: **Tweed/Canopy Growth** cannabis HQ (the legal-weed boom's poster child). Feb 2023: Canopy closes flagship; **Aug 2023: site sold BACK to Hershey Canada ~$53M**. 2025-26: no confirmed reopening date — CBC Mar 2025 "town awaits"; Mayor Pankow: talks "very high-level… very premature." **One building = chocolate century + cannabis bubble + cocoa-crisis era. Chocolate outlived the weed boom.**
- **Cadbury Gladstone Ave, Toronto:** built by **William Neilson from 1906**; Cadbury bought Neilson's chocolate business 1996; now Mondelez; **~350+ employees, ~500 million bars/yr — every Cadbury bar sold in Canada (Caramilk, Dairy Milk, Mr. Big, Crunchie, Mini Eggs) made there**; $37M reno 2019; Torontonians rallied to it during the 2025 trade war.
- **Nestlé Sterling Rd, Toronto** (ex-Rowntree): runs 24/7 — **KitKat, Aero, Smarties, Coffee Crisp**. **Coffee Crisp = Canadian original since 1939** (~153M bars/yr, made only here; basically unavailable in the US). **Canadian Smarties ≠ US Smarties** (name owned by a different US candy company). Big 50+ nostalgia beat.
- **Moirs, Halifax/Dartmouth (gone):** lineage to 1815; **Pot of Gold introduced 1928**; Hershey bought 1987; Dartmouth plant closed **Dec 21, 2007** (~580 workers), production to Mexico — same wave as Smiths Falls. Pot of Gold survives as a Hershey brand.
- 🔥 **Price-fixing case (conduct ~2002-2008) — precise outcomes MANDATORY:** **Hershey Canada pleaded guilty (June 2013), fined $4M** (leniency for cooperating). Charges laid against Nestlé Canada, Mars Canada, ITWAL + 3 executives (Leonidas, Martinez, Stevens) — **ALL STAYED by Nov 17, 2015** (PPSC; Competition Bureau: "Today's decision marks the end of the chocolate price-fixing matter"). **"No one but Hershey Canada was convicted; stayed charges are unproven allegations" — say this sentence on air.** Civil: class actions settled **$23.2M** (2013), without admission of liability. → The bread price-fixing story had a chocolate-coated prequel.
