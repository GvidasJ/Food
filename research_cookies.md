## 8. LABEL DECODES
### 8.1 "Chocolate" vs "chocolatey" (the law)
- Chocolate standards: "not less than 35% total cocoa solids, of which not less than 18% is cocoa butter"; products where vegetable fats replace cocoa butter "cannot legally be called chocolate when sold in Canada" → "chocolatey" (CFIA confectionery page + CFCS).

# PART 2 — LAW, HISTORY & ECONOMICS

## 1. FOP SYMBOL & COOKIES — legal centrepiece ✅

### 1.1 Cookies are a 30-gram food
- Health Canada Table of Reference Amounts, **Item A.10: "Cookies, with or without coating or filling; graham wafers, macarons, coconut macaroons" — reference amount 30 g** (vs Item T.1 soup 250 mL). https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/technical-documents-labelling-requirements/nutrition-labelling-table-reference-amounts-food.html

### 1.2 The stricter class — NAILED
- FDR B.01.350(1) verbatim (symbol required when nutrient "meets or exceeds the applicable threshold"); **B.01.350(2) verbatim: %DV "calculated on the basis of the amount of the nutrient, by weight, per serving of stated size or per reference amount, whichever is greater."** — the anti-dodge clause: printing a smaller serving can't escape; 30 g is the floor.
- Health Canada FOP guide Table 4.1: **RA ≤30 g → symbol at ≥10% DV**; general foods 15%; main dishes 30%.
- **THE BEAT: cookies (30 g = last gram of the small class) trigger at 10% DV; soup triggers at 15%. The law holds a cookie to a stricter line than a bowl of soup.**

### 1.3 DVs + honest math (OUR arithmetic — anchor to real NFT panels on camera)
- DVs: sugars **100 g**, sat+trans fat **20 g**, sodium 2,300 mg.
- 10% sugars = 10 g per 30 g serving → typical chocolate-chip serving (~10–12 g) triggers "High in sugars." 10% sat fat = just **2 g** → typical serving (~3–4 g) far over → "High in saturated fat." (Typical figures = illustrative; read printed NFTs on camera — flag #13.)
- No Health Canada worked example uses cookies — present as arithmetic on public DVs, never as their example.

### 1.4–1.5 Symbol + deadline ✅
- "Black and white… magnifying glass… high in: saturated fat, sugars, sodium" (Health Canada verbatim). Mandatory Jan 1, 2026, "no enforcement discretion after"; pre-2026 stock sells through (CFIA). Underlying amendment SOR/2022-168 (Canada Gazette July 20, 2022).
- FoodNavigator-USA (Jan 2026) makes our exact point re: lower limit "for prepackaged foods eaten a little bit at a time, like cookies" — fetch before quoting verbatim.

## 2. STANDARDS & NAMING ✅

### 2.1 The cookie is legally undefined
- CFCS (19 volumes; Vol 12 Grain & Bakery) — **no standard for "cookie"/"biscuit" anywhere** (verified on consolidated index; Vol 12 standalone URL 410'd; FDR B.13 not separately paged — phrase as "we could find no standard"). Bread is standardized; the cookie is not.

### 2.2 Chocolate law — the chip that dare not speak its name
- **CFCS Vol 4 s. 4.1.6 verbatim:** "Chocolate, Bittersweet Chocolate, Semi-sweet Chocolate or Dark Chocolate… contains not less than 35% total cocoa solids, of which (i) not less than 18% is cocoa butter…"
- CFIA confectionery page verbatim: products with "added vegetable fats other than fat from cocoa or chocolate products… cannot use a standardized common name"; **"'Chocolate flavoured', 'chocolate-like' and 'chocolaty' have been accepted as appropriate descriptions of such coatings and chips."** Chips called "chocolate chips" must be real chocolate.
- Even the Girl Guides' fall cookie is officially the **"Chocolatey Mint."**

### 2.3 Sugars grouping — FDR B.01.008.3 ✅
- Sugars-based ingredients grouped in parentheses after "Sugars" (FR "Sucres"), descending order; SOR/2016-305 s. 5; transition ended Dec 14, 2021. Oreo's first ingredient is the showcase.

### 2.4 "Made with real butter" rules ✅
- CFIA highlighted-ingredients verbatim: emphasis "should be accompanied by a statement regarding the amount"; "Over-emphasis occurs when a highlighted ingredient is either present at very low level or at least partially replaced by a similar ingredient…" Worked example: a "Butter Cake" with 5% butter + 8% canola = "not acceptable" without clarification. Statutory backbone: SFCA s. 6(1) / SFCR 199(1)(b).

### 2.5 Bilingual beat ✅
- FDR B.01.012(2): label info "shall be shown in both official languages." French for cookie = **"biscuit"** → the classic "Cookies / Biscuits" package face. A Peek Freans box = a British biscuit labelled "cookie" in English and "biscuit" in French. ("Biscuit" = Latin *bis coctus*, twice-baked — safe trivia.)
