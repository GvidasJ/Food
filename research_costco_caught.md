### 1.3 THE PATTERN — the real finding
**When the product is a DRUG or NATURAL HEALTH PRODUCT, the manufacturer is named. When it is FOOD, Costco is named.**
Costco-named food recalls: Traditional Basmati Rice 5 kg ("Costco Wholesale Canada Ltd.", 2025-08-26); Four Cheese & Spinach Manicotti (2023-01-16); Oatmeal Cranberry White Chocolate Cookie (2023-10-11); Pepperoni Pizza (2019-02-12); Non-GMO Infant Formula (2019-10-25); Creamy Almond Butter ("Costco Canada Inc.", 2020-01-30).
**THE REASON IS THE LAW — best explainer beat in the piece:**
- NHP Regulations Part 5 (ss. 86-95) + FDR require an NHP/drug label to carry the **product licence holder's name and address** with the NPN/DIN. Canadian law forces a real company name onto a Kirkland vitamin bottle. (Health Canada Labelling Requirements Checklist.)
- **For FOOD there is no such rule.** CFIA verbatim: "All prepackaged foods that require a label must declare the name and principal place of business" of the person who manufactured it "**or of the person for whom** the food has been manufactured, prepared, produced, stored, packaged or labelled." (https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/name-and-principal-place-business)
**SCRIPT LINE (airtight):** "Your Kirkland multivitamin is legally required to tell you who made it. Your Kirkland lasagna is not. That's not Costco being sneaky — that's Canadian law. But Costco chooses to use the gap."
