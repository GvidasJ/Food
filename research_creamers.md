# PART 1 — BRANDS & LABELS

## 1. COFFEE-MATE (Nestlé) — "Canada's preferred coffee whitener" (their words)
- **Original Powder** [OFFICIAL — Nestlé Professional Canada]: "Glucose syrup, Hydrogenated palm kernel oil, Polydextrose, Dipotassium phosphate, Modified milk ingredients, Calcium carbonate, Mono- and diglycerides, DATEM, Natural and artificial flavour, Turmeric oleoresin, Annatto."
  - **THE PARADOX, on one official page:** claims "Cholesterol free," "Lactose free," "non-dairy whitener," "2-Year Shelf Life" — AND the allergen statement **"Contains: Milk."** The single most camera-ready receipt in the episode (screenshot the page + read the physical canister).
  - First ingredient = glucose syrup (a sugar); second = hydrogenated palm kernel oil. **No cream anywhere.**
- **Original Liquid singles** (the diner "cream cup") [OFFICIAL]: "WATER, COCONUT OIL, SUGAR, SODIUM CASEINATE (FROM MILK), DIPOTASSIUM PHOSPHATE, ACETYLATED TARTARIC ACID ESTERS OF MONO- AND DIGLYCERIDES, MONO- AND DIGLYCERIDES, ARTIFICIAL FLAVOUR, COLOUR." + "Contains Milk." 9-month unrefrigerated shelf life.
- **Flavoured liquids — Canadian legal name: "Liquid Coffee Enhancer."** French Vanilla 956 mL [TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "Water, sugar, high oleic soy oil, sodium caseinate (milk), dipotassium phosphate, DATEM, cellulose gel, mono- and diglycerides, cellulose gum, flavour and artificial flavour, carrageenan."
- Canadian range includes **"Double Double-Mate"** and Nestlé-candy limited editions: KIT KAT, Coffee Crisp, TURTLES, AERO Coconut Crème (madewithnestle.ca) — the Coffee Crisp crossover is a fun callback to the chocolate episode.
- History (verified): launched by **Carnation Company, February 1961**; Nestlé's ~US$3B Carnation acquisition announced Sept 1984, completed Jan 1985. MANDATORY: "announced 1984, completed 1985, roughly US$3 billion" — never "exactly $3B in 1985."
- Prices at time of research: powder 450 g $6.47 (Amazon.ca — Walmart/Superstore blocked, capture in-store); Double Double-Mate 946 mL **$4.50** Superstore; French Vanilla value 1.89 L $9.49.

## 2. INTERNATIONAL DELIGHT — American-owned, made in Canada by… Saputo
- Ownership [VERIFIED]: brand = **WhiteWave → Danone** (Paris) since 2017; Canadian site footer "©2026 WhiteWave Services, Inc." BUT WhiteWave's SEC 10-K: Saputo "manufactures and sells WhiteWave products in Canada." **The label boast "proudly prepared in Canada" = prepared by Montreal's Saputo for a French-owned American brand.**
- **French Vanilla** [OFFICIAL — internationaldelight.ca]: "Water, Sugar, Palm oil, Sodium caseinate* (milk), Dipotassium phosphate, Sodium citrate, Carrageenan, Mono and diglycerides, Sodium stearoyl lactylate, Salt, Natural and artificial flavours." Footnote: "Sodium caseinate is not a source of lactose."
  - First three: water, sugar, palm oil. **No cream. No vanilla bean named — "natural and artificial flavours."**
  - Nutrition: **35 cal, 5 g sugars per 15 mL** — the FOP-sugars beat (Part 2).
- Flavour range (official): Chocolate Chip Cookie, Pumpkin Spice, Pistachio, Cinnabon®, Maple Latte, Vanilla Toffee Caramel, Zero Sugar lines, Cold Foam…
- Price: 946 mL **$5.00 No Frills / $5.99 Superstore** at time of research.

## 3. CARNATION — the twist: it's not Nestlé here, and it's real milk
- Nestlé sold the CANADIAN Carnation canned-milk business to **Smucker Foods of Canada** effective Oct 1, 2007 (~$50M annual sales); carnationmilk.ca legal line: "Smucker Foods of Canada Corp… Carnation is a trademark of Société des Produits Nestlé S.A., used under license."
- **No Carnation-branded whitener exists in Canada today** — Carnation here = real evaporated milk ("milk with about half the water removed"). Evaporated 2% [TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "Evaporated partly skimmed milk, Sodium ascorbate (vitamin C), Carrageenan, Vitamin A palmitate, Vitamin D3."
- Beat: the old-school evaporated-milk-in-coffee generation was closer to real dairy than the modern creamer aisle.

## 4. STORE-BRAND WHITENERS — same recipe, three jackets
- **no name Coffee Whitener 450 g** [OFF TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "Sugars (glucose), Coconut oil, Sodium caseinate (milk), Vegetable mono- and diglycerides, Dipotassium phosphate, Silicon dioxide, Soy lecithin, Natural flavour, Annatto." **French legal name on pack: "Colorant à café" — literally "coffee colouring."** On-camera gold. $5.00 No Frills / $5.49 Superstore.
- **Compliments Coffee Whitener 450 g** [OFFICIAL — compliments.ca]: "Sugars (glucose solids), coconut oil, sodium caseinate (milk), mono- and diglycerides, dipotassium phosphate, silicon dioxide, soya lecithin, natural flavour, annatto."
- **Great Value Coffee Whitener** [TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "Corn Syrup Solid, Hydrogenated Coconut Oil, Sodium Caseinate, Dipotassium Phosphate, Mono- and Diglycerides, Silicon Dioxide, Soy Lecithin, Artificial Flavour, Colour, Dimethylpolysiloxane." (Dimethylpolysiloxane callback to the VH soy-sauce label.)
- Pattern: first ingredient is always a sugar, second always a tropical oil, third the milk protein. NO PC-branded whitener found — verify in-store before claiming.

## 5. PLANT "CREAMERS" — and what Canada makes them call themselves
- **Silk vanilla oat** — Canadian legal name on shelf: **"Vanilla Oat-Based Coffee Enhancer"** (Metro listing; URL slug literally "vanilla-oat-coffee-whitener"). Ingredients [TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "Oat base (filtered water, gluten-free oat flour), Cane sugar, Vegetable oil, Pea protein, Potassium citrate, Baking soda, Gellan gum, Natural flavour, Sunflower lecithin." $5.49–6.49/890 mL. Owner: Danone Canada.
- **Earth's Own Oat Original Coffee Creamer** [OFFICIAL]: "Oat base (filtered water, gluten-free oats), Sunflower oil, Cane sugar, Potassium citrate, Sodium bicarbonate, Sea salt, Gellan gum, Locust bean gum, Sunflower lecithin, Amylase." 1 g sugars/15 mL. "Made in Canada."
- Fair-treatment note: these contain no dairy AND no dairy pretence beyond the category name — the beat is the legal naming ("enhancer"), not deception.

## 6. TIM HORTONS GROCERY CREAMERS — the pivot beat (real dairy, priciest in the aisle)
- Launched Aug 14, 2023 [OFFICIAL release]: Double Double™, Vanilla Dip Donut, Chocolate Dip Donut, 750 mL — "made with 100 per cent real Canadian dairy and do not contain artificial preservatives or added vegetable oils."
- Double Double creamer [OFF TRANSCRIPTION — re-read]: "milk, sugar, cream, sodium citrate, disodium phosphate, carrageenan, locust bean gum" — 40 cal, 4 g sugars/15 mL.
- Price: **$7.49/750 mL = $1.00/100 mL — the most expensive dairy in this dossier.** Real dairy, premium branding tax.
- DO NOT SAY: "Tims uses 18% cream in restaurants" (no official source — "Tim Hortons doesn't publicly specify"); who manufactures the grocery creamers (undisclosed — read the "prepared for" line on carton).

## 7. WINNERS — REAL CANADIAN CREAM
- **Dairyland 10% Creamo** (Saputo, Western Canada) [OFFICIAL]: complete ingredient list: **"Milk, Cream."** Two words. (A Save-On listing of a variant shows stabilizers — formats differ, read the exact carton.)
- **Natrel Fine-Filtered 10% Half & Half** (Agropur co-op) [OFFICIAL]: "Fine-filtered skim milk, Cream." Site claims: "no stabilizers," "only two ingredients." $5.99/1 L.
- **Lactantia 10% / 18%** (Lactalis Canada) [OFFICIAL]: "Milk, Cream, Sodium citrate, Sodium phosphate, Carrageenan, Locust bean gum." (Metro render adds DEXTROSE on the 10% — read carton; flag if present.) $4.99/1 L (10%); $5.70–5.99/1 L (18%).
- **Gay Lea 18% Table Cream** (Ontario–Manitoba farmer co-op, ~1,200 member farms, founded 1958) [OFFICIAL foodservice]: "Milk, cream, sodium citrate, sodium phosphate, carrageenan." "All Gay Lea Milk and Cream Products start with Canadian farm fresh milk."
- **The contrast: every real cream's first two ingredients are milk and cream. Every whitener's are a sugar and a vegetable oil.**

## 8. OWNERSHIP MAP (verified)
- Coffee-Mate = **Nestlé** (Swiss). International Delight = **Danone** (French) via WhiteWave, made by **Saputo**. Silk = Danone Canada. Carnation Canada = **Smucker** (US), Nestlé trademark licensed. Lactantia + Beatrice = **Lactalis** (French family-owned; Parmalat Canada renamed 2019). Natrel/Sealtest/Island Farms = **Agropur** (Quebec farmer co-op, ~2,700–2,900 member farms; Sealtest trademark owned by Unilever, licensed). Gay Lea = Ontario/Manitoba farmer co-op. Dairyland = **Saputo** (Montreal, via 2001 Dairyworld acquisition).
- TWIST BEAT: **Saputo makes both the biggest fake cream (International Delight) and Western Canada's realest cream (Dairyland "Milk, Cream").** Same company, both sides of the aisle — the hot-dog "one company, different fonts" beat again.
