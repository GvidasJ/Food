## PART B — BRAND HISTORY DEEP DIVES

### B1. SCHNEIDERS

**Founding (Berlin, Ontario):** Maple Leaf's own history page (company self-published) tells the story: J.M. (John Metz) Schneider, after injuring his hand at a button factory, made sausage using "his mother's German sausage recipe" and began "selling it door to door" — brand founded 1890, in Kitchener, Ontario, "formerly known as Berlin." — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/stories/schneiders-canada-history/
Corroboration: Wikipedia's Maple Leaf entry: "In 2003, the company purchased rival meat packer Schneider Foods (founded by John Metz Schneider in 1890 in Berlin)." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Leaf_Foods
Schneiders' own site: "J.M.'s first satisfied customers were his own neighbours. He began selling sausages, made to his family's recipes, out of his home on Courtland Ave." — https://schneiders.ca/our-story/

**Berlin→Kitchener rename (the city, not the company):** "Berlin, which was not on the ballot, disappeared from the map on 1 September 1916" after a referendum; the new name honoured Lord Kitchener, "killed in early June en route to Russia when his warship struck a German mine"; anti-German unrest included a bust of Kaiser Wilhelm I thrown into Victoria Lake — Canadian War Museum — https://www.warmuseum.ca/firstworldwar/history/life-at-home-during-the-war/enemy-aliens/anti-german-sentiment/ ; centenary coverage: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/kitchener-waterloo/kitchener-ontario-berlin-name-change-100-years-tom-reitz-berry-vrbanovic-1.3744212 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-to-Kitchener_name_change

**Dutch Girl:** per Maple Leaf's history page, the Dutch Girl originally appeared "alongside a Dutch Boy too. When government labeling requirements came into effect," the boy was dropped for space; the Dutch Girl was "trademarked in 1955." Orange-and-blue colours came from rust-inhibiting paint: an engineer suggested painting "the tanks a bright orange colour and added the Schneiders name in blue as advertising." — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/stories/schneiders-canada-history/
(A CTV Kitchener piece exists titled "The iconic 'Dutch Girl' face behind the Schneiders brand," noting "85-year-old Nancy Ann Featherstone-Maclean is the face of Schneiders meats" — full text unfetchable; see UNVERIFIED for the "since 1936" claim.) — https://www.ctvnews.ca/kitchener/article/the-iconic-dutch-girl-face-behind-the-schneiders-brand

**"Famous for Quality" + the 401 sign:** the famous Highway 401 sign was erected 1961 and rebuilt 2013; the slogan "Famous for Quality" "remains the same" on the sign — Maple Leaf history page (self-published) — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/stories/schneiders-canada-history/
Other verified milestones from same page: 1925 "a new plant on Courtland Ave. in Kitchener"; 1927 first company picnic; 2015 Maple Leaf "donated 250 Schneiders artifacts to the Waterloo Region Museum."

**The 1998 takeover battle:** The Globe and Mail: "Smithfield -- the world's largest pork and hog producer -- won Schneider in 1998 after a drawn-out battle sparked by Maple Leaf when it made a hostile $130-million bid for the 107-year-old specialty meats producer." Smithfield "ended up winning the company for $144-million." — https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/maple-leaf-buys-schneider/article18431413/
Family control detail (Ontario court record, Pente v. Schneider): Schneider was "a public corporation controlled by the Schneider Family through its ownership of 70.5% of Schneider voting common shares," while equity was "77% owned by nonFamily members through their ownership of nonvoting A shares"; the family rejected Maple Leaf's offer but accepted Smithfield's, and Justice Farley's ruling (upheld on appeal) found the board acted reasonably — https://ca.vlex.com/vid/pente-inv-mgt-v-680558033
Maple Leaf's opening bid was $19.00/share announced Nov 5, 1997, later raised to $22 (Smithfield SEC filings/search-corroborated; primary: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000091388/000091664198000850/0000916641-98-000850.txt).

**2003–04: Maple Leaf finally gets Schneiders:** Smithfield 8-K: sale of "Schneider Corporation, to Maple Leaf Foods, Inc. for approximately $378 million, subject to closing adjustments, including the assumption of the company's outstanding debt," closed April 5, 2004 (agreement announced Sept 25, 2003); Schneider described as "one of Canada's largest producers of premium-branded food products… about 5,000 people at 20 facilities." — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000091388/000119312504065628/dex991.htm
The Globe reported it as "a $413-million deal to buy the venerable packaged meat company" (CAD figure vs Smithfield's ~US$378M — use one currency, flag the other). Michael McCain's money quotes: "Welcome back to Canada, Schneiders" and the deal is "somewhat a repatriation of a great Canadian organization." — https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/maple-leaf-buys-schneider/article18431413/

**Kitchener plant closure:** Maple Leaf news release: the Courtland Ave. facility, "originally constructed in 1924 and owned by Schneider Foods," closed **February 27, 2015**; production moved "primarily to a new, state-of-the-art 400,000 square foot prepared meats plant in Hamilton, Ontario." McCain: "We are well along with transitioning production to our new, state-of-the-art facility in Hamilton…" — https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/maple-leaf-closes-largest-legacy-plant-in-kitchener-ontario-516978431.html

### B2. MAPLE LEAF FOODS LINEAGE

**William Davies Company / Hogtown:** "The William Davies Company was a pork processing and packing company in Toronto… founded circa 1854 by William Davies… At one time, it was the largest pork packer in the British Empire" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Davies_Company. Davies "set up a stall in Toronto's St. Lawrence Market, where he sold cured hams and bacon" after emigrating in 1854 (Dictionary of Canadian Biography) — https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/davies_william_15E.html
Hogtown, verbatim (Flavour Network): "By the early 1900s, with the help of business partner Joseph Flavelle, Davies had built what was believed to be the largest pork plant in the British Empire, processing nearly half a million hogs a year at his Front Street plant near the mouth of the Don River and earning Toronto its nickname: Hogtown." — https://www.flavournetwork.ca/article/the-history-of-canadian-peameal-bacon/

**Canada Packers, 1927:** "Canada Packers was founded in 1927 as a merger of several major Toronto meat packers, most prominently William Davies Company," and "was immediately Canada's largest food processor, a title it would hold for the next sixty years." York brand: by 1950 its diversified foods were "marketed under the York brand." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Packers

**1991 merger / Hillsdown:** Maple Leaf's own timeline: "Maple Leaf Foods Inc. is created through the merger of Maple Leaf Mills Limited and Canada Packers Inc." (1991). Also: origins "trace back more than 180 years to Grantham Mills, built in 1836 in St. Catharines, Ontario" and "The Maple Leaf brand first appeared in 1898." — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/about/history/

**1995 McCain/Teachers:** Maple Leaf timeline, 1995: "McCain Capital Corporation and the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan Board acquire controlling interest in Maple Leaf Foods from Hillsdown Holdings plc." — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/about/history/ (Wallace McCain's backstory — expelled from joint command of McCain Foods, then led the leveraged bid — via https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/maple-leaf-foods-inc)

**Michael McCain era:** CEO for 25 years; in May 2023 "Curtis Frank" was appointed "President and Chief Executive Officer" as McCain moved to Executive Chair — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/news/curtis-frank-appointed-president-and-ceo-of-maple-leaf-foods/ ; https://canadiangrocer.com/maple-leaf-foods-ceo-michael-mccain-stepping-down
2008 listeria: outbreak from a Toronto plant killed 22–23 (sources differ: Wikipedia "would kill 22 people"; CBC-era reporting says 23) and prompted McCain's famous televised apology; one-year-anniversary newspaper ads promised "becoming a global leader in food safety… On behalf of our 24,000 employees, we promise to never forget." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Leaf_Foods ; https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/how-maple-leaf-foods-is-handling-the-listeria-outbreak-1.763404 ; https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/small-business/sb-growth/the-best-legal-advice-is-often-an-apology/article626797/

**CANADA PACKERS IS BACK (verified, hard):** Maple Leaf completed the spin-off of its pork business as **Canada Packers Inc.** on **October 1, 2025**; shares began trading on the TSX **October 2, 2025 under "CPKR."** Michael McCain (Executive Chair of both) called it "an exciting, and historic moment for Canadian business." — https://www.mapleleaffoods.com/news/maple-leaf-foods-inc-completes-spin-off-of-canada-packers-inc/ ; also https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/maple-leaf-foods-inc-completes-spin-off-of-canada-packers-inc--832846042.html and https://panow.com/2025/10/03/maple-leaf-foods-completes-pork-spinoff-of-canada-packers/ (Brandon MB + Lethbridge AB plants, ~3,700 employees, capacity near five million pigs/year). Note: sources differ on Maple Leaf's retained stake (19.9% vs 16% post-closing) — check the final release before scripting a number.

### B5. DISAPPEARED MEAT BRANDS (nostalgia beat)

- **Burns Meats (Calgary):** Patrick Burns established a Calgary slaughterhouse in 1890 (Provincial Archives of Alberta) — https://searchprovincialarchives.alberta.ca/burns-foods-2 .
