# PART 2 — KETCHUP LAW, THE SAGA TIMELINE & ECONOMICS
### (Verbatim from CFCS/CSI/FDR/CFIA/StatCan/CBC archives, retrieved Sept 1, 2026)

## 2.1 THE LEGAL KETCHUP — CFCS Vol. 10, s. 10.2.9 + CSI Vol. 4, s. 38
- CFCS 10.2.9 verbatim: "**Tomato Catsup, Catsup** or products whose common names are variants of the word Catsup, is the product made from the juice of red-ripe tomatoes or sound tomato trimmings from which skins and seeds have been removed; (a) contains (i) vinegar, (ii) salt, (iii) seasoning, and **(iv) a sweetening ingredient**; and (b) contains mould filaments in not more than 50% of the microscopic fields (official method MFO-5…)."
- **THREE BEATS: (1) SUGAR IS LEGALLY MANDATORY** — sugar-free "ketchup" can't meet the standard (French's stevia product is named "No Sugar Added Ketchup TYPE SAUCE"). **(2) No minimum tomato solids for ketchup** (paste needs 20%, puree/pulp 12% — ketchup: none). **(3) There is a legal mould-filament limit** — a federal lab method for counting mould in ketchup exists (Howard mold count, MFO-5, dated Nov 30, 1981).
- CSI Vol. 4 s. 38 verbatim: "Tomato Catsup, Catsup, Tomato Ketchup or Ketchup is the food that meets the standard set out in section 10.2.9… except that it **may contain (a) tomato paste; (b) water**…" — the carve-out that legalizes modern paste+water remanufactured ketchup (both big brands list tomato paste first).
- Old FDR B.11.014 repealed by SOR/2024-244 s. 68; survivor: **B.11.012** "No person shall sell tomato catsup unless it has been obtained by heat processing…"
- Name protection: SFCR s. 201 — bold standardized common names must meet the standard in interprovincial trade/import/export. Quebec "fruit ketchup" legality = OPEN QUESTION — never assert either way.

## 2.2 GRADES — "Canada Fancy" decoded
- Grade Compendium Vol. 3 s. 54: ketchup MAY be graded (voluntary), using tomato-puree grades (s. 51): "**Canada Fancy** … (a) possesses a typical tomato puree flavour of well-ripened tomatoes; (b) possesses a good red, ripe tomato colour; and (c) is practically free from skin, seed particles, black specks, core material and other defects." Canada Choice = "fairly good" everything.
- CFIA verbatim: "Grading is optional for… tomato ketchup…" — **the juice can carries "Canada Fancy"; the ketchup bottle almost never does because it doesn't have to.** ("Fancy" on old labels = this legal grade vocabulary.)

## 2.3 FOP SYMBOLS — the escape twist (OUR COMPUTATION)
- RA for ketchup = **15 mL** (TRA item R.5, major condiments). RA ≤ 30 mL ⇒ 10% DV triggers: **10 g sugars / 230 mg sodium per 15 mL**.
- Heinz Canada label (QC registry): per 15 mL — 20 Cal, **4 g sugars (4% DV), 140 mg sodium (6% DV)**. French's: 4 g sugars, 170 mg (~7%). **NEITHER triggers any symbol.**
- THE HONEST TWIST: ketchup is roughly a quarter sugar by volume (~27 g/100 mL) — caught by the stricter small-RA column and still sails under, because a food judged in tablespoons almost cannot be "high in" anything. (If ketchup had pasta sauce's 125 mL RA, ~33 g sugars/RA would blow past every threshold.) Frame as how the math works, never as scandal; use 140 mg NOT 160 (older listings).
- Compliance: "no enforcement discretion after January 1, 2026"; pre-2026 stock may remain on shelves.

## 2.4 SUGARS ON THE LABEL — glucose-fructose & the grouping exemption
- Both big brands list ONE sugars-based ingredient, "liquid sugar" — B.01.008.3(4)(d) exemption (single ingredient containing the word "sugar") means no "Sugars (…)" bracket required. A ketchup using sugar + glucose-fructose would need "Sugars (sugar, glucose-fructose)."
- **"Glucose-fructose" is Canada's label name for high-fructose corn syrup** — the decode beat for any store brand that lists it (verify per label in Part 1). US bottles say HFCS; Canadian bottles say glucose-fructose; most Canadians never connected the words.

## 2.5 THE SAGA — precisely dated, quote bank (all CBC-verbatim unless noted)
- **1909**: Heinz opens Leamington — first Canadian plant. WWI: Heinz beans fed Canadian troops; 1917 soldier letter story (town historian Scott Holland; centennial history book exists).
- **2013**: Berkshire Hathaway + 3G buy Heinz (US$23.3B). **Nov 14, 2013**: closure announced — Leamington 740 employees (with seasonal, closer to 1,000 — "740 jobs, and closer to 1,000 people including seasonal workers, per CBC"). Warren Buffett, Nov 2013: "It's really a question of having an unprofitable plant and concentrating production in a more profitable plant. You see that in the auto industry."
- **June 27, 2014**: final day. Severance: two weeks/year + $2,500 bonus + 52 weeks benefits; $1.8M paid to 43 farmers. Scale: "Close to 40 per cent of all field tomatoes grown in Ontario were shipped to Leamington in any given year" (~225,000 tons/yr).
- **Same day**: **Highbury Canco** (consortium led by Toronto businessman Pradeep Sood) takes over the plant — co-packing, at least 250 jobs, NO ketchup. By 2022: **630 employees**, 26 growers, and "It now produces more tomato paste than the old Heinz plant produced ketchup" (CBC).
- **Dec 2015**: French's launches ketchup in Canada "pledging to make ketchup with Leamington tomatoes"; president Elliott Penner promises Leamington-only tomatoes (Jan 2016).
- **Late Feb 2016**: Brian Fernandez (construction worker, Orillia) Facebook post — verbatim as CBC quoted: "Since Heinz decided to pull the plug on its Canadian plant in Leamington, 740 jobs were lost… Then, French's (known for its mustard) stepped in… We bought a bottle. - **Feb 29, 2016**: French's triples Leamington paste order. **Mar 3, 2016**: MPP Taras Natyshak petitions Queen's Park to serve only French's — and later wins the legislature-cafeteria campaign. His quote: "Leamington is the tomato capital of Canada. Ketchup is the iconic product… immortalized in a Stompin' Tom Connors song. It is quintessential Canadiana." - **Jan 2025**: Trudeau urges buying Canadian, citing "Heinz's ketchup being replaced by French's ketchup because French's was still using Canadian tomatoes." **Kraft Heinz hits back** — calls it misleading; verbatim: "HEINZ Ketchup is made in Canada, by Canadians, using Canadian tomatoes" — and runs a full-page Globe and Mail ad.
- **The wrinkle (CBC Jan 29, 2025)**: readers kept finding "Product of USA" Heinz bottles. ## 2.8 ORIGIN-LINE DECODER (CFIA verbatim)
- **"Product of Canada"**: "all or virtually all major ingredients, processing, and labour… are Canadian" — non-Canadian content "less than a total of 2%" (spices, additives, vitamins, cane sugar…). "The claim 'Canadian' is considered to be the same as a 'Product of Canada' claim."
- **"Made in Canada"** requires a qualifier: "'Made in Canada from domestic and imported ingredients'… regardless of the level of Canadian content"; "'Proudly Made in Canada' would need a qualifying statement…"
- **"Prepared in Canada"** = truthful processing claim (Heinz's choice). The decode: three different bottles can say Product of USA / Prepared in Canada / Made in Canada from… — and the fine print is the only referee.
- Bonus: legal meaning of "local" = same province or within 50 km across a border.
