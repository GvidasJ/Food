## 1. THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

### Where compositional standards live now
**SOR/2024-244**, Canada Gazette Part II, Vol. 158 No. 26, **December 18 2024** — moved compositional standards out of Part B of the *Food and Drug Regulations* into the **Canadian Food Compositional Standards (CFCS)** document, incorporated by reference, organized in **19 volumes**: 1 Miscellaneous · 2 Alcoholic Beverages · 3 Baking Powder · 4 Cocoa and Chocolate · 5 Coffee · 6 Spices, Seasonings and Dressings · 7 Dairy · 8 Fats and Oils · 9 Flavouring Preparations · 10 Fruits, Vegetables and Substitutes · 11 Prepackaged Water · 12 Grain and Bakery · **13 Meat, Its Preparations and Products** · 14 Salt · 15 Sweetening Agents · 16 Vinegar · 17 Tea · 18 Marine and Fresh Water Animal Products · 19 Poultry.

**Worked example — CFCS 7.6.1 verbatim:** *"Ice Cream is the frozen food obtained by freezing an ice cream mix… (a) contains not less than (i) 36% solids, (ii) 10% milk fat, or, where cocoa or chocolate syrup, fruit, nuts, or confections have been added, 8% milk fat, and (iii) 180 g/L of solids of which amount not less than 50 g is milk fat…"*

**The mechanism:** §7.6 contains standards only for Ice Cream, Ice Milk and Sherbet. There is **no standard for "frozen dessert."** The absence of a standard is what makes that name available.

### Common name
Definition [B.01.001(1) FDR; s.1 SFCR]: the boldface, non-italic name in the CFCS or the *Canadian Standards of Identity*; otherwise *"the name by which it is generally known or a name that is not generic and that describes the food."*

CFIA: *"A food that does not meet the requirements of a standard cannot use the prescribed name for that standardized food."* … *"A modified standardized common name or another name that accurately describes the food may be used"* — but *"it must always be clear to consumers that the food so described does not meet the standard"* and *"the consumer is told, in all respects… the way in which the food does not meet the standard."*

⚠️ **"Style / product / beverage" are NOT CFIA-defined categories.** Only **"simulated"** is mandated by regulation; only **"flavoured"** is recommended by guidance. Frame the rest as industry practice responding to the loss-of-name rule.

### Simulated meat — the declaration
[B.01.100(1) FDR] the common name is the simulated product's name *"modified by the word 'simulated'"*, in letters at least the same size and prominence as the rest of the name. [B.01.100(4)] the declaration **"contains no meat"** or **"contains no poultry"** must appear on the principal display panel *"in close proximity to the common name and in letters of at least the same size and prominence."*

### Ingredient order
[B.01.008.2(3)(a) FDR] *"Ingredients must be declared… in **descending** order of their proportion by weight… The order must be the order of the ingredients **before they are combined** to form the prepackaged product."* — the "before they are combined" clause is why sugar can be split into sugar / glucose-fructose / cane syrup and each fall below flour.

### Highlighted ingredients — CFIA's three named failure modes (verbatim)
- **Substitution** — *"occurs when an imitation ingredient is present in a food to replace a genuine ingredient."*
- **Over-Emphasis** — *"occurs when a highlighted ingredient is either present at very low level or at least partially replaced by a similar ingredient… giving an erroneous impression the highlighted ingredient is present in a higher amount than it really is."*
- **Misrepresentation of flavours** — *"occurs when flavours or very small amounts of ingredients are added for the purpose of flavouring but representation… creates an impression that either the actual ingredient was added (rather than a flavour) or there is more of the ingredient in the food than is actually the case."*

**The "flavoured" rule:** *"When the highlighted ingredient is present at very low levels or as a flavour preparation, the word 'flavoured' or equivalent should be displayed adjacent or within the highlighting."*

**CFIA's own substitution table** (excellent on-screen graphic):
| Highlighted | CFIA-listed "similar ingredients" |
|---|---|
| Butter | *"Margarine, vegetable oil, lard, shortening, ghee, etc."* |
| Chocolate | *"Cocoa, carob, etc."* |
| **Beef** | *"Pork, chicken, extended meats, simulated meats"* |
| Mozzarella cheese | *"Cheddar and other varietal cheeses, cheese-like dairy products, vegetable-based loaves… cheese powder"* |
| Whole grains | *"Non-whole grain such as bran, flour, starch or maltodextrin"* |

**Imitation table:** Chocolate chips → *"Carob chips, compound chips, **cocoa flavoured palm oil chips**"*; Crab → *"Kamaboko"*; Peach pieces → *"Composite product (consisting of sugar, flavour, starch and gelling agents), peach colored… fruit/vegetable bits (other than peach)."*

### MANDATORY vs NOT — the fairness distinction
**Mandatory:** deviating from a standard forfeits the prescribed name [B.01.001(1)] · simulated meat declaration [B.01.100(1),(4)] · imitation-flavour statement where a vignette depicts the natural substance [**224(1) SFCR**] · organic ≥95% + named accredited certifier [353(1), 354 SFCR] · descending ingredient order [B.01.008.2(3)(a)] · mixed nuts % of predominant nut [B.01.071].

**NOT mandatory:** ⚠️ **percentage of a highlighted ingredient.** CFIA's own words: *"there is no broadly applied requirement for labels to declare the quantity of a characterizing ingredient that is emphasized on the label."* It was a *proposed* amendment (Canada Gazette Part I, June 22 2019). **Do not tell viewers this is law.** · **"Made with real X"** — permitted *"if the named ingredient is present in the food, regardless of what form."* No minimum. · **"Product of Canada"/"Made in Canada"** — guidelines, voluntary once chosen. · **"Local"** — policy only: within the province, or within 50 km across a provincial border.

**Vignette rule:** pictures *"must not be deceptive, misleading or misrepresent the composition, qualities or value of a product."* CFIA's own example: a juice of apple juice, orange juice, sugar and flavour sold as "tropical juice" with coconut/mango/pineapple/papaya images violates the rules.

### "Modified milk ingredients" → "milk-derived ingredients" — ALREADY DONE
CFIA notice **February 11 2026**. English "modified milk ingredients" → **"milk-derived ingredients"**; French "substances laitières modifiées" → "ingrédients dérivés du lait". **Mandatory compliance January 1 2030**; either name accepted during transition.

Old definitions verbatim: *"**Milk ingredients** — …any component of milk whose chemical composition has not been altered and that exists in the food in the same chemical state in which it is found in milk."* / *"**Modified milk ingredients** — …any component of milk whose chemical state has been altered from what is found in milk."* (casein, caseinates, whey, ultrafiltered milk, milk serum proteins…)

## 5. BREAD PRICE-FIXING — who admitted what
- Alleged conspiracy **2001–2015**, packaged bread. Bureau began investigating **January 2016**.
- Weston Foods and Loblaw *"had previously admitted their participation in an 'industry-wide price-fixing arrangement' and received **immunity from prosecution** in exchange for co-operating."* Self-reported 2015; not public until 2017.
- Bureau alleged **"at least $1.50 was added to the price of a loaf of bread."**
- **$25 gift card program** ran 2018–2019, credited at **$96 million**.
- **$500M settlement** = George Weston $247.5M cash + Loblaw $252.5M ($156.5M cash + $96M gift-card credit). Announced July 25 2024, **approved by Justice Ed Morgan May 7 2025** — *"an excellent and fair result for all concerned."* 78% to Ontario. Buyers Jan 2001–Dec 2021 who didn't take a gift card get **up to $25**. Four objections, 475 opt-outs out of *"the estimated 20 million-plus class members."*
- Galen Weston: *"This behaviour should never have happened… Reaching a settlement on this matter was the right thing to do in response to previous behaviour that did not meet our values and ethical standards."*

**⚠️ FAIRNESS — WHO DENIES:** Canada Bread was fined **$50M in June 2023** (highest price-fixing fine in Canadian history) but **denied** a wide-ranging conspiracy in its Oct 2023 defence. **Metro** cross-claimed that Loblaw and George Weston **conspired to implicate it**. **Sobeys** says it was falsely implicated. **Walmart Canada** and **Giant Tiger** deny involvement. > *"**Water, Wild Alaska Pollock, Wild Pacific Whiting, Corn starch, Liquid egg-white, Sugar, Sorbitol, Tapioca starch, Sea salt,** Mirin wine…, Natural flavour, Potassium chloride, Sodium phosphates, Konjac flour, **Crab meat**, Alaska pollock oil, Carrageenan, Canthaxanthin, Titanium dioxide"*

**no name Original Hot Dogs 450 g:** *"**Pork, Mechanically separated chicken, Water, Modified corn starch, Enriched wheat flour,** Salt, Sugars…, **Wheat gluten, Beef,** …"* — **mechanically separated chicken is #2**, then three fillers, and **beef is ingredient #9, behind wheat gluten.**

**no name Bologna, Sliced 500 g — $5.00:** **Mechanically separated chicken is #1** in a product most shoppers read as a pork/beef deli meat.

**no name Beef Burgers, Club Pack 2.27 kg:** *"**Beef, Water, Toasted wheat crumbs, Textured soy flour, Salt,** Sugars…, **Hydrolyzed soy protein,** Spice, Onion extract"* — two of the top four ingredients are **filler** per FDR B.14.001. Because filler is present, the product need only hit **13% protein** instead of the **16%** required of a plain seasoned ground-beef patty. **The word "Burgers" — not "hamburger," not "ground beef" — is what makes that legal.**
**Contrast:** PC Thick & Juicy Prime Rib Burgers = *"Beef, Water, Salt, Seasonings"* — still water-extended, but no soy/wheat filler.

**no name Whipped Topping 1 L:** *"**Water, Sugars (corn syrup, glucose-fructose), Modified palm kernel oil, Modified milk ingredients,** Polysorbate 60…"* — **no cream, no milk fat.** Sits in the dairy case beside real whipping cream.

**PC Lemon Iced Tea Flavoured Beverage:** *"Water, Sugars (glucose-fructose), natural flavour, citric acid, potassium citrate, sodium phosphate, **caramel colour**…"* — **there is no tea in the iced tea.** Not tea extract, not instant tea, not tea solids. "Flavoured Beverage" is the legally required admission.

**no name Chocolately Chip Cookies 907 g** (Loblaw's own listing misspells it): chips are *"sugar, palm kernel oil, cocoa, dextrose, modified milk ingredients, soy lecithin"* — **palm kernel oil and cocoa powder, but no cocoa butter.** Fails B.04.006, so the word "chocolate" is forbidden → **"chocolatey."** Note **sugar is the first ingredient of the chip itself.**

**no name Grated Cheese Product with Parmesan 250 g:** *"PASTEURIZED MILK, MODIFIED MILK INGREDIENTS, SALT, LIPASE, CALCIUM CHLORIDE, BACTERIAL CULTURE, MICROBIAL ENZYME, **CELLULOSE**"* — a "cheese **product**… **with** parmesan," and **cellulose (wood pulp) is a declared ingredient right on a Canadian label.** No lawsuit required. ⚠️ direct loblaws.ca URL not located — get a shelf photo.

**no name Crispy Southern-Style Chicken 1 kg — ~$18.99:** *"**Chicken (breasts, thighs, drumsticks), Water,** Enriched wheat flour…"* — **water is #2**, and the page's own spec sheet declares **"16% meat protein."** "Southern-***Style***" is a non-standardized descriptor doing all the work.

**no name Fruit Punch Drink Mix:** **"Fruit" Punch contains no fruit** — the red is beet and black carrot. Legally a "Drink Mix," never a juice. Ironic bonus: the tub carries Loblaw's **"Simple Check"** seal ("made without 10 ingredients such as synthetic colours, artificial flavours…"). All true. It's still sugar with acid in it. ⚠️ two formulations in market.

**no name Beef / Chicken Flavour Instant Noodle Soup:** the label says **"Artificial beef flavour"** outright — **zero beef, zero chicken.** Brown comes from caramel colour. ⚠️ current Loblaw URLs not located.

**PC Jamaican-Style Ginger Beer:** *"Carbonated water, Sugars…, **Natural flavour**, Citric acid, Sodium benzoate, **Caramel colour**, Gum arabic"* — **no ginger.** ⚠️ Loblaw URL not located.
