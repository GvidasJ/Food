## 1. THE POETIC SPINE (cold open — verified)
**The irony holds, and it's better than assumed.** George Weston's empire didn't just start with bread in 1882 — the company admitted its role in fixing the price of bread 135 years later (Dec 19, 2017), and then sold the entire bakery business the founder built. The empire kept the groceries and let go of the bread.

### George Weston's start — verified
- Born "23 March 1865 in Oswego, N.Y." — Dictionary of Canadian Biography (https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/weston_george_15E.html). CAUTION: Ontario Heritage Trust plaque says 1864. Avoid stating a year or attribute; DCB (1865) is the scholarly source.
- Age-12 detail is the APPRENTICESHIP, not the route purchase: "At age 12 he found employment in the Yonge Street bakery of Charles J. Frogley, where he learned the physically demanding craft of bread-making." — DCB
- 1882: "In 1882, at 17, Weston purchased two bread routes from Bowen." — DCB. "George Weston, a Toronto bread salesman and former baker's apprentice, went into business by purchasing a bread route from his boss." — The Canadian Encyclopedia (https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/george-weston-limited). Route count differs (one vs two). SAFE VO: "In 1882, a teenaged bread deliveryman named George Weston bought his boss's bread route." Employer: Gilbert H. Bowen.
- Model Bakery 1897: "In 1897 he opened what became known as the Model Bakery on nearby Soho Street" (DCB); "With a staff of 40 and a weekly payroll of roughly $350, it produced 3,200 loaves daily." (DCB)
- "By the turn of the century, George Weston had become Canada's biggest baker." — TCE
- Co-founded Canada Bread Company in 1911 (Heritage Trust) — THE SAME Canada Bread fined $50M in 2023 (then under different ownership). Second layer of irony.
- Died April 6, 1924, home on Palmerston Boulevard (DCB). Provincial plaque at 18 Beverley St., Toronto.

### Succession — verified (TCE)
George → W. Garfield (1924) → W. Galen (CEO of Loblaw 1972) → Galen G. Weston (executive chairman 2006). Four generations.

### Weston buys Loblaw — verified
"In 1947, baking company George Weston Ltd., led by W. Garfield Weston, began purchasing shares in Loblaw Groceterias. Weston gained a controlling interest by 1953." — TCE Loblaw article. SAFE VO: "started buying in 1947, controlled it by 1953" — never "bought Loblaw in 1947."

### The bread admission — payoff quotes
- "On 19 December 2017, George Weston Ltd. and Loblaw Companies Ltd. revealed that both companies had participated in a bread price fixing scheme between 2001 and 2015." — TCE
- "This sort of behaviour is wrong and has no place in our business or Canada's grocery industry." — Galen G. Weston, Dec 2017 (CBC: https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/bread-price-fixing-loblaw-george-weston-sobeys-1.4463060)
- On the $25 card: "The gift card is a direct acknowledgment of that to our customers. We hope that they'll see it as a meaningful amount that demonstrates our commitment to keeping their trust and confidence." — Galen G. Weston (CBC)
- $500M settlement CBC headline: "Loblaw, George Weston to pay $500M for bread price-fixing scheme in record antitrust settlement" (https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/loblaw-bread-price-settlement-1.7274820)

### Bonus irony — Weston exited bread entirely
Weston Foods (Wonder, ACE Bakery, Country Harvest, D'Italiano, Casa Mendosa, Gadoua) sold to FGF Brands, announced Oct 26, 2021, closed Dec 10, 2021; with the ambient business sale, entire bakery went for ~C$1.47 billion (George Weston release: https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/george-weston-limited-announces-the-sale-of-its-weston-foods-fresh-and-frozen-businesses-811633272.html). Cold-open kicker: "The company that began with one man's bread route no longer bakes bread at all. It just sells it to you."

## 2. LOBLAW ORIGINS — Theodore Pringle Loblaw (verified)
- 1919 first self-serve "groceteria": "He and business partner John Milton Cork opened the first Loblaw Groceterias store in Toronto's Junction neighbourhood in 1919." — TCE. "Customers could pick their own packaged foods at their own pace rather wait for a clerk." — TCE
- First store: 2923 Dundas St. W., Toronto, June 1919; second at 528 College St. (Toronto Journey 416: https://www.torontojourney416.com/loblaw-groceterias/). "Groceterias" from "cafeteria."
- Alliston heritage plaque (https://www.ontarioplaques.com/Plaques/Plaque_Simcoe40.html): orphaned in his teens, raised by grandparents; "At age 17 he moved to Toronto with twenty dollars and a dream"; donated to Toronto Western Hospital and Stevenson Memorial; Canadian Business Hall of Fame 1999. (Plaque's "J. Milton Cook" is a typo — use CORK.)
- Died 1933 (1872-1933); "When Theodore Pringle Loblaw died in 1933, John Milton Cork became president." — TCE
- Narrative beat: Loblaw the man died 14 years before Weston money arrived — the "Loblaws" Canadians boycott has had no Loblaw in it since 1933.

## 3. HOUSE-BRAND HERITAGE (the over-35 beats, verified)
- **No Name, March 21, 1978**: launched with 16 generic items in black-and-yellow (Tedium: https://tedium.co/2020/12/11/canada-generic-products-no-name/; count confirmed by Canadian Grocer). Dave Nichol launched it during a March 1978 price war, inspired by Carrefour's "Produits Libres". "Within three weeks of launching, the line sold as much as Loblaws expected to sell in a year" (Tedium). Savings pitch at launch: "between 10 and 40 percent over national brands."
- **Insider's Report, November 1983**: "combining zaniness and food tips in a comic book format," modeled on Trader Joe's-style flyers (Wikipedia President's Choice); "reflected his belief that success required not just great products, but great stories as well" — Globe and Mail obit of Dave Nichol (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/veteran-loblaws-pitchman-dave-nichol-dies-at-age-73/article14539628/)
- **President's Choice, 1984**: "To appeal to customers who wanted higher-quality products at affordable prices, the President's Choice line was introduced in 1984." — TCE. Evolved from 1983 "President's Blend" coffee.
- **The Decadent Chocolate Chip Cookie, 1988**: "contained 39% chocolate chips by weight, more than double the leading national brand. It took supplier Colonial Cookie of Kitchener, Ontario, more than a year to create a product that met Nichol's standards" (Wikipedia PC). Best-seller claim SAFE VERSION: "within three years [it] was Canada's best-selling cookie, even though it was available in only 20% of Canadian food stores."
- **PC Cola, 1991**: Cott launched PC Cola; Nichol demanded a cola 30% below Coke/Pepsi prices; "Loblaws' private label placed Cott on the map and contributed to Canadian Coca-Cola Limited's C$45 million loss one year later." (Encyclopedia.com Cott)
- **Nichol left 1994; died Sept 22, 2013, age 73**, Toronto Western Hospital (CBC). Quotes for the close: "Dave's passion for food and his vision helped to transform the way Canadians eat" — Galen Weston Sr. (Globe obit); he "attained the status of a cult hero and taste arbiter to the nation" — Anne Kingston (Globe obit).
- Script irony: the man whose persona was fighting Big Brands on price built the store brands for the company later caught fixing the price of bread.

## 1. THE BOYCOTT (May 2024)
### Founding and growth
- r/loblawsisoutofcontrol founded November 2023 by Emily Johnson, an Ontario mental health and addictions worker, later joined by eight organizers across Canada (blogTO: https://www.blogto.com/eat_drink/2024/05/loblaws-boycott-organizer-met-ceo/; The Hoser: https://www.thehoser.ca/posts/understanding-the-loblaw-boycott).
- Member counts (dated): ~45,000 (Apr 2024) → ~56,000 on May 1, 2024 launch day (CBC) → ~77,000 at mid-May ("up from 56,000 on May 1" — Yahoo Finance) → 84,000+ late May (blogTO). NO reliable 2025-26 count — screenshot the live counter on filming day with a date stamp.
### Launch and demands
- Boycott of all Loblaw banners began May 1, 2024 — the same day Loblaw reported Q1 2024 revenue of $13.58 billion, up 4.5% (CBC).
- Demands as reported: sign the Grocery Code of Conduct; cut prices 15%; price caps on essentials; no more 2024 price increases; no dividend increases; end member-only pricing; shrinkflation transparency (CBC, Globe, The Hoser).
- Loblaw spokesperson at launch: "The last few years have been tough for Canadians, and we continue to do what we can to combat inflation at our stores."
- Poll (May 2024, National Observer): 58% of Canadians supported the boycott; only 18% said their household joined.
### Executive responses — VERBATIM (attribution is critical)
- Galen G. Weston, Loblaw annual meeting, May 2, 2024: "As a well-known company and Canada's largest grocer, it is natural that Loblaw would be singled out as a focal point for media and government and of course consumer frustrations." He called the criticism "misguided" — THE "MISGUIDED" QUOTE IS WESTON'S, NOT PER BANK'S.
- Weston, same meeting: "There's a group of people who have been relentlessly propagating a narrative that they know is false. And it is now being used to justify theft on a grand scale." MANDATORY CONTEXT: he was referring to shoplifting; he also argued the demanded 15% cut equated to roughly three times annual earnings. (Global News May 2, 2024: https://globalnews.ca/news/10465250/loblaw-boycott-leaders-criticism-reaction/; BNN/CP.)
- Per Bank, same meeting: "Let me assure you that in every corner of the business, our colleagues are working hard to reduce costs and do things more efficiently."
- Bank (April 2024, internal note via Global News): called boycott plans a response from "deeply unhappy" customers and blamed online "misinformation" (https://globalnews.ca/news/10456810/loblaw-may-boycott-per-bank/).
- Emily Johnson met CEO Per Bank on May 2, 2024 (blogTO; CP).
### Measurable effect — Loblaw's own words (Q2 2024, July 25, 2024)
- Revenue $13,947M (+1.5%); net earnings available to common $457M (−10.0%), decline driven primarily by the bread class-action settlement charge; food same-store sales +0.2% vs +6.1% a year earlier (Loblaw release: https://www.loblaw.ca/en/loblaw-reports-2024-second-quarter-results/; CBC).
- CFO Richard Dufresne: "We did notice a bit of an impact in certain stores in specific markets, but that said, at the end of the quarter, things have returned to normal."
- CEO Per Bank: overall impact was "minor." Executives never said the word "boycott" on the call.
- May 16, 2024 — two weeks into the boycott — Loblaw said it was ready to sign the Grocery Code of Conduct if competitors did; Bank said the decision had nothing to do with the boycott (CBC: https://www.cbc.ca/amp/1.7206278).
### 2025-26 status
- No major anniversary coverage; honest framing: the organized boycott faded from headlines while the behaviour went mainstream — Loblaw's own releases: "The Company's discount banners once again outperformed, reflecting continued consumer demand for value" (Q2 2026 release, July 30, 2026).
