## VERDICT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The Tesco template transfers cleanly as a **beat structure**, but two of its load-bearing pieces cannot be copied. Its news-peg hook is a food-additive story, and its #9 is a meat-water story. Both would break house rule 1. The No Frills redo needs a non-health peg. The best candidates are Loblaw's **1 Sep 2026** plan to open "approximately 75 new locations in 2026", which Loblaw says is "focused predominantly on its No Frills and Maxi banners" (A13), and Loblaw's **2 Oct 2026** account-incident update (P16).

**The title premise is not supported by the dated record.** Every Loblaw filing from Q2 2024 to Q2 2026 says the discount banners are leading growth. Loblaw is opening more No Frills, not fewer. The competitor claim that Loblaw's "own quarterly reports" show Canadians leaving No Frills (Broken Canada, 2 Oct 2026) is **contradicted by those reports** (§6).

The honest framing is: *"Here's what's pushing some shoppers away, checked against the record. And here's what the record says about whether they're actually leaving."*

Our own Dec 2025 video contains **32 problem lines** a redo must not repeat (§3). They include factual errors that viewers corrected in the comments.

**Devices inside each numbered item.** Copy these, because they suit our rules:
1. A **title line with a hook noun phrase** ("the checkout that treats you like a suspect").
2. A **relatable scene** in the second person ("You've popped in for a few bits…").
3. The **mechanism**, explained plainly.
4. An explicit **evidence label**: "This one is verified." / "treat it as a widespread frustration rather than a laboratory finding" / "There's no verified evidence of that at Tesco, and I'm not going to claim it." / "the regulator looked and didn't find that."
5. A **fairness line**: "Tesco is far from the only chain…", or the company's rebuttal ("Tesco rejects that characterization").
6. A **then-vs-now nostalgia contrast** (the old butcher, the cashier who knew your name).
7. A **one-line practical fix** ("Turn the pack over…").

The template uses **regulator outcomes in both directions**. In #3, the CMA found loyalty prices were broadly genuine. That is what makes the video credible. Our redo should do the same.

**Template elements we must NOT carry over:**
- The hook: additives, aspartame, MSG, "Frankenstein food". This breaks rule 1.
- #9: water and phosphates in meat, "shrinks into a puddle". This is rules 1 and 2.
- The teaser line "Premium salads pull[ed] from the shelves over contamination warnings". Rule 1. Recalls go only in the DO-NOT-USE appendix.
- Positive 1: "cleaner food". This is a health framing, rule 1.
- The template's UK figures: the ℮ mark, CMA, DEFRA, NI. Rule 6. Canada has no ℮ mark (nf_policies §9).

## 6. HONEST NOTE: IS ANYONE ACTUALLY LEAVING NO FRILLS?

**Short answer.** No dated primary evidence we hold shows Canadians leaving No Frills. Everything Loblaw has filed since 2024 points the other way. Loblaw does **not** publish No Frills-only sales or traffic, so "leaving" cannot be disproved at banner level either. What *can* be shown is (a) a real 2024 boycott of Loblaw banners, and (b) documented frustrations. The evidence for discount growth is listed first because it carries most of the weight.

**Evidence that points away from "leaving" (company filings and statements, tier a; corporate claims, attribute them):**
- **Q2 2024 (the boycott quarter), N7 / A8:** "Food sales growth was led by the ongoing strength of the Company's Maxi and NoFrills hard discount stores." The same quarter's Loblaw-wide food same-store sales growth was **0.2% (2023 – 6.1%)**, and "Food Retail traffic increased and basket size decreased" (A8). **No causal link to the boycott may be stated** (nf_record §3).
- **FY2023–FY2025 MD&A, N8:** "In 2023… consumers increased their focus on value, which benefited the Company's sales due to its strength in private label products, discount banners…" / "In 2024… hard discount banners…" / "In 2025, consumers remained focused on value which benefitted the Company's sales due to its high mix of hard discount stores…"
- **2025 openings, N8:** "open 48 new No Frills® and Maxi® stores (including 39 small format stores)". Q4 2025: "This increased traffic resulted in continued market share gains across its banners." No share percentage is given.
- **Q2 2026, A3 (30 Jul 2026):** "The Company's discount banners once again outperformed, reflecting continued consumer demand for value and greater access to Maxi® and No Frills® stores." Food Retail sales were "supported by higher customer traffic and basket size".
- **Store count, A1 → A3:** franchise stores (all No Frills are franchised, but not all franchise stores are No Frills) rose from **562 to 569** between 3 Jan and 20 Jun 2026.
- **1 Sep 2026, A13 + N1 (CP via Globe):** "approximately 75 new locations in 2026… focused predominantly on its No Frills and Maxi banners, as more Canadians choose hard discount formats". Per Bank: "As they mature, we're seeing strong double-digit same- store sales growth." N1 also says: "Grocers have been expanding their discount footprint across Canada since 2023". That is the outlet's line.
- **Survey (tier d), N5:** Dalhousie Agri-Food Analytics Lab + Caddle, Feb 2025, 3,000 Canadians, reported by Canadian Grocer (Rebecca Harris, 10 Mar 2025). "Consumers said they find the best value when shopping for groceries at discount retailers (35%), followed by traditional grocery stores (21%), Costco (18%) and Walmart (12%)." "Nearly one quarter of respondents (24%) have changed where they shop for groceries in the last year due to economic pressures." The method is described only as "survey". The questionnaire was not seen.

**Evidence of dissatisfaction (real, but not evidence of a No Frills exodus):**
- The **May 2024 boycott** of Loblaw banners, including No Frills (B1–B4). Membership as reported was 45,000 (Apr) and 62,000 (1 May). Per Bank: "The overall financial impact was minor" (B3).

## KEY FACTS (each one dated and sourced in the sections below)

1. **No Frills is a Loblaw "Hard Discount" banner, and every No Frills store is franchised.** As at 3 Jan 2026 Loblaw's AIF lists **No Frills: 0 corporate, 336 franchised stores** (A2). In Loblaw's own words: "Franchisees are independent operators" (A2). Loblaw calls the franchisee an "Owner-Operator" (A4), and its own releases name stores "[First name]'s No Frills®" (A6, A7).
2. **Store names follow an owner-name pattern.** Loblaw's PC Express API listed **351 No Frills store locations** on 3 Oct 2026. Only 6 of those names begin with "NOFRILLS". The rest are prefixed with a name, for example "Nick's NOFRILLS Toronto Yonge" (A5).
3. **Loblaw-wide figures, FY2025 (53 weeks to 3 Jan 2026):** revenue **$63,903 million**, up 6.3% including a 53rd week worth $1,138 million. There were 1,128 food retail stores (566 corporate, 562 franchise) and 2,504 stores in total (A1). In Q2 2026 (to 20 Jun 2026), retail revenue was **$15,046 million** (+4.1%). Loblaw said "The Company's discount banners once again outperformed" (A3). Loblaw's filings give **no numeric market share**. They say only "continued market share gains" (A1).
4. **2024 boycott.** A month-long boycott of Loblaw stores was organised from the Reddit group r/loblawsisoutofcontrol. It began **1 May 2024**, and media named organizer Emily Johnson of Milton, Ont. (B1–B4). Loblaw's Q2 2024 filing reported food retail same-store sales growth of **0.2% (2023: 6.1%)**. It said traffic increased and basket size decreased, and it does not use the word "boycott" (A8). On the call, CEO Per Bank said, per CBC: "The overall financial impact was minor" (B3).
5. **Bread price-fixing: an ADMISSION plus IMMUNITY, not a conviction.** Loblaw and George Weston admitted in 2017 to "an industry-wide price-fixing arrangement involving certain packaged bread products" from late 2001 to March 2015. They received immunity and "will not face criminal charges or penalties" (A1, A2). Canada Bread entered a **GUILTY PLEA** and was fined $50 million on 21 Jun 2023 (A10). The **SETTLEMENT** of the consumer class actions was $500 million, approved by the Ontario Superior Court (May 2025) and the Quebec Superior Court (July 2025) (A1). Claims closed 12 Dec 2025. Payments of **$49.11**, or **$24.11** for those who took the 2018 $25 Loblaw Card, began the week of 11 May 2026 (B8, B9). Allegations against Metro, Sobeys, Walmart Canada, Giant Tiger and Maple Leaf Foods remain **ALLEGATIONS**. Those companies deny them, and the Bureau says it "continues" to investigate (A9, A10, B7).
6. **Grocery Code of Conduct.** Loblaw announced support on **16 May 2024** (A11). The Code has been fully in force since **1 Jan 2026**, with "Loblaw Inc." registered (A12).
7. **Country-of-origin labels.** On **26 Aug 2026** Loblaw said it is "reintroducing the country-of-origin in our fresh produce aisles" and reinstating the "T" tariff symbol (B13, B14). Separately, the CFIA issued two **$10,000 penalties**, in January 2026, to Loblaw-owned stores for maple-leaf / "Product of Canada" displays on imported foods. The stores were a Superstore and a Fortinos, **not No Frills**. CBC also documented No Frills signage examples in 2025 that were corrected (B15–B17).
8. **"Surveillance pricing."** Premier Ford rejected a ban on 16 Apr 2026 (B19). Global News (Colin D'Mello, 18 Aug 2026) reported, from FOI calendar records, that Ford met Loblaw CEO Per Bank at Ford's home on 17 Apr 2026. Global said "the calendar doesn't reveal the subject of the conversation". Loblaw said it is "already on the record as not using surveillance pricing" (B18). Loblaw told The Logic: "We do not use, nor do we plan to use, algorithmic pricing to set or increase prices in our stores, including on our electronic shelf labels" (B20). Manitoba's Bill 49 is listed as enacted, **SM 2026, c. 41** (A14).
9. **Electronic shelf labels.** Loblaw began introducing them in 2018 (B20). Loblaw statement, Apr 2026: "In any particular store, pricing will not vary by customer, time of day, or demand—everyone in a store pays the same price for the same product" (B21).
10. **Drone ad.** The CBC "drone-powered ad" story (11 Sep 2026) was about a **PC Health chatbot**, a Loblaw business. **It was not a No Frills ad** (B22).
11. **Stores.** Loblaw is converting stores to No Frills. Examples: the Loblaws at 2280 Dundas St. W., Toronto, closed **2 May 2026** to become a No Frills (B24, B25), and a No Frills opened **17 Sep 2026** in the former Whole Foods at 4771 Yonge St. (A6, B26). Loblaw now plans **about 75 openings in 2026**, "focused predominantly on its No Frills and Maxi banners" (A13). No closure of an operating No Frills in 2025–2026 was confirmed by a tier-a or strong tier-b source (see UNVERIFIED).

## KEY FINDINGS FOR THE WRITER (each one is sourced below)

1. **No Frills dropped multi-buy pricing in July 2024.** Loblaw's release (3 Jul 2024) said: "Stores across the country have removed the multi-buy promotion". Loblaw's own PC Express API still shows "MIN" multi-unit badges at other Loblaw banners: 208 items at Loblaws #1032 and 91 at RCSS #1080 in our 3 Oct 2026 sample. At four No Frills stores the sample showed none, with one exception: No Frills #3907 (Mississauga) showed **7 two-litre soft drinks badged "$1.69 MIN 3", regular price $2.79**. Treat this as an API observation and nothing more (§2).
2. **The No Frills price-match ("Won't Be Beat") programme appears to be live, but we could not open its page.** nofrills.ca/en/price-match draws its text in the browser with JavaScript, and none of our tools could read it. On 3 Oct 2026 Loblaw's own pickup-location API still told shoppers at 61 No Frills locations: "No coupons or price matching for PC Express." The terms we have (limit 4 per item, list of exclusions) come only from search-engine summaries. **Verify on the live page before air** (§1).
3. **Returns.** nofrills.ca, verbatim: refund or exchange "within 14 days of purchase with original tags and packaging. Exchange only after 14 days." There is also a "Try It You'll Love It Guarantee" on PC and no name food (§3).
4. **Scanner Price Accuracy Code (voluntary).** The Competition Bureau describes it this way: a scanned price higher than the displayed price means the item is free if it is worth less than $10, or the shopper gets $10 off. It does not cover items priced by weight, items with a price tag on them, or loyalty/member pricing. Canadian Press (19 Feb 2026) names "Loblaw Cos. Ltd." as a signatory. It does not apply in Quebec, which has its own $15 law (§7).
5. **Separate from the code, the Competition Act s.54 ("double ticketing") is a statute.** No one may supply a product above "the lowest of two or more prices clearly expressed", and that includes "an in-store or other point-of-purchase display". The fine is up to $10,000 (§8).
6. **Canada checks net quantity with tolerances plus a lot average, not a minimum-only rule.** SFCR s.199 and Schedule 4 set a tolerance for each package. Under s.200(4) a lot fails if the weighted average is below the declared quantity, if too many units fall short by more than the tolerance, or if two or more fall short by more than twice the tolerance. The Competition Bureau's guidance calls the method "commonly known as the Average System". **There is no Canadian 'e' mark** (§9).
7. **Unit pricing is not required by law anywhere No Frills trades.** It is mandatory only in Quebec, and No Frills has no Quebec stores according to Loblaw's own store API. Manitoba announced on 22 Jun 2026 that it will adopt unit pricing. Federal Bill C-226 has been in committee since 22 Apr 2026. The Competition Bureau's 3 Sept 2026 follow-up still lists unit pricing as a recommendation (§10).
8. **"Product of Canada" vs "Made in Canada".** These are CFIA rules. The CFIA has fined two Loblaw-owned stores $10,000 each, issued 15 Jan 2026: RCSS store #1033 and Fortinos #7920, both in Toronto. CBC reported that both were for promoting imported food as Canadian. No No Frills store appears by name in the CFIA penalty table. CBC found "Prepared in Canada" maple-leaf signage on US strawberries at a Toronto No Frills in July 2025, and the CFIA says "Prepared in Canada" is inappropriate for a product that was only packaged in Canada. Loblaw's "T" tariff shelf symbols returned in Aug 2026 (§11).
9. **Self-checkout and security, as reported by named outlets.** Loblaw tested receipt-scanner exit gates in 2024 (CBC) and wheel-locking carts in 2023 (CBC). It expanded a body-worn camera pilot in 2025 (Loblaw's own page). It is piloting pcogo scan-as-you-shop at three Toronto No Frills (Loblaw release, 6 Nov 2025). Loblaw's July 2026 statement says electronic shelf labels "cannot identify who is standing at the shelf" (§12).
10. **PC Optimum.** Points come from loaded personalised offers and on-shelf promotions. Offers expire "typically around 7 days" after release. PC Financial was sold to EQB, deal completed 1 Jul 2026, and EQ Bank says "no changes to the PC Optimum program… as a direct result of the acquisition". The PC Optimum Insiders page says "new subscriptions… are now closed". Loblaw's 2 Oct 2026 account notice says a March 2026 incident exposed details that included names, addresses, dates of birth and loyalty account numbers (§5).

## 7. Scanner Price Accuracy Code (voluntary)

**Competition Bureau, S1 (a), date modified 2022-01-19, verbatim:**
> "Although there is no specific law that applies to the accuracy of a scanned price, some overcharges may violate the Competition Act, for example, charging more than the advertised price."
> "The Competition Bureau regards scanner price accuracy as an important element of maintaining consumer confidence. This is why we have endorsed the Scanner Price Accuracy Voluntary Code, which is managed by the Retail Council of Canada on behalf of participating Canadian retailers. Under the code, when the scanned price of an item without a price tag is higher than the displayed price, the customer is entitled to receive the item free of charge when it is worth less than $10, or receive a $10 reduction if the correct price is worth more than $10."

**Canadian Press, S2 (b), Tara Deschamps, 19 Feb 2026, verbatim:**
- "The code is a voluntary policy created in 2002… supported by three industry groups: the Retail Council of Canada, the Canadian Federation of Independent Grocers and the Neighbourhood Pharmacy Association of Canada."
- "If you're buying multiples of the same item, the code says the shopper gets the first one free and all subsequent items at the price they should have been charged."
- "If more than one is being purchased, the customer receives $10 off the first item."
- "the code doesn't apply to items priced by weight… (For a pre-packaged bag of apples with a set price, and scanned with a barcode, the code can be enforced, said Belanger.)"
- "The code does not apply to items with prices physically attached to them — merchandise with sale or clearance stickers…"
- "The code applies across most of Canada but not in certain provinces or territories like Quebec…"
- "It's only applied at retailers that sign the code, which include Best Buy, Canadian Tire, Costco, Giant Tiger, Loblaw Cos. Ltd., Metro, Rona, Shoppers Drug Mart, Sobeys, Home Depot Canada and Walmart Canada."
- "look for signage at the front of the store or ask a cashier" (Santo Ligotti, RCC)
- "Retailers can limit the quantity of items in a single transaction they apply the code to, Ligotti said."
- "the code only applies when the product matches the shelf label."
- "loyalty and membership-based pricing does not fall under the purview of the code."
- "The organizations overseeing the code run an online complaint form…"

**Retail Council page (S3):** HTTP 403. **Not read**, so the official participant list is not verified.

**Competition Bureau's role:** it endorses the code (S1). It does not run it; RCC manages it. The code itself is not law.

**OUR NOTE:** No Frills stores are franchised. Store names in P4 read "Bo's NO FRILLS", "Dean's NOFRILLS", and C4 lists "Jordan's No Frills". Whether Loblaw's signature covers each franchised No Frills is not stated in any source we read. The CP advice applies: look for the code sign at the front of the store.

**What it means for the shopper:** if a barcoded item without its own price sticker scans above the shelf price, ask for the code. Under $10 you get it free; over $10 you get $10 off. This applies to the first unit only. It does not cover weighed produce or meat, stickered clearance items, or PC Optimum member prices. In Quebec the provincial law (S5) is stronger: free under $15, $15 off above.

## 8. Shelf-label accuracy: the law behind "two prices"

**Competition Act s.54 (S4, a, current to 2026-09-21), verbatim:**
> "54 (1) No person shall supply a product at a price that exceeds the lowest of two or more prices clearly expressed by him or on his behalf, in respect of the product in the quantity in which it is so supplied and at the time at which it is so supplied, (a) on the product, its wrapper or container; (b) on anything attached to, inserted in or accompanying the product, its wrapper or container or anything on which the product is mounted for display or sale; or (c) on an in-store or other point-of-purchase display or advertisement."
> "(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence and liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding one year or to both."

**What it means for the shopper:** if the shelf label and the till disagree, federal law says the lower clearly-expressed price applies. That is separate from the voluntary $10 code. Quebec's OPC applies the same idea to individually tagged items: "they must sell items at the advertised price, never at a higher price" (S5).

## 9. Net quantity and weighing: Canada's system vs the UK 'e' mark

### 9.1 Who enforces what
- **Measurement Canada (M5, a, modified 2020-02-04), verbatim:** "Examples of products inspected by Measurement Canada for accurate quantity are: clerk-served products such as meats sold at deli counters…; bulk or non-packaged retail products, such as loose fruits and vegetables, bulk candies sold from bins…" "Retail, packaged products are not subject to the Weights and Measures Act but are subject to the requirements of the Consumer Packaging and Labelling Act (CPLA). Responsibility for enforcement of the CPLA is shared by: Competition Bureau (packaged, non-food products); Canadian Food Inspection Agency (packaged food products)."
- **Measurement Canada complaints page (M6, a, modified 2025-12-22), verbatim:** "The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) sets the requirements for consumer prepackaged food sold in retail stores, such as grocery stores and supermarkets. Suspected measurement errors related to consumer prepackaged food, such as inaccurate pricing or net quantity or weight declarations, should be reported to the CFIA." "Sellers must not include the weight of the container holding individually measured foods in the weight of the food." "You should always try to resolve the issue before filing a complaint."
- **Scale inspection frequency (M7, a, modified 2024-03-11):** "2201 Canadian supermarkets and grocery stores… All devices used in this subsector are subject to a five-year mandatory examination frequency."
- **Weights and Measures Act s.33(1) (M9, a), verbatim:** "Every person who sells or offers for sale any commodity, by number or unit of measurement, is guilty of an offence if the quantity of the commodity that that person delivers or offers for sale is, subject to prescribed limits of error, less than the quantity that that person (a) purports to sell or offer for sale; or (b) should deliver or offer for sale on the basis of (i) the total price paid or to be paid for the commodity, and (ii) the stated price per number or unit of measurement."

OUR NOTE: the M5 page predates the SFCR wording and still says "CPLA". For food, the current net-quantity rule is in the SFCR (M1–M3).

### 9.2 The packaged-food rule (SFCR), verbatim
**s.199(3) (M1):** a net quantity declaration is not false or misleading if "(b) the actual net quantity of the food is, subject to the tolerance provided under subsection (5), not less than its declared net quantity."

**s.200(4) (M2), lot inspection:**
> "The lot from which a sample was collected and examined by an inspector does not meet the conditions of paragraphs 199(3)(a) and (b) if the inspector determines that
> (a) the weighted average quantity of the units in the sample, as determined by the formula set out in Part 2 of Schedule 5, is less than the declared net quantity;
> (b) the number of units in the sample that contain less than the declared net quantity by more than the prescribed tolerance set out in Schedule 4 for that quantity is equal to or greater than the number set out in column 2 of Part 4 of Schedule 5 for the sample size set out in column 1 of that Part; or
> (c) two or more units in the sample contain less than the declared net quantity by more than twice the prescribed tolerance set out in Schedule 4 for that quantity."

**Schedule 4, Table 3 (M3), metric, food other than catch-weight, verbatim:**

| ≤ 50 g or mL | 9 | — |
| > 50 to ≤ 100 g or mL | — | 4.5 |
| > 100 to ≤ 200 g or mL | 4.5 | — |
| > 200 to ≤ 300 g or mL | — | 9 |

**Schedule 4, Table 1 (M3), metric, catch-weight (individually weighed packs such as meat trays), verbatim:** ≤ 60 g: 10%; > 60 g to ≤ 600 g: 6 g; > 600 g to ≤ 1 kg: 1%; > 1 kg to ≤ 1.5 kg: 10 g; > 1.5 kg to ≤ 3 kg: 0.66%; > 3 kg to ≤ 4 kg: 20 g; > 4 kg to ≤ 10 kg: 0.5%; > 10 kg to ≤ 15 kg: 50 g; … **Table 6, by count:** "< 50: 0"; "≥ 50 but ≤ 100: 1".

**Competition Bureau's explanation (M4, a, enforcement guidelines dated 1 Oct 1999; page modified 2022-01-19), verbatim:**
> "sample results are used as the basis for determining lot compliance. individual samples may vary from the declared net quantity within the range of the allowable tolerance. the number of units in the sample which do not meet the accuracy requirements is limited. the weighted average net quantity of the samples must meet or exceed the stated quantity."
> "In the OIML document the inspection methods, compliance criteria and sampling procedures, commonly known as the Average System, were developed…"
> "1. … the average net contents of the packages in a lot may not be less than the declared quantity… 2. … No more than 2.5% of the lot may have a negative error larger than the tolerance… 3. … Not more than one package may contain less than twice the permitted tolerance."
> Guide note on the average: "There is no tolerance on the weighted lot average, so the value calculated by the formula must be equal to or greater than the declared quantity."

**OUR NOTE (answer to "average or minimum?"):** Canada uses a hybrid that the Bureau itself calls the "Average System" (OIML-based). A lot must meet the declared quantity on (weighted) average, and only limited short-fills beyond a per-package tolerance are allowed. Canada has **no 'e' estimated-sign mark**. Worked example from Table 3: a 400 g pack has a 3% tolerance (12 g). One pack at 390 g is within tolerance. A pack at 380 g is "short by more than the tolerance", and two packs below 376 g (more than twice the tolerance, 24 g) fail the lot under s.200(4)(c). The lot also fails if the weighted average is under 400 g. (The UK comparison is not sourced in this dossier; do not quote UK figures as Canadian.)

### 9.3 Pricing by weight must be metric (M8, a, modified 2021-05-02), verbatim
> "Stores must use metric units when displaying the price of fruit, vegetables, nuts and other food sold in bulk (unpackaged) on signs as well as in print and online advertising. They can also choose to include Canadian (imperial) units along with the metric units."
> "price per kilogram ($/kg) or per hundred grams ($/100 g) (required)"; "price per pound ($/lb) or per ounce ($/oz) (optional)"; "both prices must be equivalent (e.g. $10/kg and $4.54/lb)".

## 10. Unit pricing in Canada

- **Status where No Frills trades: not mandatory.** Bill C-226's sponsor told the Commons (U4, Hansard): "Quebec is currently the only province where unit pricing is mandated by law" and "The practice of unit pricing remains voluntary, and it is applied inconsistently from one province or territory to the next".
- **Quebec (S6, a, last update 30 May 2025), verbatim:** "In businesses that are exempt from the individual price labelling rule, the same unit of measurement must be used for all food products of the same nature in order to make them easier to compare… For example, all juice containers would indicate a price per 100 ml, whereas all cereal boxes would indicate a price per 100 grams." Also: the member price "may not exceed 25% of the size of the price for non-members"; for "3 for $5" style offers, "the price per unit must be sufficiently visible relative to the price for the set". (No Frills has no Quebec stores, per P4.)
- **Manitoba (U2, a, 22 Jun 2026):** the study includes "measures to combat shrinkflation by adopting unit pricing"; highlight: "implementing unit pricing to combat shrinkflation". This is an announcement, not yet law.
- **Federal (U5, a, 26 Jan 2026, PMO):** the planned National Food Security Strategy "will also include measures to implement unit price labelling and support the work of the Competition Bureau…"
- **Bill C-226 (U3, a, LEGISinfo):** "National Framework for Food Price Transparency Act". Introduced 18 Sept 2025. Second reading and referral to committee (AGRI) on 22 Apr 2026. "Current status: At consideration in committee in the House of Commons". It is a private member's bill.
- **Competition Bureau (U1, a, 3 Sept 2026):** recommendation 3: "Implement clear and consistent unit pricing: Encourage all provinces and territories to adopt clear, consistent unit pricing requirements…". The Bureau also writes: "The Government of Canada launched its National Food Security Strategy. The strategy… urges provinces and territories to implement standardized unit price labelling".
- **Canada Grocery Code (U6, a, 5 Jan 2026):** "as of January 1, 2026, the Canada Grocery Code has been fully implemented"; "All major national grocery retailers (Costco Wholesale Canada Ltd., Empire Company Limited, Loblaw Inc., Metro Inc., and Wal‑Mart Canada Corp) have formally registered". ## 11. "Product of Canada", "Made in Canada", maple leaves and "T" tags

**CFIA rules (O1, a, modified 2023-12-06), verbatim** (also in eggs_rules_labels.md §5):
- "The use of 'Product of Canada' and 'Made in Canada' claims is voluntary. However, once a company chooses to make one of these claims, the product to which it is applied should meet these guidelines."
- "A food product may use the claim 'Product of Canada' when all or virtually all major ingredients, processing, and labour used to make the food product are Canadian… Generally, the percentage referred to as very little or minor is considered to be less than a total of 2% of the product".

## KEY FINDINGS FOR THE WRITER

**Recommended "3 things No Frills gets right"**, strongest first, each with its catch:

1. **Lower prices than its sister Loblaws banner, and No Name usually undercuts the national brand on the same shelf.**
   - On 3 Oct 2026, the same 15 product codes cost **$73.24 at No Frills #7952 (Toronto)** and **$78.51 at Loblaws #1032 (Markham)**. No Frills was cheaper on 11 of the 15 items [reused, nf_prices §4b].
   - At No Frills #7952 and #3155, No Name Tomato Ketchup 1 L was **$3.69 against Heinz 1 L at $5.79**, both at regular price (§1.3).
   - Loblaw's own claim (Annual Report 2025): "25% Average savings by switching from comparable brand to no name®".
   - **Catch:** No Frills was *not* the cheapest Loblaw banner. Real Canadian Superstore #1080 Mississauga came in at $70.97 for the same basket. A national brand on sale sometimes beats No Name: Unico Diced Tomatoes 796 ml was $1.79 on sale vs No Name at $2.00. The **egg data does not show a No Frills advantage**: the No Name dozen cost the same at No Frills, Loblaws and Superstore in the same city, and matched Walmart's Great Value (§1.4).
2. **PC Optimum is free to join and its redemption value is plainly stated.** pcoptimum.ca says: "Joining PC Optimum™ is free" and "10,000 points = $10 redeemable value*". Points can also be donated to charity. **Catch:** at grocery stores the site lists no base points-per-dollar for a plain card, only "weekly offers in the app, and in your flyer". Offers must be loaded and lapse after about 7 days. An account "can be closed" after 2 years with no earning or spending. CBC Go Public (7 Apr 2025) reported members locked out of accounts. Loblaw's 2 Oct 2026 notice reports a data incident (§2).
3. **Food-waste tools that also save money: Flashfood and the restored 50% near-date markdown.** Loblaw and Flashfood said (2 Mar 2026) that in 2025 the partnership "saved customers more than $58 million on groceries" and is in "over 900 Loblaw® grocery stores… including select No Frills®". In January 2024 Loblaw told CBC: "We've listened to the feedback from our customers and colleagues and are reverting [where it existed before] to the 50 per cent off discounts". **Catch:** only "select" No Frills have Flashfood, and the pounds-diverted figures are modelled on an assumed 50% discount. The 50% markdown reversal came after public backlash, and we have no current primary page confirming it at every No Frills (§3).

**Strong alternates:**
- Every No Frills is franchise-owned by a named "Owner-Operator". The AIF lists 0 corporate and 336 franchised stores (§5).
- Country-of-origin labels returned to produce aisles (26 Aug 2026), along with "T" tariff tags (§6).
- Loblaw is a named signatory of the Scanner Price Accuracy Code: item free under $10, $10 off above that (§7).
- "Try It You'll Love It" guarantee and the produce "replace AND refund" pledge (§8).
- Rain checks with 10% back in points, plus a live "Hit of the Month" badge (§4).

**Named surveys (§10):**
- **BrandSpark 2026 Most Trusted** (published 20 Nov 2025; 45,394 shoppers; unaided write-in) named No Frills "Grocery Store for Low Prices / Affordability (National)" and Most Trusted for "Store Brand / Private Label Products". It named PC Optimum "Grocery Loyalty Program". **Catch:** BrandSpark does not publish field dates, and winners pay to license the award seal.
- **Leger WOW 2025** (fieldwork Oct–Nov 2024) is not a positive. No Frills scored 65.5 in Ontario, which is mid-pack: above Loblaws (59.2) and Real Canadian Superstore (62.7), below Food Basics (66.4) and FreshCo (68.5). In the West it scored 56.2.

**Do NOT say:**
- "No Frills has the cheapest eggs."
- "Haul deals" as a current programme. No such programme was found; "Hauler" is 2018–19 campaign language.
- That the No Name price freeze is ongoing. It ran 17 Oct 2022 – 31 Jan 2023 and ended.
- That every No Frills has Flashfood, 50% markdowns or the scanner code. None of these is confirmed store by store.
