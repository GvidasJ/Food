### 2.3 THE MATH — SHOW EVERY STEP (the honest version)

**The documented "cooked < raw" case — Costco, April 2022 (CBC/RCI):**
- Rotisserie bird: **$7.99**, weighed **1.35 kg cooked**; CBC estimated it was ~**1.64 kg raw** before roasting.
- Raw whole chicken at the same Costco: **$6.49/kg**.
- Step 1: To take home the same bird raw, you'd buy ~1.64 kg × $6.49/kg = **$10.64**.
- Step 2: Cooked bird price = **$7.99**. → The cooked bird was **$2.65 cheaper (~25% less) than buying its raw self** — before spices, energy or labour. CBC framed it: raw chickens cost **33% more per kilogram** than the rotisserie equivalent ($6.49 vs ~$4.88/kg raw-equivalent for the cooked bird).
- CBC/RCI (Dec 30, 2022): rotisserie chickens are "$8 to $12 at larger grocery chains in Canada," and Rowe Farms (Ontario) confirmed the birds are sold **below cost as a loss leader**; "Loblaws, Sobeys, Save-On-Foods, Costco and Metro" all declined to discuss pricing strategy. Source: https://ici.radio-canada.ca/rci/en/news/1945312/

**The 2026 re-run (our arithmetic on sourced numbers — label as "our math" on screen):**

*Costco:* cooked bird $7.99–$9.00 (RCI, Feb 2026), label weight 1.2 kg. Raw national average $7.62/kg (StatCan, Jul 2026). A typical raw whole bird per CBC is 1.6–1.8 kg → raw bird costs 1.6–1.8 kg × $7.62 = **$12.19–$13.72**. Cooked Costco bird at $7.99–$9.00 = **cheaper than an average Canadian raw whole chicken by $3–$5.50 per bird. The paradox HOLDS at Costco, per bird, on current sourced numbers.** Even per raw-equivalent kilogram (a 1.2 kg cooked bird ≈ 1.45–1.6 kg raw using CBC's shrink ratio), $7.99 ÷ ~1.5 kg ≈ **$5.33/kg vs the $7.62/kg national raw average — cooked still wins.**

*Loblaws/Superstore:* cooked PC bird $13.00 / 900 g–1 kg = **$13.00–$14.44/kg cooked**. Raw PC whole chicken $15.00/bird (flyer, Sept 2026, aggregator) — a raw PC bird is typically 1.6–2 kg, so ≈ $7.50–$9.40/kg raw. **Per bird: cooked $13 < raw $15 — headline holds. Per kilogram: raw is roughly half the cooked price — headline fails.** The cooked bird is also smaller (900 g–1 kg vs 1.6–2 kg raw). Be explicit on camera: at Loblaw banners you pay less per bird but take home less chicken.

*Walmart:* cooked 800 g bird $8.87 = $11.09/kg; cooked 1.2 kg bird $14.97 = $12.48/kg. StatCan raw average $7.62/kg → a 1.5 kg raw bird ≈ $11.43. **Per bird: Walmart's $8.87 cooked 800 g bird beats an average raw bird's total price; per kg it's ~46% more than raw average.** (Walmart's own standard raw whole-chicken per-kg price must be captured in store for the clean same-store comparison.)

*Sobeys:* cooked 1 kg $12.99 (Voilà) = $12.99/kg vs $7.62/kg raw national average — **per kg raw wins; per bird a 1.7 kg raw bird ≈ $12.95 — essentially a tie with the $12.99 cooked bird.**

**BOTTOM LINE FOR THE SCRIPT (do not overclaim):** The title's claim is documented, verbatim, at Costco: CBC found the cooked bird "literally cheaper than a raw chicken at the same store" (April 2022), and on September 2026 sourced numbers the Costco bird still costs less than the average Canadian raw whole chicken. At full-price grocery banners (Loblaws, Sobeys, Metro, Save-On, IGA), the cooked bird usually costs less **per bird** than a raw bird of typical size but **more per kilogram** — largely because rotisserie birds are smaller (CBC: raw birds 1.6–1.8 kg vs rotisserie ~1.2 kg). Say both halves.

**Why (attributed, no speculation):** Rowe Farms told CBC the chickens are sold below cost as a loss leader; Calgary Co-op's Barb Munro said (verbatim above) they're priced "as a convenience to our members" who "fill their baskets with other items"; Costco's scale — **157.4 million rotisserie chickens sold worldwide in fiscal 2025** (reported from Costco's January 2026 shareholders' meeting; The Takeout: https://www.thetakeout.com/2094384/costco-sold-millions-rotisserie-chickens-2025/ ; Tasting Table: https://www.tastingtable.com/2207713/how-many-rotisserie-chickens-costco-sold-2025/), up from 106 million in fiscal 2021 (CBC/RCI, 2022).

## SECTION 3 — WEIGHT INVESTIGATION

- **Costco's published standard (US):** The Daily Meal (Dec 2, 2023): "Costco's rotisserie chickens reportedly weigh 3 pounds in size, including bones." ## SECTION 4 — LABEL DETAILS (WHAT CANADIAN RULES REQUIRE ON THESE BIRDS)

All quotes below are verbatim from CFIA, "Labelling requirements for meat and poultry products," https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/meat-and-poultry-products (retrieved Sept 2026):

1. **The in-store rotisserie label is a special category.** *"Meat and poultry products barbecued, roasted or broiled on the retail premises where sold must be labelled to inform the consumer about the safe storage requirements for cooked meats, poultry and their by-products [B.01.003(1)(b) to (c), FDR]. These labels must include, the common name of the product on the principal display panel, the name and principal place of business of the distributor, the packaging date and the net quantity (by weight) of the meat or poultry product being sold."* — So every bird's label must show a **net weight and a packaging date**: read both on camera at all nine stores.
2. **Why some birds have no ingredient list:** *"prepackaged meat, meat by-products, poultry, poultry meat and poultry meat by-products that are barbecued, roasted or broiled on the retail premises are exempt from declaring a list of ingredients [B.01.008(2)(d), FDR]"*. This is why a hot-counter bird can legally carry a sparser label than a packaged chicken — present neutrally as "the rules are different at the hot counter."
3. **But added phosphates/water can trigger an ingredient list at retail:** *"For products packaged at the retail level, an ingredient list is required when a type 1 or type 2 meat product that is not cured to which phosphate salts or water have been added is packaged for retail sale by the retailer [B.01.091, FDR]."*
4. **Nutrition Facts on additive/water-added birds:** CFIA: a Nutrition Facts table *"is required on meat and poultry to which food additives or water have been added whether packed at retail or packed at the manufacturer. The FDR exemption for raw single ingredient meats does not apply to meats containing a food additive or water added"* — and such products are also *"subject to the FOP nutrition symbol requirements [B.01.350, FDR]"*. (Consistent with the Costco Canada bag carrying a full NFt.)
5. **"Seasoned" is a regulated word:** *"the label of a consumer prepackaged poultry carcass that is dressed or partially dressed and has been graded and seasoned must be labelled with the words 'Seasoned' / 'assaisonné' [293, SFCR]"* (as applicable to interprovincially traded/imported/exported graded carcasses). Note how many roster products bake it into the name: Kirkland "**Seasoned** Rotisserie Chicken," PC "**Seasoned** Whole Chicken Barbecue," Walmart "Roasted **Seasoned** Chicken."
6. **"Up to X% solution" wording:** The US-style phrase "seasoned with up to X% solution" is not the Canadian formula. Canada's retained-water wording for raw single-ingredient meat is: *"'up to X% water retained', 'less than X% water retained', and 'up to X% retained water added due to processing'"* — and note: *"a declaration of retained water for dressed poultry carcasses is not required"* because water-retention standards for poultry carcasses are set in the Canadian Standards of Identity, Volume 7. **On camera: check each label for water/broth in the ingredient list rather than expecting a % solution statement.**
7. **Nutrition on deli signage:** CFIA: *"A disclaimer such as 'subject to change with various suppliers' is not acceptable on retail store signs to qualify nutrition information covering a variety of meats on display. The retailer is responsible for providing accurate information concerning the product as sold."*
8. **Bone rule for NFt values:** CFIA: *"the fat trim and skin are considered part of the edible portion... The bone is not counted toward the values in the NFt."* Useful when comparing per-100 g label values across birds.
