# PART 2 — REGULATION, SCIENCE, CANADIAN CONNECTIONS

## a. The law — Canada has NO standard for "soy sauce" (CONFIRMED)
- Full-text search of FDR (C.R.C. c.870) + all 19 CFCS volumes: **no soy sauce standard exists anywhere.** Vol. 6 (spices/seasonings/dressings) standardizes curry powder and salad dressing — not soy sauce. Soy sauce = "unstandardized food."
- **The decode:** acid-hydrolyzed soy protein + caramel colour + glucose-fructose + salt water, never fermented, made in days, can legally be sold as "soya sauce" in Canada. Only backstops: FDA s.5(1) (not misleading) + accurate ingredient list.
- **The one legal gift to viewers:** FDR B.01.010(3)(a) table item 8 (since SOR/2011-28) — hydrolyzed plant proteins MUST name the plant in the common name: **"hydrolysed soy protein" is forced onto the label.** The fingerprint is mandatory. Brewed reads: water, soybeans, wheat, salt.
- **International contrast (the killer beat):** JAPAN legally grades and discloses method (honjōzō = genuine brewed vs kongō = blended). **CHINA: GB 2717-2018 (in force Dec 2019) — unfermented/blended products CANNOT be called "soy sauce" (酱油) at all**, reclassified as "compound seasoning." Taiwan (2019): mandatory brewed/blended labelling. US: no standard (FDA 1 ppm 3-MCPD guidance only). EU: no standard but hard 3-MCPD limit 20 µg/kg (50x stricter than Canada's guideline). 🔥 **China regulates the words "soy sauce" more strictly than Canada does.**
- "Naturally brewed"/"fermented" = voluntary claims under CFIA method-of-production guidance (truthful + substantiable on request) — not a regulated grade.
- Allergens: soy AND wheat = priority allergens (declared). 🔥 Brewed shoyu is ~half wheat — "soy sauce is a wheat product" surprises most viewers; tamari = the wheat-free branch.

## b. The FOP sodium symbol — the timely on-shelf visual (CONFIRMED, in force NOW)
- SOR/2022-168: "High in sodium" magnifying-glass symbol **mandatory since Jan 1, 2026**.
- Soy sauce reference amount = **15 mL** (HC Table of Reference Amounts, item R.5 verbatim: "Major condiments, such as ketchup, steak sauce, soy sauce…"). ≤30 mL → **10% DV threshold = just 230 mg per serving.**
- Kikkoman ≈ **960 mg/15 mL = 42% DV — more than 4x the trigger.** Even Less Sodium (~590 mg, 26% DV) exceeds it. **Virtually every regular soy sauce bottle in Canada must now wear the symbol.** Film the shelf.
- Fun aside: restaurant/takeout packets escape via the restaurant-portion exemption. Pre-2026 stock may lawfully sell through (CFIA implementation plan) — if a bottle lacks the symbol, that's old stock, not an exemption.
- Sodium math: one tablespoon ≈ 900–1,000 mg ≈ **~40% of a day's sodium**. VERIFY exact mg/%DV on the physical Canadian bottle at shoot.

## c. 3-MCPD — regulator-position framing ONLY (replaces reference's scare spine)
- What it is: a process contaminant that can form when protein is hydrolyzed with hydrochloric acid in the presence of residual fats — an acid-HVP issue, NOT a fermentation issue.
- **Health Canada's current List of Maximum Levels, Table 1 verbatim: "3-MCPD — 1 mg/kg — In Asian-style sauces such as soy, oyster, mushroom sauces, etc."**
- Attributed history: UK FSA June 2001 survey — ~a quarter of samples over the limit, worst ~2,000x; brands named and withdrawn; FSA's 2002 follow-up: failures down to ~6% — industry cleaned up fast. Chinese retail surveys: traditionally brewed samples were below even the EU's ultra-strict 0.02 mg/kg limit — 🔥 the cleanest neutral proof of why brewed vs hydrolyzed matters.
- **MANDATORY FAIRNESS CLAUSE:** modern HVP production controls 3-MCPD; regulators report dramatic improvement since 2001; Canadian-shelf products are subject to Health Canada's 1 ppm maximum — NO claim that anything on Canadian shelves exceeds it. No "carcinogen," no "toxic," no kidney-damage framing. "A managed process contaminant with a Health Canada maximum" — full stop.

## d. The process (neutral: craft vs shortcut)
- Brewed: roasted wheat + steamed soybeans + **koji mould (Aspergillus oryzae)** → moromi mash in brine → **months (up to 6+) of fermentation**; enzymes create natural glutamates (umami); Kikkoman SAYS "more than 300 aroma compounds" (attribute — their marketing).
- Hydrolyzed: defatted soy protein boiled in **hydrochloric acid** (hours; whole process ~days), neutralized with sodium hydroxide, then caramel colour + corn syrup + salt added to imitate what fermentation would have made. **Legal, safe, built affordable pantry staples for generations — "days vs months, chemistry vs microbiology," the consumer point is flavour + what you're paying for, not danger.**
- 🔥 The additive-compensation logic (reference's best decode, fully house-safe): every extra ingredient on a hydrolyzed label is a patch for something fermentation would have produced free — corn syrup = missing sweetness, caramel = missing colour, MSG/disodium salts = missing umami, potassium sorbate = missing natural preservation.
- **WWII origin (CONFIRMED):** wartime import cut-offs pushed North American brands to hydrolyzed vegetable protein — the shortcut stuck. "Your grandparents' soy sauce is a wartime substitution that never got un-substituted."

## e. Canadian connections
- 🔥 **Japan's soy foods run on Canadian soybeans:** Canada = world's **#4 soybean exporter**; ~1.2M t/yr food-grade, "virtually all exported" (Soy Canada); **~44% of Japan's food-grade soybean import market (USDA FAS, 2020)**; Soy Canada (Dec 2025): **"in 2024, Canada exported $400 million of soybeans to Japan"** (~45% for tofu). Ontario/Quebec identity-preserved beans (CIPRS certification via Canadian Grain Commission) named by the trade for "edamame, tofu, soy sauce and miso." ⚠️ Do NOT claim Kikkoman specifically buys Canadian (they say "North American-grown" — unverified).
- **China Lily context (respectful, checkable):** Lee's Food Products **founded 1947 — the very year the Chinese Exclusion Act was repealed**; founder born in Guangdong, arrived 1913 (head-tax era), died 1962; Queen St E, Leslieville factory made China Lily **1947–2020** (production then moved to Scarborough); three generations of the family; put Chinese ingredients into mainstream Canadian grocery stores decades before "international aisles"; Toronto Preservation Board fought for heritage status, Council denied it Oct 2022. ⚠️ Founder's personal name NOT confirmed — coordinate with brand dossier before naming.
- History beats: soy sauce advertised for sale in **Saint John, NB in 1831 — 36 years before Confederation**; first Canadian soybeans grown at Ontario Agricultural College (Guelph) **1881**; Kikkoman traces to 1603 Noda (Mogi/Takanashi families; Noda Shoyu Co. formed 1917); Walworth, Wisconsin plant 1972-73 = "**one of** the first" US plants built by a Japanese company (⚠️ never "the first").
- **Sodium & the 50+ audience (neutral):** average Canadian intake 2,760 mg/day vs 2,300 max; 77% of dietary sodium from processed foods (Health Canada 2017); **~51% of adults 60-79 have hypertension** (StatCan CHMS). Landing is useful, not preachy: less-sodium versions cut ~40%; the new FOP symbol now does the shelf-scanning for you.
