## 1. BRAND TABLE — verified

### Jamieson — Canada's #1 (open the countdown here)
- ~25% of the Canadian VMS market, TSX-listed (JWEL), makes in Windsor ON. Owns Progressive, Smart Solutions, Iron Vegan, Precision, youtheory.
- **"360 Pure" is Jamieson's own internal QC program (≥360 internal steps) — not third-party certification.** The label reads like an external seal; it isn't one.
- Base **Jamieson Magnesium 250 mg**: elemental magnesium as "(**oxide**, gluconate, lactate)" — oxide-led blend — with **titanium dioxide** as a non-medicinal.
- **April 2026 Health Canada Type-3 recall**: certain Joint Pain Relief, Magnesium Bisglycinate and Maca lots for **defective tamper-evident seals**.
- Multi gummies ~1.24 g sugar/gummy ⚠️ retailer-sourced, read pack on camera.

### Kirkland Signature (Costco Canada)
- **Costco doesn't make it.** Canadian Kirkland vitamins are made by **Vita Health Products Inc., Winnipeg** (named recalling firm on Kirkland recalls).
- **Vita Health has been owned since Sept 28 2021 by IVC (US CDMO), whose parent is China's Aland group.**
- **Two metal events:** July 2024 "metal fibres" recall (multiple store brands) + **May 21 2026** recall of Kirkland Signature Women 50+ Multivitamin (NPN 80052405) for **metal fragments**.
- ⚠️ The famous **USP Verified** mark applies to **US** Kirkland SKUs — NOT confirmed on Canadian SKUs. Do not claim it.
- **Kirkland D3 1000 IU, 2×360 tabs = $8.99** — legitimately the cheapest D3 in Canada (~1.2¢/tablet).

### 🔥 Life Brand (Shoppers) + Equate (Walmart) — same factory
- Both appeared in the **July 2024 metal-fibres recall run through Vita Health Products** — the same Winnipeg line as Kirkland. Recall list also included Option+, Wellness by London Drugs and more.
- **Costco's, Walmart's and Shoppers' "competing" store brands can come off the same contract line.**
- ⚠️ Blanket "made by Vita Health" is verified **only for the recalled lots** — phrase it that way.
- Equate Vitamin D 1000 IU × 300 = $7.34; 180 chewables $5.78.

### Centrum (Haleon, ex-GSK)
- Canadian SKUs NPN-licensed (Select Essentials 50+ NPN 80037019).
- **US label** (NIH DSLD) shows: **magnesium oxide, cyanocobalamin, folic acid, dl-alpha-tocopheryl acetate**, plus **titanium dioxide, Yellow 6 Lake, BHT**.
- 🚨 **Canadian ingredient list NOT verified — buy a Canadian bottle and read it on camera before asserting any of this for Canada.**

### Materna (Nestlé) — the prenatal
- **Owned by Nestlé.** "#1 doctor recommended prenatal," SOGC-recognized.
- Uses **folic acid (600 mcg)** and **cyanocobalamin (2.8 mcg)** — the synthetic/cheapest forms.
- ⚠️ FAIRNESS: for neural-tube-defect prevention, **folic acid is the form with the outcome evidence** — decode, don't scare. Canada's guideline is 400 mcg folic acid for anyone who could become pregnant.

### One A Day (Bayer)
- 🔥 Canadian One A Day **gummies direct a dose of TWO gummies daily** (14+). **The brand name is not the dose.**

### Flintstones (Bayer)
- Chewables sweetened with **sorbitol, fructose and sucralose**. Candy-shaped format; the sharper story is the child-appeal format + the iron-warning class, not sugar grams.

### Vitafusion / L'il Critters (Church & Dwight)
- ~2–3 g sugar per 2-gummy serving.
- **April 2021 Canadian recall**: certain lots of L'il Critters Gummy Vites, vitafusion MultiVites and Fibre Well for **25 mm stainless-steel wire fragments** from a failed filter screen. National distribution.

### Olly / SmartyPants — Unilever. Sisu — US-owned since 2005 (NBTY, US$5.7M; not in the Nestlé-Bountiful deal). "Indie wellness" aesthetic, multinational owners.

### Webber Naturals (WN Pharmaceuticals, Coquitlam BC)

- ⚠️ Caveat: Webber lots also appeared in the July 2024 metal-fibres recall list.

### Natural Factors (Factors Group, BC)
- Entire line **ISURA-certified** (Canadian not-for-profit lab, mass-spec, 800+ contaminants) — but ISURA operates in **close partnership with Factors Group itself**. Disclose the affiliation. Factors Group also had a 2024 metal-fibres lot recall.

### Pure Encapsulations / Genestra / Douglas Labs

## 2. REGULATION (brands-agent findings; deep-dive tracked in Part 2)

- **NHP Regulations SOR/2003-196** (in force Jan 1 2004). Every legal NHP carries an **8-digit NPN** (or DIN-HM for homeopathics). **No NPN = unlicensed = illegal to sell in Canada.** Amazon.ca began purging no-NPN listings Oct 2018; unlicensed imports persist.
- **Mandatory label elements** (Part 5): NPN, medicinal ingredients by proper name + quantity per dose, licensed use claim, risk information, non-medicinals, lot, expiry, licence holder.
- **2022 labelling amendments**: standardized **Product Facts table**; after March 2025 ministerial exemption orders, **compliance deadline is June 21 2028**.
- **What an NPN doesn't mean**: monograph attestation lets companies license by checkbox; traditional-use claims license on history-of-use references. McGill OSS: an NPN *"does not mean that the specific product has been shown to 'maintain, restore or improve health'."*
- **The exact permitted claim wording** (Health Canada Multi-Vitamin/Mineral Monograph, June 2026): *"Helps to maintain/support immune function/the immune system"* — pre-approved for vitamins A, C, D, B12, zinc at minimum doses, **no product-specific trial required**. Also *"Maintains/supports good health."*
- **Monograph-mandated warnings**: iron >250 mg/package → octagon + *"There is enough iron in this package to seriously harm a child"*; **B6 ≥10 mg/day → mandatory numbness/tingling stop-use warning** (Health Canada Summary Safety Review — possible neuropathy at doses as low as 10 mg/day); Mg >350 mg/day → diarrhea advisory; beta-carotene >6,000 μg → smoker warning.
- **Titanium dioxide — GET THIS RIGHT**: Health Canada's June 2022 State of the Science: *"no conclusive scientific evidence that the food additive TiO₂ is a concern for human health"* — **explicitly diverging from the EU ban** (precaution-driven). TiO₂ in Canadian tablets is legal and reviewed. Honest angle: "the EU banned it as a precaution; Canada reviewed the same science and kept it."
- **No legal definition**: "third-party tested," "lab tested," "pharmaceutical grade," "pure." Auditable marks: USP Verified (mostly US SKUs), NSF/ANSI 173, NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport/Choice, ISURA (with affiliation disclosure).

### 2021 OAG audit (exact figures)
- **"88% of our sampled natural health products were advertised with misleading product information, and 56% with misleading label information"** (75 licensed products sampled).
- Health Canada inspected **~6% of 766 active licensed companies** (2017–2019); regulatory action at nearly half of 46 sites inspected.
- **91,000+ licences** issued since 2004.
- At audit time, Health Canada **could not order a label change or a mandatory recall of an NHP.**

### The fix and the fight
- **Bill C-47 (June 22 2023, ss. 500–504)** extended **Vanessa's Law** to NHPs: mandatory recalls, label-change orders, larger fines.
- **Bill C-368** (Dec 5 2023) seeks to repeal those powers; industry "Save our Supplements" campaign; proposed cost-recovery fees (up to $58,332 for Novel Class III) fuelled backlash.

### Nutrient-form science (decode segments)
| Form on cheap labels | Better form | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium **oxide** | glycinate/citrate | **~4% fractional absorption** (Firoz & Graber 2001) |
| Vitamin **D2** | D3 | D3 raises 25(OH)D **~40% more** (Tripkovic 2012 + 2023 meta) |
| **dl**-alpha-tocopherol | d-alpha | synthetic has **half the biological activity** (official 2:1); both appear as just "Vitamin E" |
| Ferrous **sulfate** | bisglycinate | sulfate ~**doubles odds of GI side effects** vs placebo (Tolkien 2015) |
| Folic acid | L-5-MTHF | 5-MTHF bypasses MTHFR — BUT for pregnancy the NTD evidence is folic acid; Canada's guideline is 400 mcg folic acid |
| Cyanocobalamin | methylcobalamin | conversion step; methyl sold by Webber at mass prices |

### Gummy problem (frame as "same brands, US testing")
- **ConsumerLab 2023: ~30% of multis failed testing; all four gummy multis tested had potency inaccuracies** — three near **double** the labelled folic acid (approaching the UL). Makers add 20–50% **overages** to outrun degradation. Gummy shelf life 1–2 yrs vs 3–5 for tablets.

## 3. STRONGEST REVEALS — ranked
1. **The 88% stat** — Canada's own commissioner: 88% of sampled NHPs advertised with misleading info, 56% misleading labels; 91,000+ licences; ~6% of companies inspected.
2. **Kirkland, Equate and Life Brand can come off the same Winnipeg line** (July 2024 metal-fibres recall, Vita Health named).
3. **That factory is foreign-owned** — IVC (US), parent Aland (China).
4. **It happened again May 2026** — Kirkland Women 50+ recalled for metal fragments.
5. **Until 2023, Health Canada legally could not force a vitamin recall** — and a bill to repeal the new power advanced in Parliament.
6. **"Helps support immune function" is copy-paste government wording** — monograph attestation, no product trial.
7. **An NPN doesn't license the ad** — NuvoCare fined $100K with NPN-licensed products.
8. **Magnesium oxide ≈ 4% absorbed** — first-listed in Jamieson's base magnesium.
9. **"One A Day" gummies = two a day** on the Canadian directions.
10. **B6 ≥10 mg carries a mandatory nerve-damage warning in Canada** — many B-complexes exceed it.
11. **Every gummy multi tested failed potency** (ConsumerLab 2023, US) — deliberate overages.
12. **"Third-party tested"/"pharmaceutical grade" have no legal definition.**
13. **"Practitioner-grade" is Big Food** — Nestlé owns Pure Encapsulations AND Materna; Unilever owns Olly + SmartyPants; Sisu US-owned since 2005.
14. **AG1 ≈ CA$120+/month vs $8.99 for two years of Costco D3** — and McGill says you probably don't need it.
15. **Canada's own dietary guidelines: most people don't need a multivitamin.** Vitamin D 400 IU for 51+; 400 mcg folic acid if you could become pregnant; B12 for older adults. Canada legally fortifies milk with D (levels ~doubled effective Jan 1 2026). StatCan: **45.6%** of Canadians take supplements.

## WINNERS (defensible picks)
1. **Kirkland Signature D3 1000 IU** — $8.99/720; right form; the one supplement Health Canada actually tells adults 51+ to take. Caveat the recall history as lot-specific.
2. **Webber Naturals B12 Methylcobalamin 1000 mcg** (~$14, Canadian-owned) or **CanPrev Magnesium Bis-Glycinate 200**.
3. **Thorne Basic Nutrients 2/Day** — NSF contents-certified, ships to Canada — for people who insist on a multi.
+ Mandatory honest caveat: per Canada's Dietary Guidelines, a varied diet plus vitamin D is all most healthy adults need.
