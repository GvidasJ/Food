Before number one, four things to check on every carton. One, the grade and the size. Under the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations, the grade name goes on the top of the carton, and for Canada A it sits inside the outline of a maple leaf. Canada A sets out requirements for the shell, the yolk and the air cell at grading, and Large means each egg weighs at least fifty-six grams. Every carton in this video says Canada A. So the grade is the ticket in, not a ranking between cartons. And that maple leaf is a grade mark. Imported eggs graded by a Canadian licence holder carry the same Canada grade name, and they have to say Product of, followed by their country. Two, the housing words, or the lack of them. Three, the name the law requires: the person by or for whom the eggs were packaged or labelled. On a store brand, that can be the store. Four, price divided by count. Cents per egg. Hold on to that number, because it's how we'll compare everything that follows.

How to protect yourself. Four steps, about ten seconds at the shelf. First, read the grade, then look for the housing word. Canada A in the maple leaf has to be on top, and it's the floor, not a ranking. Then find the housing word: none, enriched or colony or furnished, free run, free range, or organic. If there's no word, the lid hasn't told you, so don't fill the gap with a guess. Second, find who the carton names. The regulations require the name and principal place of business of the person by or for whom the eggs were packaged or labelled. On a store brand, that's often the store. Imported eggs have to say Product of, and then the country, on the top or side of the carton. Statistics Canada's trade data shows Canada imported about forty-three point six million dozen hen shell eggs in 2025, effectively all from the United States, so that line is worth a glance. Third, know which words have rulebooks. Organic is certified under a federal regime, and the label has to name the certifier. Free run and free range are defined by Egg Farmers of Canada and the national code, not by the CFIA pages we read. Fresh and natural, in the CFIA's own position, describe all eggs. The Egg Quality Assurance mark, Egg Farmers of Canada says, identifies made-in-Canada eggs from regulated farms, and imported eggs can't carry it. Fourth, divide price by count, and compare inside the same housing word before you compare across words. Three ninety-three over twelve is thirty-two point eight cents. Seventeen twenty-five over thirty is fifty-seven point five.

- Any statement of how hens behind a no-housing-word carton are housed. "Silence means cages" is not supported.
- Advocacy scorecards and the retailer cage-free percentages that come only from them (Costco 22.6%, Walmart Canada 9%, Metro 13.7% as found in snippets). Mercy For Animals, Animal Justice, W5 footage.

- A grading-station number decoded from a carton; "the grader's name is in the fine print" as a rule.
- Market-share claims ("biggest egg company", "two companies pack most eggs"). A date for the P&H transaction beyond the company's own words.
- Burnbrae "since 1893" (lid) vs 1891 (company site): say 1891 only as the company's own founding year, attributed, or say neither.
