The jam aisle in a Canadian grocery store is one of the hardest stretches of shelf space in the building to read, because the front of the jar and the back of the jar tell two different stories. Gingham lids that suggest a grandmother. Orchard drawings that suggest a farm. Handwriting fonts that suggest a small batch. Canada's food regulations say a jar can only be called simply Jam, with no qualifier, if it contains at least forty five percent of the named fruit. There is a second standard right beside it that lets a jar be called jam with pectin at far less fruit. Seven brands on the avoid list today, two that earned genuine worth it placements. Every jar on the avoid list is strawberry. Every price was pulled from a named store or the retailer's own listing on the twenty sixth of September twenty twenty six. The list runs from the brand we found on the fewest shelves to the one on the most, counting stores and different jars across ten Loblaw stores in five cities, Walmart, Costco and Sobeys online. Number one is the brand with the most jars on the shelf. Don't skip ahead.

Before the first brand, here is the yardstick you will use seven times. Four checks. Three are printed on the jar, most of them in the smallest type on it, and the fourth is on the shelf tag. Check one is the common name. Canadian law makes the manufacturer print what the food legally is on the front panel, and in this aisle four answers matter. Jam means at least forty five percent of the named fruit under section ten point four point one of the Canadian Food Compositional Standards. Jam with pectin, section ten point four point two, means at least twenty seven percent fruit. Preserve is its own standard, forty five parts fruit to fifty five parts sweetener on a dry basis. And spread, or fruit spread, has no standard at all. Check two is the first three words of the ingredient list. Under the Food and Drug Regulations, ingredients are listed in descending order by weight as they went into the batch. And when a recipe contains sugars, one or several, they have to be grouped in brackets after the single word Sugars, with that word placed in the list by their combined weight. A jar whose only sugar is plain sugar skips the bracket. So when you see Sugars, bracket, and then strawberries, the sugars together outweigh the fruit. Check three is the origin line. Check four is the price divided by the millilitres. Every price you hear came from ten Loblaw stores in five cities, Walmart, Costco and Sobeys online, and that week's flyers, all on one day, so re-check your own shelf.
