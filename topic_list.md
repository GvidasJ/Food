## The 30 items (popularity-descending order)
Facts below are the verified safe phrasings; full sources and binding caveats in verification_log.md.

### Tier 1 — universal weekly basket (1–10)
1. **Sliced bread** — "whole wheat" flour may have up to 5% of the kernel (mostly germ) removed; whole wheat ≠ whole grain (Health Canada). *Most universal grocery item in Canada.*
2. **Butter** — 2021 Buttergate: harder butter blamed on palm-derived feed supplements; Dairy Farmers of Canada panel found evidence inconclusive — framed as controversy (Globe and Mail / RealAgriculture). *Supply-managed fridge staple.*
3. **Eggs** — "free run" = in-barn only; "free range" = weather-permitting outdoor access, no single legal definition; brown vs white = breed (BC Egg / BC SPCA). *Near-universal weekly.*
4. **Processed cheese slices** — Kraft Singles don't meet Canada's cheese standard; sold as "Process Cheese Product," first ingredient modified milk ingredients (Kraft Heinz Canada label). *Lunchbox fixture.*
5. **Yogurt** — flavoured/fruit-bottom yogurts ~10–13 g sugar per 100 g; gelatin/starch/modified milk ingredients common (Canadian labels + surveys). *Weekly dairy staple; channel's proven topic.*
6. **Chicken breast** — "seasoned" chicken legally injected with water-salt-phosphate solution; disclosure in fine print (CFIA). *Most-purchased meat.*
7. **Bacon** — wet-cured with brine injection; some brands use liquid smoke — label tell: "smoke flavour" vs "naturally hardwood smoked." *Weekend-breakfast staple; 158K prior hit.*
8. **Deli meats** — "no added nitrites (except celery extract)" still means nitrites — chemically identical curing agent (CFIA). *Weekly sandwich staple.*
9. **Ketchup** — Heinz left Leamington 2014; French's Leamington-tomato ketchup sparked Buy-Canadian wave; Kraft Heinz returned production to Montréal 2021 (CBC / Globe and Mail). *Pantry universal; Buy-Canadian anchor.*
10. **Peanut butter** — Kraft smooth lists added sugars + soybean/hydrogenated oils; natural versions are peanuts only (Kraft Heinz Canada label). *Breakfast staple.*

### Tier 2 — near-weekly pantry & beverages (11–20)
11. **Table syrup** — Pearl Milling Company Original Syrup contains zero maple: glucose, glucose-fructose, colour, flavour (Superstore label). *Pancake-aisle default in the country that makes real maple syrup.*
12. **Honey** — CFIA 2018–19: 22% of 240 targeted imported samples adulterated; 100% of Canadian-origin honey authentic (CFIA / CBC). *Pantry staple; buy-Canadian payoff.*
13. **Breakfast cereal** — "made with whole grains" has no minimum %; berry imagery often = flavoured bits, not fruit (Harvard Health / labels). *Weekly breakfast staple.*
14. **Apple juice** — most is from imported concentrate (~30M litres from China in one year, CBC Marketplace 2018); reconstitution = "substantial transformation," so no origin statement required. *Family staple; labeling-arc proof point #1.*
   — **CTA placement: immediately before item 15 —**
15. **Orange juice** — "not from concentrate" is deaerated, tank-stored, re-flavoured with unlabelled orange-derived flavour packs ("Squeezed" / ABC News). *Premium-juice illusion.*
16. **Ground coffee** — documented shrinkflation: Maxwell House 34.5→30.6 oz (2012), Folgers 51→43.5 oz; the one-pound tin is long gone (Consumer World / CBC). *Bought every few weeks for decades — the reveal lands.*
17. **Bottled water** — Dasani and Aquafina are purified municipal water; Aquafina labels say "Public Water Source" since 2007 (CBC). *Routine multipack purchase.*
18. **"Olive oil" margarine** — Becel with Olive Oil: 68% canola, 6% olive oil, per its own label (Metro listing). *Health-marketed spread aimed at this exact audience.*
19. **Olive oil** — CFIA targeted testing: 76% satisfactory 2023–24, 67% 2024–25; UC Davis 2010: 69% of imported "extra virgin" failed standards (CFIA reports / UC Davis). *Monthly pantry staple; current Canadian government data.*
20. **Canned "San Marzano" tomatoes** — consortium's former head estimated at most ~5% sold in North America are certified genuine; echoed by 2022 RAI investigation — attributed estimate (TASTE / Gustiamo). *Premium-Italian pantry angle.*

### Tier 3 — common but occasional (21–27)
21. **Frozen whipped topping** — Cool Whip Original (Canada): water, glucose-fructose, 22% hydrogenated coconut/palm kernel oil — no cream (Superstore label). *Holiday dessert-table regular.*
22. **Frozen fish fillets** — Canadian-caught fish processed in China returns labelled "Product of China" — labels show last transformation, not catch origin (CBC 2025 / Oceana Canada). *Freezer staple; labeling-arc proof point #2, in reverse.*
23. **Atlantic salmon** — commercial wild Atlantic salmon fishery closed since 2000; essentially all store Atlantic salmon is farmed (Fisheries and Oceans Canada). *Top fresh-counter fish.*
24. **Imitation crab** — surimi: minced pollock + starch/sugar/flavour, little or no crab; must be labelled imitation (CFIA). *Budget salad/sushi regular.*
25. **Grated parmesan** — 2016 Bloomberg/FDA: "100% Parmesan" with zero parmesan; tubs at 7–9% cellulose; Canada permits ≤2% cellulose anti-caking (Food Safety News / Health Canada). *Long-lived pantry cheese.*
26. **Vanilla** — vast majority of vanilla flavour is synthetic vanillin — historically clove oil and wood-pulp lignin, today mostly petrochemical guaiacol. *Baking-cupboard item.*
27. **"Blueberry" bits** — in some muffin mixes/cereals: sugar, starch, oil, dye — little or no blueberry; anchored to ingredient labels. *Occasional bakery-aisle buy.*

### Tier 4 — niche closers (28–30)
28. **"Chocolatey" coatings** — Canadian standards require chocolate's fat to be cocoa butter; vegetable-fat versions can't use the name — hence "chocolatey"/"chocolate-flavoured" (CFIA). *The word-trick reveal.*
29. **Truffle oil** — most contains no truffle: oil + synthetic 2,4-dithiapentane (American Chemical Society). *Aspirational one-off.*
30. **Wasabi** — vast majority outside Japan is horseradish + mustard + green dye; tubes list horseradish first (byFood). *Most niche; light closer before the labeling-arc finale.*
