# Verification Log — "30 Fake Foods Canadians Buy Every Week"
Verified 2026-08-07. Each claim below is the SAFE phrasing that survived source-checking; caveats are binding on the script.

| # | Item | Verdict | Safe claim | Source |
|---|------|---------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Sliced bread | VERIFIED | Canada's Food and Drug Regulations let flour be called "whole wheat" with up to 5% of the kernel (mostly germ) removed; whole wheat ≠ whole grain — look for "whole grain whole wheat flour" | Health Canada, Whole Grains – Get the Facts (canada.ca) |
| 2 | Butter | PARTIAL | 2021 "Buttergate": harder butter widely blamed on palm-derived feed supplements; Dairy Farmers of Canada asked farmers to pause and convened a panel — which found no firm proof. State as controversy, not proven link | Globe and Mail (Buttergate); RealAgriculture (panel outcome) |
| 3 | Eggs | VERIFIED | "Free run" = loose in a barn, no outdoors; "free range" = outdoor access weather permitting (no single legal definition); brown vs white = breed, not nutrition | BC Egg, Egg Labels 101; BC SPCA |
| 4 | Processed cheese slices | VERIFIED | Kraft Singles in Canada don't meet the cheese compositional standard — sold as "Process Cheese Product," first ingredient modified milk ingredients (contains some real cheese lower down) | Kraft Heinz Canada product page |
| 5 | Yogurt | VERIFIED | Flavoured/fruit-bottom yogurts run ~10–13 g sugar per 100 g (~15–20 g per 175 g serving); many list modified milk ingredients, gelatin, pectin, or corn starch | Yoplait Canada labels; 2018 BMJ Open survey (UK — cite as "surveys") |
| 6 | Chicken breast | VERIFIED | "Seasoned" raw chicken may legally be injected/tumbled with water-salt-phosphate solution, adding retained water and sodium, with label disclosure. No fixed % — point to label/sodium line | CFIA meat and poultry labelling requirements |
| 7 | Bacon | PARTIAL | Mass-market bacon is wet-cured (brine-injected: water, salt, phosphates, nitrite); some brands use liquid smoke. Don't claim prevalence — tell viewers to compare "smoke flavour" vs "naturally hardwood smoked" on labels | Industry curing descriptions; label check |
| 8 | Deli meats | VERIFIED | "No added nitrites (except celery extract)" products still contain nitrites — cultured celery powder is a permitted source of preformed nitrites, chemically the same curing agent | CFIA preventive controls: nitrites in cured meat |
| 9 | Ketchup | VERIFIED | Heinz announced Leamington closure Nov 2013 (~740 jobs, exit Jun 2014); French's launched Leamington-tomato ketchup; Kraft Heinz announced return to Mont-Royal Nov 2020, production mid-2021 (initially with US tomatoes — don't imply all-Canadian from day one) | CBC (closure; French's); Globe and Mail (return); Radio-Canada (tomato sourcing) |
| 10 | Peanut butter | PARTIAL | Kraft smooth PB in Canada lists sugars (corn maltodextrin, sugar) + soybean and hydrogenated oils; Kraft All Natural = peanuts (or peanuts and salt). Don't say "icing sugar" — current label wording differs | Kraft Heinz Canada product page |
| 11 | Table syrup | PARTIAL | Pearl Milling Company (ex-Aunt Jemima) Original Syrup in Canada: glucose, glucose-fructose, water, cellulose gum, colour, flavour — zero maple. Do NOT include Rogers Golden Syrup (cane syrup, no maple claim) | Real Canadian Superstore listing |
| 12 | Honey | VERIFIED | CFIA 2018–19 enhanced surveillance: 22% of 240 targeted samples adulterated with foreign sugars (rice/corn/cane syrup); 100% of Canadian-origin samples authentic. Targeted sampling, not market-wide | CFIA honey authenticity report; CBC |
| 13 | Cereal | PARTIAL | "Made with whole grains" can appear with no minimum whole-grain %; berry imagery often ≠ real fruit. Anchor fake-berry claims to specific ingredient lists, say "some," not "most" | Harvard Health; NPR (2011, US — soften) |
| 14 | Apple juice | VERIFIED (reworded) | CBC Marketplace: most apple juice on Canadian shelves is from imported concentrate (~30M litres from China in one year, 2018 data); reconstitution = "substantial transformation," so no origin statement is required at all. CFIA: "Product of Canada" ≈ 98% Canadian; "Made in Canada" = last transformation here + qualifier if imported ingredients | CBC Marketplace (2018); CFIA origin-claims guidance |
| 15 | Orange juice | VERIFIED | "Not from concentrate" OJ is commonly deaerated, tank-stored for months, then re-flavoured with orange-derived flavour packs not on the label (Alissa Hamilton, "Squeezed") | Boston Globe Q&A (2009); ABC News (2011) — US industry-standard practice |
| 16 | Coffee | PARTIAL | Documented shrinkflation: Maxwell House 34.5→30.6 oz (2012), Folgers 51→43.5 oz — same brands sold in Canada; the old one-pound tin shrank industry-wide from the late 1970s. No Canadian 454→340 g citation found — use documented examples | Mouse Print / Consumer World; CBC shrinkflation |
| 17 | Bottled water | VERIFIED | Dasani and Aquafina are purified municipal water, not spring water; PepsiCo agreed in 2007 to print "Public Water Source" on Aquafina. Say "purified/filtered tap water" | CBC (2007); Snopes |
| 18 | Olive-oil margarine | VERIFIED | Becel with Olive Oil (Canada): 68% canola oil, 6% olive oil per its own label. Read the current tub on camera | Metro product listing |
| 19 | Olive oil | VERIFIED | CFIA targeted testing: 76% of olive oil samples satisfactory 2023–24, 67% in 2024–25 (targeted, not market-wide); UC Davis 2010 found 69% of imported "extra virgin" failed standards (old, US, partly sensory failures) | CFIA Food Fraud annual reports (via Western Producer); UC Davis Olive Center |
| 20 | San Marzano tomatoes | PARTIAL | Former DOP consortium head estimated at most ~5% of "San Marzano" sold in the US is certified genuine — consortium estimate (2011), echoed by 2022 RAI investigation; no Canadian figure; DOP not legally enforced in North America. Frame as "the consortium has estimated" | TASTE; Gustiamo/RAI |
| 21 | Whipped topping | VERIFIED | Cool Whip Original (Canada): water, glucose-fructose/sugar, 22% hydrogenated coconut and palm kernel oil — no cream (contains sodium caseinate, a milk derivative, so not dairy-free) | Real Canadian Superstore listing |
| 22 | Frozen fish fillets | VERIFIED | Canadian-caught fish can be processed in China and re-imported labelled "Product of China" — labels show country of last substantial transformation, not where it was caught. Say "a significant share," no tonnage | CBC (Mar 2025); Oceana Canada |
| 23 | Atlantic salmon | VERIFIED | Commercial wild Atlantic salmon fishery closed in Canada (moratorium since 2000) — essentially all store Atlantic salmon is farmed | Fisheries and Oceans Canada; Oceana Canada |
| 24 | Imitation crab | VERIFIED | Surimi: minced pollock/whitefish + starch, sugar, egg white, flavourings; little or no crab (some brands add trace crab extract); CFIA requires imitation labelling | CFIA fish labelling; Healthline |
| 25 | Grated parmesan | VERIFIED | 2016 Bloomberg/FDA: Castle Cheese "100% Parmesan" had no parmesan; retail brands tested 7–9% cellulose; Canada permits cellulose ≤2% as anti-caking in grated cheese. US findings — flag | Food Safety News; TIME; Health Canada |
| 26 | Vanilla | VERIFIED | Vast majority of vanilla flavouring is synthetic vanillin — historically from clove oil and wood-pulp lignin, today mostly petrochemical guaiacol; a tiny fraction of world vanillin comes from beans | Vanillin production literature |
| 27 | Blueberry bits | PARTIAL | "Blueberry" bits in some muffin mixes/cereals are sugar-starch-oil-dye constructions with little or no blueberry — anchor to specific ingredient lists on air (the 2011 "study" is weakly sourced) | NPR/Seattle Times (2011); product labels |
| 28 | "Chocolatey" coatings | VERIFIED | Canadian compositional standards: "chocolate" must get its fat from cocoa butter; vegetable-fat versions must use a non-standardized name (e.g., "candy") — hence "chocolatey" and "chocolate-flavoured" wording | CFIA confectionery labelling; Canadian Food Compositional Standards |
| 29 | Truffle oil | VERIFIED | Most truffle oil contains no truffle — oil + synthetic 2,4-dithiapentane (one aroma compound of real truffles). Say "most," not "all" | American Chemical Society; NATGA |
| 30 | Wasabi | VERIFIED | The vast majority of "wasabi" outside Japan is horseradish + mustard + green dye; real wasabi is rare and costly. No hard %, tube labels list horseradish first | byFood; Fine Dining Lovers |

## Connective arc (verified)
- CFIA origin labelling: "Product of Canada" = all/virtually all (~98%) Canadian; "Made in Canada" = last substantial transformation in Canada with qualifier "from domestic and imported ingredients"; imported-concentrate reconstitution and last-transformation rules create the loopholes used in items 14 and 22. Source: CFIA origin claims guidance.
