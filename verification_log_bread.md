## VERDICT KEY
- **VERIFIED** — reliable secondary source (major outlet; Wikipedia with corroboration; company's own materials for claims about itself)
- **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** — primary source (Justice Laws, Canada Gazette, CFIA/Health Canada, Competition Bureau, StatCan data pulled directly, company release)
- **ATTRIBUTED** — a company/organization/analyst claim, presented as their claim
- **DERIVED** — our arithmetic on verified inputs
- **UNVERIFIED** — not confirmed; must NOT appear in voiceover
- **EXCLUDED** — deliberately kept out (reason given)

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS (binding on the voiceover — items 1–6 are LEGAL-SAFETY, do not loosen)

Alleged: Metro, Sobeys, Walmart Canada, Giant Tiger, Maple Leaf — ALWAYS with "the Bureau continues to investigate… every one of those companies denies wrongdoing. None of them has been charged." 3. Bimbo v. Maple Leaf: present ONLY as live competing lawsuits ("suing… while Maple Leaf denies everything and is suing them back"). No side taken.
4. "$500 million — reported as the largest settlement of its kind" (attributed superlative). "$50 million — the Bureau's own words: 'the highest price-fixing fine imposed by a Canadian court to date'" (that one IS primary — quote exactly).

6. Galen Weston quotes verbatim, both of them ("This sort of behaviour is wrong…" and "On behalf of the Weston group of companies, we are sorry…").
7. Health Canada 5% rule must be quoted with the full sentence run INCLUDING "remains a nutritious choice" — we never leave the reassurance on the cutting-room floor (house rule: no health scare by omission).
8. "Whole wheat vs whole grain" is a definitions story, never a quality/health verdict: "We're not here to tell you it's good or bad — we're here to tell you what the words legally mean" stays in.
9. Ingredient lists read factually; no additive is characterized beyond its presence and legality. The reference video's E-number/chemistry-set framing is banned.
10. Recall segment keeps the "system working" frame: traced "before anyone reported an injury."
11. "Builds strong bodies 12 ways" only as "south of the border, the ads promised" — US ad history.
