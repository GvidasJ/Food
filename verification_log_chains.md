VERDICT KEY
- VERIFIED-PRIMARY — confirmed against a primary source (statutes on Justice/e-Laws, OPC report, SEC/SEDAR, StatCan, company's own releases/filings/pages, DCB/TCE, court-carried quotes)
- VERIFIED — reliable secondary (CBC, Globe, CTV, Bloomberg, trade press)
- ATTRIBUTED — presented as someone's statement/claim
- DERIVED — arithmetic on verified numbers; show math on screen
- UNVERIFIED — only with the exact hedge specified
- EXCLUDED — researched, deliberately kept out

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS (binding on voiceover — items 1–6 are non-negotiable legal guardrails)

1. CONSOLIDATION IS NOT WRONGDOING. Every acquisition in this episode is a disclosed, lawful, and where applicable regulator-approved transaction. Banned words for any deal: "caught," "scheme," "got away with," "loophole" (except the GST/tax segments where the law itself is the subject and no company is accused of anything). The 2014 takeover was "approved by Canada's Competition Bureau" — keep that clause.
2. THE APP FINDING is the only matter stated as a violation, because regulators formally found one. Quote the OPC's words ("as often as every few minutes," "well-founded"), state Tim Hortons' remediation AND the regulator's one-year-later satisfaction, and keep "a settlement is not an admission of wrongdoing" attached to the class-action beat. Never say "spied illegally" or "broke the law" beyond the regulators' own framing ("in violation of Canadian privacy laws" is the OPC release's phrase — usable as quoted).
3. SUBWAY–CBC, exact required frame: CBC "aired a report… based on lab DNA testing"; "Subway disputed the testing and sued"; dismissal was procedural, overturned on appeal, Supreme Court declined to intervene; "What no court has ever done — as far as the public record shows — is rule on whether the report was true or false. We're not going to do what the courts didn't." NEVER state the 50%-soy figures as fact, never say either side won on the merits, never call the report "debunked" or "confirmed."
4. FRANCHISEE LITIGATION: the Always Fresh class action was DISMISSED and upheld on appeal — always paired with the judge's reasoning. The 2017–19 ad-fund class actions are NOT in the script; if ever added: "allegations… denied… settled, nothing proven, not an admission." The US and Quebec franchisee suits are EXCLUDED entirely (outcomes unknown).
5. HORTON'S DEATH: only the TCE-sourced facts as scripted — crash, autopsy finding, counselling, his father's death, his age. No speed, no chase, no dramatization, no moralizing. This is a person with living family.
6. DOORDASH: "the Bureau has now sued DoorDash alleging… those are allegations DoorDash is contesting, and nothing there has been proven." All three clauses required. Cineplex: "penalty… upheld on appeal" is decided fact; the SCC leave attempt is "reported."
7. RBI HQ: only the two-part formulation — Canadian-incorporated/TSX-listed, but "RBI's own website says its principal executive offices are in Miami." Tax-inversion motives: excluded.
8. NO health commentary anywhere. Calories appear ONLY as disclosure law; the mandated menu sentence is quoted as a legal script, not as advice. None of the reference video's sanitation/outbreak content survives in any form.
9. Company-claimed stats stay attributed: "by the company's count" (McDonald's guests), "calls itself" (Boston Pizza #1 casual), "by its own count" (MTY, Foodtastic), "the company's own history page" (St-Hubert first-delivery claim), TCE's dated 8-of-10 QSR coffee stat is NOT in the script — if ever added, only the QSR-qualified, dated version.
10. Counts and prices are dated: store counts "as of" their source dates; franchise fees "as reported"; all CPI/coffee/StatCan figures re-pulled in edit week (Aug 2026 CPI releases mid-September — recompute the percentages and update VO if changed).
11. KFC: no first-city/first-year claim, ever. A&W: never "Canada's first fast-food chain"; the approved line is the drive-in framing + "eleven years before Canada saw a McDonald's."
12. Subway store count: "more than McDonald's, second only to Tim Hortons" — never "the most in Canada."
