# VERIFICATION LOG — "COSTCO CANADA JUST GOT CAUGHT..."

Script: script_costco.md | Research: research_costco_caught.md (Parts 1-3)
Research date: 15 September 2026. All prices "at time of research." All figures CAD unless marked US$.

TIER KEY
(a) ADMITTED / CONVICTED / SETTLED — company admission, own filing or terms, guilty plea, fine paid, court-approved settlement
(b) REGULATOR FINDING / ACTION — a government body did something on the record
(c) LIVE ALLEGATION / PROCEEDING — unproven; company's dispute line mandatory
(d) DOCUMENTED CONSUMER / MEDIA RECORD — complaints, investigations, price documentation. Never a finding.

---

## THE CONTROLLING CONSTRAINT ON THIS EPISODE

There is no Competition Bureau enforcement action, no CFIA penalty, no Measurement Canada penalty and no provincial consumer-protection prosecution against Costco Wholesale Canada Ltd. that could be verified. The title says "caught." The script defines what that means in the thesis, on purpose, in the third paragraph: caught by its own paperwork. Every countdown item is a document Costco wrote, a filing Costco signed, a court record, a federal regulator's database, Statistics Canada, or sworn-equivalent testimony to a House of Commons committee.

Do not let anyone in post add a line implying Costco was fined, charged, investigated or found liable in Canada. It was not.

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS — 18 lines that must air exactly as written

1. "Costco Wholesale Canada has not been fined by the Competition Bureau. It has not been penalised by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency. It has not been prosecuted by any provincial consumer regulator." — This is the defamation firewall for the whole episode. It cannot be cut for time.

2. "a company that got caught by its own paperwork" — the thesis. Defines the title.

3. On the class action: "This is an allegation. It has not been tested. The court has not certified it as a class action, no evidence has been heard, and no judge has found that Costco did anything wrong." — mandatory, verbatim, in the same breath as the allegation.

4. "a proposed class action was filed" — the word "proposed" must appear every time the case is referenced.

5. Costco's published defence must air in full: "products sold online may have different pricing than the same products sold at your local Costco warehouse. That's due to the shipping and handling fees charged for delivery to your home or business." Plus: "Costco's position is that the website and the warehouse are not the same store. A court will decide whether that holds. It hasn't yet."

6. "this is arithmetic, not an accusation" — before the Executive 2% break-even math.

7. "That's not Costco being sneaky. That's Canadian law leaving a door open. But Costco walks through it every single time." — the food-labelling item collapses without this.

8. "The reason is not a scandal. It's the law." — same item, earlier.

9. "That's our calculation, from Costco's own filed and audited numbers" — attached to the Canada-vs-US margin figure. It is our arithmetic on audited segment data, not a Costco statement.

10. "Those are blended US and Canada figures in US dollars, and Costco doesn't break out Canada, so I won't pretend it did." — mandatory before any wage or renewal number.

11. "That's an absence, not an accusation. But we looked." — the buy-Canadian labelling item.

12. "Nobody found Costco did anything wrong. Costco just didn't sign until the federal government said the word mandatory out loud." — the Grocery Code item. The code is voluntary and governs grocer-supplier relations, not consumer prices.

13. "Costco is not on that list." — the CFIA maple-washing item. Costco was NOT among the five penalised businesses. This is a credibility win and must be stated plainly.

14. "Both of those are true." — the close. The episode is not a hit piece and must not land as one.

15. "The problem was never that Costco is the villain."

16. On shrinkflation: attribute every weight to the person who measured it — "Nathaniel Christopher is a Burnaby blogger and former journalist" / "Daily Hive reported ... on a Canadian shopper's photo comparison." Never present these as findings.

17. On the receipt check: Bryant must be identified as "then executive director of the Canadian Civil Liberties Association." He no longer holds the post.

18. On prices: "at time of research" must appear on the exchange-rate line and on the Narcity price table.
