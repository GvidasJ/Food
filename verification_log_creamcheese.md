# VERIFICATION LOG — CREAM CHEESE & CHEESE SPREADS EPISODE (post date: September 11, 2026)

Companion to script_creamcheese.md and research_creamcheese.md (Parts 1–3). Every scripted claim below carries a verdict. The MANDATORY PHRASINGS section is binding on the voiceover.

VERDICT KEY
- VERIFIED-PRIMARY — confirmed against a primary source (Justice Laws, CFIA, Health Canada, StatCan, SEC, CBSA, Global Affairs, company's own pages/filings/releases, DCB)
- VERIFIED — confirmed against reliable secondary sources (CBC, Globe, CTV, AP, Britannica, trade press)
- ATTRIBUTED — presented on air as someone's statement ("the company's own count", "food writers report")
- DERIVED — arithmetic performed on verified numbers; show the math on screen
- UNVERIFIED — do not state as fact; only with the exact hedge specified
- EXCLUDED — researched, deliberately kept out

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS (binding on voiceover)

1. NO health commentary anywhere. Gums, stabilizers, sorbic acid, modified milk ingredients, sodium and saturated fat appear ONLY as declared ingredients / printed values / regulatory arithmetic. Never "better/worse for you", never "clean" as a health judgment (the reference video's frame is banned).
2. December 2021: NEVER claim Canada had a shortage or empty shelves. Exact frame: "that was an American crisis… We found no reports of Canadian shelves running dry." The $20 offer: "US-only."
3. "Product" naming: present as legal mechanics, never as deception. Required line kept in script: "Kraft isn't hiding it — it's printed in plain sight." Never say any company mislabels or breaks the law.
4. Store-brand packers: never state or imply who packs any store brand. Keep verbatim: "a fair question to ask on camera, and an unfair thing to claim without proof, so we won't."
5. Cheez Whiz: NEVER assert whether it meets or fails any compositional standard, and never state its legal category from the website title. On-camera action: read the jar's printed common name. The US-vs-Canada recipe comparison is ATTRIBUTED to food writers (Tasting Table), not our lab.
6. FOP: basis is "per serving of stated size or per reference amount, whichever is greater" — at RA 30 g say "per thirty-gram serving." Cheddar "CAN pass" the calcium test (varies by product). Frame symbols as "watch new stock" (older stock legally sells through).
7. Market share: NO Canadian percentage exists for Philadelphia — never quote 66% or 69% (US figures). Dominance is carried by BrandSpark (cite the survey) + the attributed Strategy quote.
8. Western Family / Great Value label wording: conditional on reading the physical pack — if the pack differs, drop the beat.
9. Liberté: all quotes (St-Viateur manager, Kat Romanow, General Mills statement) must be re-pulled manually from the CBC/CTV articles before captioning. Attribute the historian by name only if the article confirms the wording.
10. Western Creamery: "reported discontinued / we couldn't find it on shelves" — never a bare "discontinued" as our own finding, and never blame Gay Lea; no cause is on record.
11. MacLaren's: only the DCB hedge — "evidently one of the first soft, processed cheeses to be commercially distributed." Never "Canada's first branded cheese." The two-names conflict is presented as a puzzle the physical tub referees.
12. Philadelphia origin: "early 1870s"; the name story as marketing fact (well-sourced); Lawrence's "happy accident" framing only as "as the story goes" if used at all (currently not scripted).
13. Angel campaign: "one of the most successful advertising campaigns in Canadian history" is The Canadian Encyclopedia's line — attribute if challenged.
14. Kraft Heinz split: "announced… paused" only. Do not quote the Reuters/KFGO Cahillane line without verifying against the Reuters original.
15. Tre Stelle: the product-name explanation stays as two texts side by side ("skim milk is not on that list") — never assert Arla's internal reasoning or compliance calculus. Required takeaway kept in script: "product doesn't mean worse, and cream cheese doesn't mean simpler."
16. Blue Cow: certification claim quoted from DFC. NEVER state Philadelphia lacks the logo (database absence ≠ pack absence) — hunt on camera.
17. Recalls: none are scripted (see EXCLUDED). If any recall is ever mentioned: "a routine posted recall — corrective action, no wrongdoing alleged, no charges." Nothing in this episode alleges any offence by any company.
18. All prices: "at time of research" / "when we checked" + retailer name; re-verify every price on filming day.
