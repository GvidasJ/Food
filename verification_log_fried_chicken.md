# VERIFICATION LOG — 5 Chicken Chains in Canada That Got Too Greedy and Paid the Price

Script: script_fried_chicken.md. Research: research_fried_chicken.md (ten dossiers, 28 Sep 2026). Date of log: 29 Sep 2026.

## 1 Method

First pass: workflow of 11 adversarial agents (8 section verifiers checking every claim against the dossiers and, where the dossier said so, the live URL; 3 cross-cutting lenses: house rules/libel, arithmetic and structure, hostile-viewer claims). Result: 615 claims checked, 151 flags (hard-error 7, unsupported 28, label 82, style 34). Every flag was applied, rewritten or deliberately rejected as listed in section 3. A second automated pass was proposed and declined; the revised body was read through once by hand (section 4).

## 2 Roster decision

Gate: a chain holds a slot only with at least three dated events at tier (a)/(b) and one quotable company statement. Result: Popeyes STRONG; Swiss Chalet and St-Hubert (Recipe) STRONG; KFC WEAK but qualifying (two consecutive quarterly system-sales declines in Yum 8-Ks, pending Reddick application, inflation-framed value moves); Dixie Lee WEAK but qualifying (2007–2008 SEC filings, 2023 supply failure, 2026 locator vs homepage claim); Church's, Nando's WEAK and not used; Mary Brown's, Chick-fil-A, Wingstop, Jollibee NONE (growing). Count therefore five, not seven. Order: ascending Canadian units (Dixie Lee 4 on the Ontario franchisor's locator; St-Hubert 120+; Swiss Chalet ~195–200; Popeyes 380 per RBI spokesperson Jul 2025; KFC 650+). Title drops 'Fried' because two chains are rotisserie.
