# Verification Log — Frozen Fries script
Verified 2026-08-11. Binding on script_frozen_fries.md.

## The spine (strongest material)
| Claim | Verdict | Safe phrasing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canada has a legal recipe for frozen fries | VERIFIED | CFIA Canadian Standards of Identity, Vol. 4, s.56: "French Fried Potatoes" = mature sound potatoes cleaned, sorted, trimmed, cut into strips, blanched, processed in vegetable oil; may contain ONLY (a) sodium acid pyrophosphate (b) dextrose (c) ascorbic acid (d) food colours (e) asparaginase | inspection.canada.ca — Standards of Identity Vol. 4 |
| Coated fries fall outside the standard | VERIFIED (frame carefully) | No flour/starch/batter on the permitted list, which is why coated products are sold as "Superfries Extra Crispy"/"FlavourCrisp" rather than under the prescribed name. Do NOT claim "CFIA requires the word battered" | CFIA common-name guidance |
| Frozen fries are par-fried before freezing | VERIFIED | The standard's own definition says "processed in vegetable oil" — oven fries were already cooked in oil at the factory. Fair caveat included: baked at home is still lower fat than deep-fried | Same standard |
| Phosphate class-name rule | VERIFIED | SAPP may be declared as "sodium phosphate(s)" — explains why McCain's two straight-cut SKUs read differently. Not two different chemicals | CFIA food additives labelling, ch.6 |
| SAPP function | VERIFIED | Binds the iron naturally in potatoes so cooked fries don't go grey; labels say "(to retain natural colour)" | Canadian labels + standard |
| Dextrose function | PARTIAL | "A sugar; it's there so fries brown evenly, not for sweetness." No percentages, no "dextrose dip" process claims (industry sources only) | Standard (permitted); potatopro (function) |
| Colours | VERIFIED | Annatto and caramel both on Health Canada's List of Permitted Colouring Agents | canada.ca colouring agents list |
| Product of Canada vs Made in Canada | VERIFIED | POC ≈98% Canadian; MIC = last substantial transformation + mandatory qualifier | Competition Bureau / CFIA |

## Company facts
- McCain: founded 1957 by Harrison, Wallace, Robert and Andrew McCain, Florenceville NB; privately held family company; world's largest frozen fry maker; 160+ countries. "One in four fries" is McCain's OWN marketing figure — attributed on air as the company's claim. Avoid "harmonious family business" framing (second-generation litigation reported). Florenceville-Bristol = "French Fry Capital of the World" (trademarked, per regional tourism site); note it now sits within the District of Carleton North after 2023 municipal reform — script says "the town of Florenceville-Bristol" (safe).
- Cavendish Farms: part of J.D. Irving; HQ Dieppe, New Brunswick (NOT PEI); PEI plant acquired 1980 (former C.M. McLean); plants New Annan PEI, Lethbridge AB ($430M, 2019), Wheatley ON, Jamestown North Dakota. "Canadian-owned" is accurate; "100% Canadian production" would be wrong — script discloses the US plant.
- McCain Coaldale, Alberta expansion: $600M CAD announced 2023 (the $438M figure circulating is USD). Script says "announced in 2023," not "recently."

## Industry
- ~5.8M tonnes grown annually; ~68% to processing; top provinces Alberta 24%, Manitoba 23%, PEI 20%, New Brunswick 12%. ~$2.7B fry exports (91% to US); ~$126M frozen fries imported. Script does NOT claim all Canadian fries use Canadian potatoes.
- PEI potato wart: detections Oct 2021 → US export suspension Nov 2021; fresh resumed spring 2022; seed potato exports still restricted; May 2026 US industry push to reinstate. TIME-SENSITIVE — script tells viewers to check current status; re-verify before publishing.

## Claims killed
- Any President's Choice or Great Value ingredient recitation (unverified / US-only data).
- "Most/all frozen fries in Canada are Canadian potatoes" (imports exist; Cavendish label says domestic AND imported).
- Acrylamide/health framing from the UK source (not verified this pass + channel no-fear rule).
- "1 in 3 fries" (McCain's own figure is 1 in 4).
- Palm oil claim from UK source — Canadian labels verified here declare canola/soybean, not palm.
- Beef dripping premium-fry entry (UK-only product category).
