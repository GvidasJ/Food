## PART B — MANDATORY ON-AIR PHRASINGS

1. **The targeted-sampling caveat must be spoken every single time the 19% figure is spoken.** It is in the cold open for that reason. This is the difference between accurate and actionable, and it is the single most important line in the script. Do not cut it for pace, do not move it later, do not paraphrase it into something softer.
2. **Caption the Hiemstra clip with its date, 25 July 2019, on screen.** It is seven years old and the script's whole cold-open structure depends on the audience knowing that.
3. **Caption the National Honey Board as a United States industry body, not a regulator.** It is the best-documented debunk available and it is not Canadian.
4. **Say "that division is ours, not StatCan's"** when the per-pound figures appear, and put the derivation on screen.
5. **Every company claim must be attributed as a company claim** — Doyon's "all-Canadian," Billy Bee's provinces, Bee Maid's testing policy. The script does this. Keep the attributions in.
6. **"Unsatisfactory" is a CFIA sampling classification, not a verdict on a company.** Never gloss it as "fake" or "failed inspection."
7. **Foreign ownership is not wrongdoing.** The script says this out loud in the Billy Bee beat. Keep that sentence — it is what separates the segment from an accusation.
8. **"Declined to comment" is not evidence of anything.** If any version of the CBF contract story is restored, this guard must come with it.
9. **Absences must be spoken as absences.** "I could not find an origin declaration in Sobeys' own text" is true. "Compliments has no origin declaration" is not established.
10. **Never state or imply that any named brand sells adulterated honey.** No brand in this video has any Canadian regulatory finding against it for authenticity. Not one.

## PART D — DO NOT USE

1. **"The CFIA tested them — 19% failed" as a title or as a line.** CFIA named no brand anywhere in the report. This was the working title and it was dropped for exactly this reason.
2. **Who packs any retailer's private-label honey.** None of the five retailers disclose it. The Kirkland–Bee Maid attribution is consumer-press only and both companies decline to confirm supplier relationships.
3. **That Billy Bee is owned by a British company.** It is McCormick & Company, Maryland. The 76K competitor says Ragus; that is wrong and the script corrects it.
4. **That Billy Bee has faced honey-authenticity class action litigation.** Asserted by the 76K competitor. No such action could be verified.
5. **"The majority of honey sold in Canadian grocery stores is imported."** Unconfirmable. What is documented is that most *imported* honey goes into manufacturing rather than retail jars.
6. **The US federal conspiracy case involving a Canadian honey trader.** Claims dismissed; the court found the specific adulteration allegations meritless and dismissed them with prejudice as to the Canadian defendant. Naming it without that is the most legally dangerous thing available on this topic. Omitted entirely.
7. **The April 2004 nitrofuran recall, beyond a bare mention.** Cut from the script deliberately: residue, not authenticity, and the highest health-framing risk in the dossier. Documented in research_honey.md §9.2 if a commenter raises it. **If restored, state the action and stop. Do not explain the substance.**
8. **The August 2026 Canadian Beekeepers Federation contract-cancellation claim.** Cut. Unverified, names no company, and the Canadian Honey Council chair denies it outright.
9. **"85% of the honey McCormick Canada uses comes from Canada."** Pre-2017 figure, superseded by the all-Canadian switch. Using it as current would be false.
10. **Any "Natural Honey Farms" / Chinese-origin Billy Bee sub-brand claim.** Sole source is a beekeeping forum. Unverifiable and pre-2017.
11. **The Kirkland 750g "honey (origin Canada), Sugar" ingredient record.** Almost certainly a crowdsourced data-entry error; Costco's own page says 100% pure. Do not use without a photograph of the panel.
12. **"Rowena's" honey.** Does not appear to exist as a Canadian brand. Probably a garbling of Rowse (UK, Amazon.ca only). Not in the script.
13. **"Beaverdale" honey.** Not found. Searches return a golf club and a vet clinic.
14. **Any home test presented as working** — including the CBC clip of a beekeeper demonstrating an aisle flow test. Even careful outlets have propagated these.
15. **Pollen-count logic as an adulteration test.** CFIA does not use pollen analysis; refined syrups contain no pollen, and ultrafiltration strips it. Absence of pollen proves nothing.
16. **Alberta Beekeepers Commission's claim that a Canadian/import blend can be labelled "Canada #1."** Contradicted by Grade Compendium Vol. 6 s.2. The substance of their critique stands; the specific wording does not. Use the Compendium.
17. **The ABC brief's "0.002%, not 22%" arithmetic.** Invalid comparison — seizure tonnage against a sample failure rate.
18. **"Beekeepers are disappearing."** Headcount rose from 11,994 to 16,360 in five years. A commenter will catch this.
19. **Any of the ~90 SEO, content-farm and commerce domains listed in research_honey.md Part 1 §10 and Part 2 §7.2.** Several confidently assert honey packer identities with no sourcing at all. That is precisely the trap the reference video fell into.
20. **The MPY Trading Ltd. $1,155,685 fine.** It is the only named prosecution in the 2024-25 report and it is about **crab**, not honey. Do not let it drift anywhere near the honey segment.

## PART E — CHANNEL CONTEXT WORTH KNOWING BEFORE RECORDING

This is a **redo**, not a new topic. Canadian Counter published "7 Honey Brands Sold in Canada You MUST AVOID" on 26 April 2026. It did **1,414 views**. It used "deadliest" framing, and the **top comment on it is a viewer mocking that framing** — three likes, and the entire comment is the word "deadliest" followed by laughing emoji. A second commenter asked "How is this 'DEADLIEST'??"

The channel's own audience's most-liked response to its last honey video was ridicule of a health-scare frame. **The no-health-claims house rule is therefore not only a compliance rule on this topic — it is the clearest performance signal in the entire dossier.**

Format evidence, same market, same quarter (Algrow pull, 17-18 Sept 2026): "I Investigated 10 Honey Brands in Canada (Only 3 Are Actually Real Honey)" = 76,116. "10 Canadian Honey Brands Tested" = ~19,734. Every "FAKE / AVOID at All Costs" variant landed between roughly 290 and 3,250 — including this channel's own at 1,414. Both winners ran about 30 minutes, both used "I Investigated N," and both counted down to a good brand at number one. This script follows that shape.

Two audience demands the script answers directly, both taken from top comments on the 76K video: viewers who watched 30 minutes and still did not know what to buy ("SO WHICH ONE IS REAL?", 7 likes), and a viewer asking what True Source certification actually means ("it is true source certified, whatever that means", 3 likes). The good list is a signposted act rather than a coda for the first reason. **Consider adding one on-screen recap card at the good-list segment.**
