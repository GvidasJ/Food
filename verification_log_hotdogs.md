## MANDATORY PHRASINGS (binding on voiceover — do not paraphrase)
1. MSM: NEVER "pet food," never disgust framing, never a safety claim. The beat is legal taxonomy: "the law itself declines to call it meat" + "it's inspected, permitted, and everywhere" + "the law forces the honesty."
2. Nitrites/celery: NEVER a health claim. The beat is the legal cure requirement (≥100 ppm) + CFIA's "alternative source of nitrites" verbatim + CBC's expert quote WITH attribution. The CFIA claims-guidance passage does NOT name celery — always present the two documents "side by side" and "decide for yourself." Never say Maple Leaf or PC broke a rule.
3. Maple Leaf "never use" promise: state its precise scope ("it covers the Maple Leaf brand") — the Hygrade contrast is a brand-scope fact, not an accusation of hypocrisy or breach.
4. 2008: ALWAYS "ready-to-eat deli meats — not hot dogs, and that distinction matters." Deaths: "at least twenty-two" (or the 20/22/24 range) — never a flat single number. McCain quote verbatim. Tone = empathy/history, never scare. "Top PR move" phrasing BANNED — verified honour is CP Business Newsmaker of the Year 2008 (currently unused).
5. Sodium numbers: always per-wiener WITH gram size; cross-brand comparisons only with sizes stated; Kirkland's 1,230 mg must always carry "one-hundred-and-twenty-three gram quarter-pounder" + per-100g context.
6. FOP math: "Health Canada judges…" framing with the 55 g reference amount named; multiples presented as our math on the filmed label.
7. Prices: always "at time of filming"; rollback beat states both prices as observed that week; re-verify all with shelf tags/receipts (retailer sites block robots — every price is a snapshot).
8. Popularity: "#1" only as the attributed, dated Strategy/Maple Leaf claim. No market-share percentages exist — never invent one.
9. no name: keep the "formulations shift — check your own pack" hedge (OFF-sourced label).
10. Lafleur recall: always "Class 3, the lowest-risk category," "texture and appearance issues" quoted, "nobody was hurt."
11. "All beef" interlude: the claim "can be perfectly true" while fillers ride along — myth-bust the scope, never call the label false.
12. Winners still list sodium nitrite: state it plainly with the why ("legally required to be there in some form") — "The honest label spells it."
