## VERDICT KEY
- **VERIFIED** — confirmed against a reliable source (retailer listing, corporate site, news archive)
- **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** — confirmed against a primary source (Justice Laws, CFIA, Health Canada, StatCan, Gazette, recall database, WINA)
- **ATTRIBUTED** — stated in script as someone's claim ("per the company," "by their own account") — accuracy rests on the attribution
- **DERIVED** — our arithmetic from verified inputs (must be presented as computation, not sourced fact)
- **UNVERIFIED** — could not confirm; script must not assert it (excluded or softened)
- **EXCLUDED** — deliberately left out (house style or failed verification)

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS (binding on voiceover — do not paraphrase)
1. Sodium numbers are always "on the label we filmed / on the packages we checked" + "read your own label, it varies by flavour." Never present a sodium mg as a universal constant.
2. Mr. Noodles origin: read the package's "imported by" line on camera. NEVER say "Beltek Foods" or "Huizhou, China" (Wikipedia-only). Say "made somewhere else… overseas — check the fine print on your own packet."
3. TBHQ and MSG: ALWAYS "completely legal" / "legal in Canada, listed right there." NEVER "toxic," never a health effect, never "banned elsewhere." The beat is that it's printed and most people never read it.
4. Buldak Denmark: full arc mandatory — recall (3 varieties, capsaicin, "risk of acute poisoning" as Denmark's words), partial reversal one month later, 3x Spicy stayed recalled, partly based on the company's own marketed figures, AND zero Canadian recalls ("we searched the federal recall database… zero results"). Never imply the product is dangerous or "banned."
5. "No MSG added" beat: quote CFIA's own wording ("considered misleading and deceptive when other sources of free glutamates are present") and attribute — "their words, not ours." Then "decide for yourself." Never say Nissin broke the law.
6. Nongshim market-share claim: "Nongshim itself says…" — attributed, never asserted as audited fact. Same for Toyo Importing's 800,000 cases ("by their own account").
7. Half-block serving: frame as US-format label practice vs Canadian law. NEVER accuse a named brand of running the trick on a Canadian-compliant label. "On American-format labels you might see…" / "that half-bag serving is not allowed here."
8. Shin Light honesty caveat is NON-NEGOTIABLE: "the sodium is not better… this will wear the January symbol just like its red sibling." The win is only the un-fried block, attributed to the label's own "air dried, not fried."
9. Farkay: "steam-fried still means it meets palm oil" honesty line mandatory; company is Northwest Food Products, VANCOUVER (never Toronto).
10. US egg standard: "that's US law, not ours" — never imply any brand sold in Canada is mislabeled, and never claim US brands violate the US rule.
11. Prices: always "at time of filming" (Mr. Noodles $0.39–0.79 range, no name $0.39, Buldak ~$8.97/5, Farkay ~$5.46) — re-verify every price on camera with shelf tags/receipts.
12. Recalls (Nissin Big Cup, Baixiang): state product, date, reason (undeclared allergen), and resolution scope exactly; Nissin recall applies to the IMPORTED Big Cup line via importer Watson Enterprises — say "one imported Nissin line," never the US-made cups.
