## Decoders
| Item | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Grading ladder | VERIFIED-PRIMARY | BeefResearch.ca verbatim: "Canada AAA, AA and A are equivalent to USDA Choice, Select and Standard respectively"; "The U.S. equivalent grade for Canada Prime is USDA Prime." Canada Prime = **4.8%** of graded beef from FED slaughter cattle (2023); A+AA+AAA+Prime = 95.4% of YOUTHFUL slaughter cattle. MANDATORY: different denominators — script uses only the 4.8% figure, correctly attributed to fed cattle |
| Angus = breed | VERIFIED | Canadian Angus Association = breed registry; Verified Angus = min 50% Angus genetics; "a steak can be 100% Angus and grade Canada A" (logical from grading system). Script notes NO chain here uses Angus branding (accurate) |
| "Aged" has no legal definition | VERIFIED-PRIMARY | Direct search of CFIA Canadian Standards of Identity Vol. 7 (Meat Products) for dry aged/wet aged/aging/ageing: ZERO hits (only "packaged"). Wet vs dry mechanics = standard food science. "If they dry-age, they say so" = sound inference, framed as a tell not a law |
| MECHANICAL TENDERIZATION (centerpiece) | VERIFIED-PRIMARY | SOR/2014-99, Canada Gazette II May 21 2014, in force Aug 21 2014 (FDR B.14.022). Definition verbatim ("integrity of the beef surface… punctured by blades or needles, or by the injection of a solution or marinade"). Label verbatim: "mechanically tenderized" + "Cook to a minimum internal temperature of 63°C (145°F)" + "Turn steak over at least twice during cooking." Health Canada verbatim: "It is not apparent by just looking at the meat product whether if it has undergone this process." Risk: "a fivefold increase in risk from MTB products when compared to intact cuts of beef" (HC May 2013 assessment via RIAS). RESTAURANT EXEMPTION verbatim: "It does not apply to advertisements or to menu labelling in any food service establishment." XL Foods 2012: 18 illnesses, "several cases were considered to be likely associated with the consumption of mechanically tenderized beef" (RIAS verbatim); largest recall in Canadian history (TONNAGE NOT STATED — units discrepancy 1.8M kg vs 1.5M lb unresolved). 20-37% of beef production tenderized + food service named as user (RIAS verbatim) |
| **MTB — MANDATORY EDITORIAL GUARDRAIL** | BINDING | Script does NOT allege ANY chain uses MTB. Exact required phrasing used: "we found no evidence that any chain in tonight's video mechanically tenderizes its steaks… Not one of them tells you, and under Canadian law, none of them has to." |
| MTB balance (anti-fearmongering) | VERIFIED | CFIA 2018-19 surveillance: 975 MTB steak samples, 11 cities — E. coli O157:H7 NOT DETECTED in any sample; 99.7% satisfactory. MANDATORY inclusion; script frames segment as "a consent question," not a scare |
| Meat glue | VERIFIED-PRIMARY | HC proposed removing transglutaminase from solid cut meat July 2016 (citing rare-cooking risk on formed product), REVERSED effective Feb 15 2017 choosing labelling instead ("the food additive provisions will continue to allow the use of transglutaminase"). CFIA: formed products' common name must reflect binder. Restaurant menus exempt. TRAP NOTED: the reversal page's title reads like a ban — several secondary sources got this backwards |
