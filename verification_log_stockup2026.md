## THE TWO CONTROLS ON THIS EPISODE

**CONTROL 1 — THIS IS A PRICE VIDEO, NOT A SHORTAGE VIDEO.** The title does the prepper work on its own, so the first forty-five seconds over-correct on purpose. The line "Nothing is running out. There is no shortage in this country. Supply has been fine all year" is not padding — it is what stops this landing as doomsday content and it inoculates the script against the single most common criticism in this niche. **It cannot be cut for time.**

**BANNED VOCABULARY, absolute:** shortage, crisis, collapse, panic, hoard, survival, stockpile — except where the script explicitly negates them ("there is no shortage", "won't be the ones who panic-bought") or uses them as neutral commodity terms ("India's government stockpile", "the world price came apart"). Never mention water storage, generators, power failure, home security, or anything grid-related. Every one of the fifteen items is a grocery a person eats in a normal week.

**CONTROL 2 — NO HEALTH CLAIMS.** The reference video is a health-claims channel: omega-3 in the sardines, polyphenols in the olive oil, "the healthier choice," vitamin retention in frozen vegetables. Every one of those beats has been replaced with a structural mechanism. Nothing in this script characterises any food, nutrient or ingredient as good or bad for you. The olive oil item is an **authenticity and labelling** story only — never a safety story.

## MANDATORY PHRASINGS — 16 lines that must air as written

1. "Nothing is running out. There is no shortage in this country. Supply has been fine all year, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something." — the inoculation. Mandatory, verbatim, in the first minute.

2. The one test, sourced: foods with a shelf life greater than ninety days "are not required to be labelled with a best before date." — CFIA, verbatim. This is what makes the list feel earned rather than arbitrary.

3. "For the first time since July 2024, grocery price growth increased at a slower pace than the all-items CPI in August 2026." — Statistics Canada, verbatim, The Daily, released 14 September 2026.

4. On the Food Price Report: state the band (4-6%), state the August print (2.8%), and stop. **Do not say "the report was wrong."** The verifiable claim is the gap between the two numbers.

5. Olive oil, mandatory in the same breath as the 33%: "Results of targeted sampling are not representative of overall compliance in the Canadian marketplace." Plus: "They deliberately aim at higher-risk products. This is not a statement about a third of the shelf." **Never name an olive oil brand. Never imply wrongdoing by any identified company.**

6. Peanut butter: "That's his statement, not the company's — Kraft Heinz's own pages don't say." The US peanut sourcing is Sylvain Charlebois's public statement, not a company-confirmed fact. **Attribute it to him by name on screen.**

7. Salt: "that was road de-icing salt, not table salt. Different production line entirely... Canada did not run out of table salt." Mandatory. Blurring the two is the single most likely factual error in this script.

8. Honey: "And one thing I won't tell you, because I can't source it: honey has no federally stated indefinite shelf life." **Never say "honey never expires."**

9. Rice: "to be precise, because you'll see wilder claims than this" — then the $6.46 / $10.20 / $9.62 sequence. **Never say "rice inflation is 50%."** The CPI is +0.6% and a fact-checker will find that in ten seconds.

10. Lentils: the StatCan reason quoted verbatim — "lower returns relative to other crops because of tariffs in place from importing countries."

11. Canned fish: "Clover Leaf does not source its tuna directly" — verbatim from the company's own federal filing. The contrast is the company's own disclosure, not an accusation.

12. Retail concentration: "at least seventy-two per cent of the market, on the Competition Bureau's own study." **Do not imply coordination between grocers — that is uncharged wrongdoing.**

13. Laval: "while the broccoli sat in a field, perfectly fine." The strike is a logistics fact, not a scarcity claim.

14. "Canada does not run out of food. Canada runs out of ways to move it." — the thesis.

15. Every price: "Statistics Canada national average, July 2026." **No price in this script is a store price.** See Producer Actions.

16. The close does NOT ask a comment question. That is deliberate and it matches the reference. The comment prompt goes in the pinned comment instead.
